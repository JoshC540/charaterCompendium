# Playable Races

Below are the standard races, with a brief overview of each, their traits and their language skills.

**Half variants.** Only selectable if your parents are of two different races. You select the races of each parent; you get feat 1 and unlock feat 3 for one parent, and feat 2 and unlock feat 4 for the other. *Example: half dwarf, half human — Dwarf feat 1, Human feat 2, Dwarf unlock and feat 3, Human unlock and feat 4.*

## Human
hp: 5
imp: 2
languages: Common

Humans are the most basic and simple race. They are quite adaptable and live in all sorts of climates. It is common to find humans in all major settlements, where they are welcome. Humans are more than capable of filling a variety of roles in a party.

### Adrenaline Surge
Allows a human to take an additional turn once per combat as their adrenaline allows them further action.

### Generational Learning
Allows a human to increase an additional stat by 1.

### Feat 3: Learning
**Unlock:** Reach the age of 30 and have 5 years adventuring experience.

You are able to do one of the following three things:
- Improve all stats by 2 (lowest stat that can be created by this is 8)
- Advanced Weapon Training: you gain advantage with a weapon type of your choice
- Gain a class and 2 sp

### Feat 4: Advanced Learning
**Unlock:** Reach the age of 60 and have 30 years adventuring experience.

You are able to do one of the following three things:
- Improve all stats by 2 (lowest stat that can be created by this is 2); your max hp is reduced by 1/4
- Advanced Weapon Training: you gain double advantage with a weapon type of your choice, and your min damage with that weapon type is its max damage
- Gain a class and 4 sp, or gain 8 sp; your max hp is reduced by 1/2

## Dwarf
hp: 10
imp: 1
languages: Common, Dwarvish

Dwarves are hardy people. They live below ground in massive underground fortresses and cities, which are mighty and difficult to assail. The dwarves are master craftsmen and skilled warriors.

### Small but Mighty
The dwarf is immune to stuns and staggers.

### Lasting Grudge
Dwarves are notorious for their long-lasting grudges. Those who live up to this racial reputation gain a +2 bonus on attack rolls against any individual creature that attacked them 1 day ago or longer.

### Feat 3: Immovable Object
**Unlock:** Resist 10 stuns or staggers.

When a foe attempts to stun or stagger you, you may stun or stagger them as a reaction. You get +5 to any rolls against any foes you stun or stagger. Your stuns and staggers last +2 turns.

### Feat 4: Unstoppable Force
**Unlock:** Resolve 5 grudges.

When a target hits you, you gain advantage on your next attack against them. If you are fighting a grudge target, you can auto-crit against them twice in a fight.

## High Elf
hp: 3
imp: 4
languages: Common, Elvish, one other of your choice

High elves highly value knowledge and the pursuit of magic power through books. High elves think of themselves as the peak of elven society, better than both of the other elven races.

### Night Sight
Enables the elves to see at night, through a system similar to heat vision.

### Attuned to the Magic
You may learn 3 tier one spells of your choice; you may cast each once a day.

### Feat 3: Magical Adept
**Unlock:** Read a magical tome or use your magic to solve a puzzle.

You gain access to three more T1 spells and one T2 spell, and you may cast them all twice a day.

### Feat 4: Elven Grace
**Unlock:** Either pass 25 charisma checks or cast 25 spells — the unlock depends on which occurs first.

Your charisma or wisdom rolls become immune to disadvantage.

## Half-elf
hp: 4
imp: 3
languages: Common, Elvish

Half-elves are the children of elves and humans. They are not accepted by high elves, but the wood elves are okay with them. Half-elves find there is no place for them in the majority of high elf settlements, which limits their access to tomes of magic and knowledge. This treatment has left them used to being bullied and marginalised.

### Thick Skin
Half-elves are immune to enemy charisma rolls if the enemy is humanoid in appearance.

### Exile's Path
Reroll a wisdom save versus an enchantment or mind-affecting spell once per day.

### Feat 3: Strong Willed
**Unlock:** Resist 10 spells or 10 charisma rolls. The first completed determines the ability unlocked.

- **Charisma:** if you roll less than 10 on a charisma save you may reroll it once with advantage.
- **Wisdom:** if you roll less than 10 on a wisdom save you may reroll it once with advantage.

### Feat 4: Outcast No Longer
**Unlock:** No longer view yourself as an outcast/exile — for example by becoming a folk hero, business owner, etc.

When you provide the help action, the target gets double advantage. Additionally you gain a +5 bonus to your charisma rolls against humanoid creatures.

## Orc
hp: 8
imp: 3
languages: Common, Orcish

Orcs are big, angry, tusked, green-skinned creatures that enjoy nothing more than combat. They live to fight, and infighting between orcs is common. They seldom seek new lands but will happily raid other towns and settlements for riches. Nowadays, however, they have moved past that stage and become the most skilled mercenaries available for hire, leading to them being more peaceful in general.

### Defensive WAAAAH
Once per combat, orcs release a roar discouraging their foes from attacking, granting them and all their allies a min hit value of 5 for 1d3 turns.

### Offensive WAAAAH
Once per combat, orcs release a roar emboldening themselves and their allies, granting them an additional 1d4 per attack for 1d3 turns.

### Feat 3: Spectral Familiar
**Unlock:** Gain a trophy from a hunt or a combat.

You gain a spiritual familiar in the form of the creature you got the trophy from. If you killed a humanoid, the familiar takes the form of a creature the humanoid was most akin to. This familiar cannot interact with the physical world but can provide help with non-physical checks.

### Feat 4: Mightiest Warriors
**Unlock:** End a combat with a critical hit.

You can WAAAAH 2 additional times during combat. In a defensive WAAAAH, the first crit against a recipient is mitigated. In an offensive one, the first attack of each recipient is a critical.

## Half-orc
hp: 7
imp: 2
languages: Common, Orcish

Half-orcs are part orc, part human. They act as support units and shock cavalry in the orc armies, as they move swifter than the orcs themselves. Half-orcs make up for being weaker than their orc brethren by being faster and better scouts.

### Beast Rider
Grants +5 damage when mounted, to both you and your mount.

### Resilient Brute
Once per combat, convert a critical strike against you into a less damaging version.

### Feat 3: Mounted Crits
**Unlock:** You and your companion/mount must both land a killing blow in combat on different targets in four separate combats.

If you crit on your mount, you deal your damage x2 and your mount's main damage deals x1.5. If your mount crits it's the reverse.

### Feat 4: Shrug It Off
**Unlock:** Avoid death saves three times by reducing a crit's damage with Resilient Brute.

Any crit that would deal enough damage to down you instead deals half the non-critical damage. Then your next 1d4 attacks will auto-crit and deal a minimum of 50 percent of your max damage.

## Goblin
hp: 2
imp: 3
languages: Common, Orcish (with a few minor changes)

Goblins are skilled thieves and take what they want. Goblins live in the shadows of cities and towns, striking in the cover of night.

### Thief of the Night
Grants the goblin +3 on all stealth and dex rolls at night.

### Trip
Put a larger foe off-balance, granting disadvantage on dodge rolls for 1d4 turns. If they fail a dodge roll, they become prone.

### Feat 3: Sneaky Stabbing
**Unlock:** Cause 4 enemies to go prone with Trip.

All your attacks against prone enemies automatically crit, and Trip now automatically prones its target.

### Feat 4: Creature of the Dark
**Unlock:** Pass 10 stealth checks at night.

You are able to hide in the dark with practised ease: in the dark you automatically pass stealth checks against non-magic perception. Additionally you can see in the dark as if it were daytime, no longer needing light to see.

## Half-elemental
hp: 4
imp: 4
languages: Common, Arcane

Half-elementals are the hybrid of humans and pure arcane magic. They are thought to be the by-product of an elven experiment to make themselves stronger, tested on humans.

### Elemental Strike
Imbue your weapon with the magic of the element you are conjured with (e.g. fire for a fire half-elemental). Can be used once a combat and lasts three turns.

### Elemental Healing
When you are hit with the element you are conjured with, there is a 50% chance the damage is converted into healing instead.

### Feat 3: Elemental Absorption
**Unlock:** Have Elemental Healing trigger 5 times.

When damage of your type is dealt to you or an ally within touching distance, you can either trigger Elemental Healing or let the damage go through halved and grant the victim advantage on their next roll.

### Feat 4: Elemental Consumption
**Unlock:** Use your race feats a total of 25 times in combat.

You leverage your elemental half to gain a bonus depending on your element, and you are immune to your elemental half's damage type. Additionally you are considered both an elemental and a humanoid for spells and effects.

## Cat-folk
hp: 5
imp: 1
languages: Common, a secret trading tongue

These people are similar to humans but with the features of a cat. They are a nomadic people and travel trading goods. They are peaceful, and their primary goal in life is to gather as much wealth as possible.

### Cat-like Reflexes
Reroll dex-based rolls. If done on a saving throw and failed a second time, the damage taken is four times higher instead of two.

### Cat's Luck
Twice per day when a cat-folk makes a saving throw, they can roll it twice and take the better result. They must decide to use this before the saving throw is attempted.

### Feat 3: Feet First
**Unlock:** Survive a fall from 100 ft or more.

Immune to fall damage, and advantage on anything acrobatic in nature. Cat-like Reflexes now grants advantage on dex and dodge.

### Feat 4: Superior Luck
**Unlock:** With Cat's Luck, roll a nat 20 and a nat 1.

Your crit range is increased by 1. Additionally, you may reroll any 2 rolled, once.

## Reptile-people
hp: 6
imp: 2
languages: Common, Reptile

These people are half-reptile and half-human, and usually live in desert climates. They are cold blooded and enjoy harsh sunlight. They are able to shed their skin.

### Skin-shed
Remove any status effect by spending your action on a turn. This also allows you to change the tone of your skin.

### Regenerating Limbs
With enough time, and so long as the majority of the body remains alive, a reptile person can regenerate lost limbs.

### Feat 3: Reactive Skin
**Unlock:** Use Skin-shed to remove 5 status conditions.

Once per round, when you are affected by a negative status effect, automatically skin-shed to remove it. Additionally all buffs last 1 extra turn for you.

### Feat 4: Grow Back Stronger
**Unlock:** Regenerate a limb at least 5 times.

When you regrow a limb lost in combat, you gain +5 to your max hp and can improve a stat by 1. Stats cannot improve past 11 using this feat.

## Dragonkin
hp: 7
imp: 3
languages: Common, Draconic

Dragonkin are the product of one of the four primary types of dragon mingling with people: fire, water, air and earth dragonkin. Fire breath burns, water breath knocks back and puts out fire, air breath can disorientate and earth breath stuns.

### Dragon Breath
Cast your dragon-based breath attack. All breath attacks deal 1d10, plus 1d2 for status.

### Claws
Dragonkin have sharp claws. You are proficient with your unarmed strikes, which deal 3d2 slashing damage on a hit.

### Feat 3: Draconic Blood
**Unlock:** Consume fresh dragon's blood of the type you are akin to.

You temporarily gain hard scales coating your entire body, granting full immunity to your related element and ½ damage from physical strikes. Lasts 1d6 + 2 turns, usable twice a day.

### Feat 4: Draconic Roar
**Unlock:** Befriend a wild dragon.

All creatures that hear this roar must make a wisdom check or run in fear for a turn. The roar also acts as a call for aid to other draconic creatures.

## Ratmen
hp: 2
imp: 1
languages: Common, Rat

The rat people are the biggest cowards known to man. They stand slightly shorter than the average human and are highly skilled at running from their problems.

### RUUUN
Grants double advantage on disengage.

### Plagued Upbringing
Growing up and spawning in filth has its advantages. You are resistant to poison damage, have advantage on saving throws against poisons, and are immune to the effects of diseases. However, you can still spread them.

### Feat 3: Cowardly Escape
**Unlock:** Flee combat 25 times.

When you flee combat using RUUUN, any ally may use their action on their next turn to escape too.

### Feat 4: Rat Zero
**Unlock:** Spread a plague, willingly or not.

When struck by a melee attack, the attacker rolls a d2; on a 2 they are poisoned and have a chance to contract any diseases you may be harbouring.

## Demonborn
hp: 6
imp: 3
languages: Common, Infernal

Demonborn are the byproduct of human and demonic relationships. They are known to have red-tinged skin, and some have horns and/or tails.

### Infernal Blood
Ignite your weapons with demonfire (which cannot be extinguished without magic), lasting two turns.

### Fire Immunity
You are immune to fire damage.

### Feat 3: Warm Embrace
**Unlock:** Submerge in lava.

All non-friendly fire damage now grants damage to your next attack equal to half of the fire damage you would have taken. Also upgrades your Infernal Blood ignite.

### Feat 4: Hellsteed
**Unlock:** Sacrifice an item or person you have a deep connection with by tossing it (or the willing subject) into an active volcano.

You may summon a hellsteed at any time; this mount instinctively knows your thoughts. You gain no disadvantage to attacks whilst on the mount, and it can transfer its hit points to you. The hellsteed cannot make death saves; should it die, it cannot be resummoned until you toss its skull into a volcano. You are now considered a demonic entity.

## Dweller
hp: 7
imp: 1
languages: Common, Dweller

The dwellers live deep underground. They stand 6 ft tall on average, with six arms, jet black skin and deep blue eyes. They are a very reserved race and live solitary lives deep underground.

### Six Arms
Use three two-handed weapons, six one-handed weapons or a mixture of both, attacking with as many as you are capable of at that point in time.

### Heart of Stone
As an action once per day, transform your body into stone: +4 on saving throws against poison, paralysis, sleep and stunning effects, immunity to bleed, and a 50% chance to ignore extra damage from critical hits or sneak attacks. Lasts 1d3 turns.

### Feat 3: Body of Stone
**Unlock:** Use Heart of Stone to evade 1 critical hit and 3 status effects.

Heart of Stone is now permanently active, and your hp total is doubled. You can activate Heart of Stone to gain 1d20 temp hp twice a day instead.

### Feat 4: Whirlwind
**Unlock:** Pass an attack roll with each arm in one turn, three times (additional disadvantage per arm beyond what you can normally do).

When you attack, you may attack with each arm independently — six targets, one target six times, or any mix in between. This does not grant dual-wielding skill or proficiency in additional weapons.

## Lost
hp: 1
imp: 3
languages: Common, one main-race language of your choice

The "lost" are what happens when an individual is slain but their soul refuses to pass into the next realm.

### Gravebound
The lost cannot make death saves, but can exist at zero hp as long as they pass a save each turn; the save's difficulty increases each turn. When they fail, their soul slips into the afterlife.

### Already Half-way Dead
You gain immunity to necrotic damage.

### Feat 3: Truly Dead
**Unlock:** Spend 5 turns in death saves.

You are considered undead. You no longer roll any checks whilst in death saves. Your hp can go into the negatives; at the end of combat your max hp is reduced by your negative hp value divided by two.

### Feat 4: Haunted / At Peace
**Unlock:** Abandon or complete the goal that kept you around.

- **Abandon → Haunted:** you can no longer pass from this plane. When your current body dies, you become a ghost which can attempt to possess a new body.
- **Complete → At Peace:** you select a new goal or pass on to your next life. If you pick a new goal, you stay on the material plane as before but can take heroic actions at any point with a 10% chance of survival.

## Slimefolk
hp: 5
imp: 2
languages: Common

Slimefolk are sentient pools of slime that elect to take the form of humanoids. These otherwise harmless slimy humanoids display greater intelligence than other oozes, capable of the speech, cunning and insight of other humanoid races. They can completely alter their appearance overnight — body shape, colouration and height.

### Amorphous
You can move through a space as narrow as 1 inch without squeezing. You leave behind any items you are carrying, and any equipment you are proficient with merges into your form until you exit the space.

### Oozey Self
When struck with a weapon or object, you may make a strength check to partially absorb it, pinning it in place. With an object pinned in you, you may take an action to fully absorb it, and can then attempt to consume it.

### Feat 3: Absorb Memories
**Unlock:** Be in the presence of a creature dying while under the effects of a charm or similar spell.

Wrap yourself around a creature up to medium size; after 2 hours you see flashes of their major memories. The target must be willing, unconscious, unable to cancel your attempt, or dead. You may also ask the DM questions about the life you witness.

### Feat 4: Blob
**Unlock:** Have part of you cut off, either during a full moon or in the presence of a caster capable of animating it.

Sacrifice 1d4 hp to create a mini you, 1–2 ft tall, capable of performing a single basic task before reabsorbing into you.

## Forsaken
hp: 6
imp: 3

The forsaken are an ancient race who deemed themselves better than the gods they served. The gods punished them for their foolishness, cursing them to suffer for all eternity and never hear the gods again. The gods use them as a reminder to those who think themselves above the gods. Forsaken are very rare to meet, as they hide from the world, fearful of how they will be treated.

### Unable to Die
Upon taking lethal damage, you come back to life in 1d10 days with your max hp reduced by 1d6. All pain is continuous and no healing spell can remove it. Once your max hp hits 0 or below, or at 10 deaths, you are transformed into a sentient statue, still feeling everything. Upon each revival you must pass a wisdom save against a stack of madness.

### Bestow Pain
Make a target feel your pain for 1d3 turns, 5 times a day — they suffer all the pain you feel, continuously. If you have triggered Unable to Die before, the target must make a wisdom save each turn or be afflicted with madness. The target must make a strength check to act through the pain.

### Feat 3: Sacrifice for the Gods / Hunted by the Gods
**Unlock:** Attract the attention of a god.

- **Positive — Sacrifice for the Gods:** depends on the god whose attention you attracted. Upon completing each sacrifice, you gain a minor boon.
- **Negative — Hunted by the Gods:** the god's followers seek to torment you. When you slay one of them, you gain a minor boon.

### Feat 4: God's Chosen / Madness of the Gods
**Unlock:** Appease or enrage a god.

- **Appease — God's Chosen:** upon your next death you are no longer forsaken; you become a celestial entity in the image of your god. When you die in this form, you enter your god's afterlife.
- **Enrage — Madness of the Gods:** you gain 5 stacks of madness and the gods hunt you relentlessly. Your Bestow Pain grows stronger, and you become a living statue on your next death. You gain access to the feat 5 unlock condition and a legendary-tier weapon bound to you.

### Feat 5: ????
**Unlock:** Slay a god.

## Fallen
hp: 7
imp: 2

There once was a time when angels either left the higher planes voluntarily or were forced out. To be fallen is to be a descendant of one of these angels. Their powers live on in the bloodline but can lie dormant for generations with no proof at all. Their offspring have a small amount of their power, which can grow as they develop.

### Angelic Blood
While many of an angel's resistances and immunities aren't available to you, you still have resistance to charms and radiant damage.

### Celestial Power
A small amount of angelic magic lets you perform small abilities to aid those around you, cast against charisma rather than wisdom. You may cast lesser heal (1d4) three times a day and heal (1d8) once a day.

### Feat 3: Fledgling Wings
**Unlock:** Heal party members making death saves 15 times in combat using Celestial Power.

You sprout a pair of wings — either physical, or celestial and only visible while in use. You may fly for 15 mins at a time before needing a 1 hour break. The flight time restriction is removed upon unlocking feat 4.

### Feat 4: Celestial Halo
**Unlock:** Slay a greater demon, dealing at least 50% of the damage.

You gain a halo of pure celestial energy and are now considered a celestial. Twice a day you may channel it to either heal all allies within 1 km to full hp, or deal 22d2 damage to 1d4 enemies within sight.

## Wood Elf
hp: 6
imp: 3
languages: Common, Elvish, Floral, one other of your choice

Wood elves seek unity with nature and live peaceful lives protecting the forests and wildlife.

### Night Sight
Enables the elves to see at night, through a system similar to heat vision.

### Attuned to the Wild
When you are in your selected terrain type (chosen at character creation, e.g. forest or another common terrain) you gain +4 on perception checks and have advantage on travelling/tracking in that terrain.

### Feat 3: Hermit
**Unlock:** Avoid spending more than 3 total weeks in non-natural environments.

Attuned to the Wild now applies to all terrain types. You have disadvantage on charisma checks but gain advantage in two stats of your choosing.

### Feat 4: Wild Hunt Master
**Unlock:** Participate in a successful wild hunt.

You may begin a new wild hunt; upon completion you gain access to a powerful item. A person may only have one wild hunt item. Attuned to the Wild is upgraded to also grant +2 to all other stats while in the wild.

## Dryad & Treekin
languages: Common, Floral

The first dryads were created long ago when the forests felt threatened by unnatural forces. Treekin were created at the same time, as bulky enforcers. The two forms can become one another — dryads by growing into treekin, treekin by shedding their heavier shells.

### Dryad (5 hp, I.M.P. 2)
Slim and agile, with skin made of bark, well suited to working within the forests they were made to protect. It is uncommon to see dryads outside the forests they were spawned in. Dryads can be created by forests in need, or by treekin shedding their heavy shells to become more nimble. To transform into a treekin without external factors, you must spend three months rooted in the woods, and the woods must be free from corruption.

- **Woodland Armour:** your bark skin makes you more resistant to most damage. You gain 10 temp hp, which replenishes on a long rest. It also grants advantage on all things stealth-related in forests/woods, or in urban environments with green areas.
- **Defender of the Forests:** nature itself will attempt to protect and sustain you. In the forest, the forest alerts you to any dangers near you. You may root yourself in place, healing 1d4 a turn until you choose to unroot or enter death saves, but you take double damage from fire.

### Treekin (8 hp, I.M.P. 2)
Larger than dryads, with heavier bark skin and club-like arms that act as natural weapons. Treekin are also created by forests to go and create new forests. It is rumoured that treekin can become, or stem from, ents, but this has never been confirmed. To transform into a dryad without external factors, take an action to abandon your heavier armour, which roots itself to serve as a safe place for forest creatures or as the catalyst for a new forest.

- **Woodland Armour:** you gain 30 temp hp, which replenishes on a long rest, and club-like arms that deal 1d6 damage with a 50% chance to stun.
- **Defender of the Forests:** in the forest, the forest grants you 10 additional temp hp and thorns grow on you to help protect you. You may root yourself in place, healing 1d8 a turn until you choose to unroot or enter death saves, but you take double damage from fire.

### Feat 3: Ent and Sapling Forms
**Unlock:** Become a treekin from a dryad 3 times.

You gain two more powerful forms: Ent, a powerful frontline style, and Sapling, an adorable charisma-focused style. You may transition between your four forms in a matter of hours in the woods.

### Feat 4: Purger of Corruption
**Unlock:** Clear a corrupted woods.

You have advantage on saves against unnatural effects and advantage on attack rolls against things that break the natural order.

## Half Blood
hp: As base race
imp: As base race

Half bloods are individuals afflicted with the disease form of vampirism. They are weaker than vampires born of ritual but have similar power in general. The disease comes from a number of sources — most commonly bites, though contact with a half blood's blood can also cause it in rare cases.

### Former Race
Pick another race: take trait 1 or 2 of that race.

### Blood Drain
As a bonus action, bite and drain the blood from a target, dealing 1d4 damage and healing twice the damage dealt.

### Feat 3: Power from Blood
**Unlock:** Heal 40 hp from Blood Drain.

When you Blood Drain, you gain a racial trait from the target that lasts 1d3 turns, and the drain deals an additional d4 damage. However, if you haven't drained in the last 12 hours, bright lights become harsh on your eyes, and 24 hours without it causes sensitivity to the sun.

### Feat 4: Half Blood No Longer
**Unlock:** Be made into a pure blood — or be cured through ???. If cured, return to your base race with a random additional trait.

You are no longer a half blood. You are either a pure blood or cured.

## Siren Spawn
hp: 3
imp: 3

These people are the offspring of survivors of encounters with sirens. How that occurred is still often debated, but siren blood is strong: any offspring of a siren spawn is always another siren spawn, no matter how many generations ago it happened.

### Charming Looks
You look more attractive than the average person, though this only applies to individuals who would find your gender attractive in the first place. You have advantage on charisma checks against them.

### Siren's Song
- **Out of combat:** draws a random number of individuals towards you as your song calls to their innermost desires.
- **In combat:** as a bonus action, all who hear the song must make a wisdom save or be stunned for a turn.

### Feat 3: Siren's Grace
**Unlock:** Pass 10 charisma checks with Charming Looks.

You may make dex checks outside of combat based on your charisma stat. You also gain an additional advantage on all charisma checks.

### Feat 4: Song of Demise
**Unlock:** Slay a foe affected by Siren's Song.

- **Out of combat:** all who hear this song feel an urge to flee wherever they are.
- **In combat:** all who hear this song make a wisdom save; on a fail they die in 1d5 turns.

## Gnome (Dark Variant)
hp: 2
imp: 2

Gnomes are normally peaceful folk. These ones aren't. Sadly no researcher has survived an encounter with them in their villages or tribes, so little more can be reported.

### Gnaw
If you hit a target, you may gnaw them, adding an additional 1d4 damage. Be warned: some races don't taste pleasant, or can have negative effects when bitten.

### Hyperactive
When you enter combat, for the first 1d3 turns you can take an additional action. When this ends you are stunned for a turn.

### Feat 3: Bloodthirsty
**Unlock:** Participate in a combat with 10+ combatants dying.

When a foe dies while you are Hyperactive, you remain in it for one additional turn. If you are Hyperactive for 10 turns, you remain so for the rest of combat.

### Feat 4: Nightmare Creature
**Unlock:** Commit a heinous act.

People naturally avoid you whenever possible, as you innately trigger their flight response. In combat you may attempt to invoke this reaction in a foe as a bonus action.

## Ashen
hp: 4
imp: 3

All Ashen can trace their lineage, through immense effort, back to one of five Ashen men believed to be among the first of their kind; details on these men are vague at best. Born with a taste of a phoenix's power, the Ashen are harder than average to kill.

### Healing Flames
As a bonus action, ignite a target in healing flames. The flames prevent the target being affected by status effects and heal 1d2 hp a turn. You can only maintain them on one target at a time, and they end if you are downed or struck with a weapon.

### Rebirth
As a bonus action, mark a target; if they die within the next 24 hours they are revived. Only one target may be marked at a time, and once triggered this trait cannot be used again until you are revived yourself.

### Feat 3: Ashes to Ashes
**Unlock:** Revive yourself or a target thanks to Rebirth.

When you drop to 0 hp, you become ashes. These ashes can revive you by a number of methods, one of which is exposure to more powerful healing flames found in some temples or structures of note.

### Feat 4: Dust to Dust
**Unlock:** Be revived from ash form.

Your healing flames now deal double healing. Additionally, whenever any target affected by your healing flames goes down, the flames can render them into the ash form of Ashes to Ashes.

## Echoforged
hp: 3
imp: 4
languages: Common, Echo-Tongue

Echoforged coalesce where history has been violently erased — battlefields where the fallen went unnamed, ruined libraries where books were burned, abandoned homes where families vanished without trace. They are not ghosts, but living receptacles for what was meant to be remembered. Their semi-corporeal forms shimmer with trapped echoes: fading last words swirl in their chest cavities, unfinished lullabies hum in their hollow bones, and torn pages of lost stories flutter beneath their skin.

Unlike the Lost (who cling to unfinished business) or Hollow Veils (who consume magic), Echoforged *preserve*. Their touch can make old portraits whisper forgotten names, and their breath carries the scent of candles extinguished decades ago.

### Ethereal Whispers
You can hear the final words of creatures that died within 100 ft of you in the last 24 hours.

### Phantom Step

**Unlock:** Carry (in any sense of the word) a true hero — someone a bard has called such — to victory.

Move 120 ft in a straight line once per combat, knocking aside smaller creatures. Acts as an instant disengage if you wish.

### Feat 4: Mythic Charge
**Unlock:** Become the subject of a bard's epic.

Your attacks ignore resistance and leave glowing hoofprints that guide allies.

## Chimera-Touched
hp: 7
imp: 2
languages: Common, Primal

The Chimera-Touched are beings forcibly fused together through ancient alchemy, cursed magic or the whims of the gods. Unlike natural chimeras they keep humanoid forms, but their bodies are tapestries of stolen parts: a lion's paw for a hand, a dragon's scaled leg, a goat's horn bursting from the forehead. Some say a mad archmage made the first; others that they are a deity's punishment. A rare few are born this way, when a pregnant mother survives an encounter with a true chimera.

### Composite Form
Gain a minor trait from each ancestor (e.g. claws, climb speed, fire breath 1/day).

### Unstable Biology
Roll 1d6 at dawn: 1–2 = −1 to a stat (max 16), 3–4 = no effect, 5–6 = +1 to a stat (min 10).

### Feat 3: Adaptive Mutation
**Unlock:** Survive having a limb severed (it grows back mismatched).

Replace this trait with a new, permanent one from another beast (e.g. a dragon's flight, a fish's gills). You must have some of the beast to stitch on to you.

### Feat 4: Perfect Hybrid
**Unlock:** Stabilise — for 10 days, do not roll a 1 or 2 for Unstable Biology.

Stabilise your form: keep all traits and ignore Unstable Biology. You may add more traits from animals you stitch on; each new trait fuses with your feat 3 trait.

## Rookborn
hp: 4
imp: 3
languages: Common, Corvid, and at least one language picked up by listening

Rookborn stand a head shorter than a human, with hollow bones, black eyes set too far apart, and a coat of oiled feathers that lies flat enough to pass for a cloak at a distance. Their arms end in long grey fingers that are very good at three things: knots, locks, and picking up something that was not theirs.

What defines them is not the feathers. It is that Rookborn do not forget a face, and neither does anyone they have told. A slight against one Rookborn is a slight against every Rookborn who has heard about it, and they hear about everything. Merchants who cheat one find their stalls avoided, their shipments delayed, and their children followed to school by birds for the next forty years. Nobody has ever caught them doing anything actionable.

They live in loose city-roosts called Parliaments, which have no leaders and reach decisions by a process outsiders find impossible to observe. They speak Common, Corvid, and by long custom at least one language they were never taught, having simply listened.

### Perfect Recollection

You never forget a face, a voice, or a route once travelled. You automatically recognise any creature you have previously seen regardless of disguise, magical or otherwise, and you cannot become lost anywhere you have been before.

### Sleight

You have advantage on all rolls to take, hide, or conceal an object. Once per encounter, you may take a small item from a creature within reach as a bonus action without them noticing until they next look for it.

### Feat 3: Word Spreads
**Unlock:** Bring a wrong done to another Rookborn to a resolution.

Name a creature aloud to any Rookborn. Within a week, every Rookborn in the region knows the name and the reason. You gain +5 to all charisma and information-gathering rolls in any settlement with a Parliament, and the named creature has disadvantage on the same.

### Feat 4: Murder
**Unlock:** Be present at a Parliament's decision and be the one they follow.

Once per long rest, call the flock. 1d6 rounds later, an uncountable number of birds arrives. They blind, harry and obscure — all enemies within 60ft have disadvantage on attacks and cannot make ranged attacks for 1d4 turns. What arrives is not under your control and does not always leave when you do.

## Chrysalid
hp: 7
imp: 3
languages: Common, Hymn

Every Chrysalid has already been something else. They are born larval — soft, pale, six-limbed, and physically unimpressive — and they spend the first third of their lives that way, in the crèches, being curious and irritating and largely harmless. Nobody adventures as a larva. The world is not survivable at that size and Chrysalids are not sentimental about the fact.

At some point, never predictably and never on schedule, a larva stops. They find somewhere defensible, go still, and seal themselves in for three weeks. What emerges is decided by how the larval years were spent, and by the time a Chrysalid is old enough to leave the crèche, that decision has already been made and cannot be revisited. A Chrysalid character begins play adult: winged, plated, and carrying an unfaceted memory of years they can recall clearly but cannot recall *feeling*.

They are physically imposing in an angular, badly-jointed way, standing a little over six feet with hard chitin at the shoulders and forearms and wings that fold flat down the back. They speak Common and Hymn, a language of wing-tone that larvae cannot produce and adults cannot stop producing when distressed.

### First Emergence

You have already cocooned once. Choose the form your larval years shaped, at character creation:

- **Force** — heavy carapace. +10 max hp, unarmed strikes deal 2d4, and you cannot be disarmed.
- **Cunning** — patterned wings. Once per combat, become invisible until you attack or are attacked.
- **Care** — scent glands. As an action, steady all allies within 20ft, removing fear and granting advantage on their next saving throw. 3/day.

### Wings

Fly 30ft, but you must land at the end of each turn. You take no falling damage and may always choose to land upright.

### Feat 3: Second Instar
**Unlock:** Cocoon a second time. This takes three weeks, cannot be safely interrupted, and roughly one in five do not come out.

You emerge again, keeping your first form and gaining the trait of a second one of your choosing. Your flight loses its landing restriction. You may no longer produce larvae, and every Chrysalid you meet will know this on sight and have an opinion about it.

### Feat 4: Imago
**Unlock:** Cocoon a third time, which is not supposed to be survivable and generally is not.

You hold all three forms at once. Additionally, your plating no longer sets — once per long rest, you may spend an hour reshaping yourself for a known problem, gaining advantage on one stat and immunity to one damage type of your choosing until your next long rest.

## Bayborn
hp: 6
imp: 2
languages: Common, Primal

Bayborn are broad, long-jawed, and built to run for a day and a half. Standing, they clear six feet; moving fast, they do not bother staying upright. Their coats run from tawny to near-black, and every Bayborn carries a scar somewhere visible that they will happily explain at length.

They are the only race in common circulation whose strength is a function of company. A Bayborn alone is a competent fighter and an unhappy person. A Bayborn with four others at their shoulder is something else entirely — they move as one animal, they anticipate each other without signalling, and they do not need to be told the plan. Adventuring parties are the closest most Bayborn get to a pack, which is why so many of them adventure, and why so many of them will not leave a party they have grown into even when leaving is clearly the correct decision.

They do not have chiefs. They have whoever the pack is currently orienting on, which can change mid-fight and frequently does. They speak Common and Primal.

### Pack Sense

You always know the exact position and rough condition of every ally within 100ft, through walls, darkness or silence. You and your allies may communicate simple intent without speaking.

### Run Them Down

You may move at full speed for a full day without rest. Any creature you have been pursuing for an hour or longer cannot hide from you, and you gain +3 to all rolls against it.

### Feat 3: The Shoulder
**Unlock:** Fight three separate battles without a single ally being downed.

Gain +1 to all rolls for every ally within 15ft of you, to a maximum of +4. If you are alone, you instead take -2 to all rolls and cannot benefit from the help action.

### Feat 4: Bay
**Unlock:** Lose a packmate and choose to continue with the pack rather than pursue.

Once per combat, sound. Every ally who hears it immediately moves up to their full speed toward you without using their action, and all of you gain a minimum hit value of 8 until the end of your next turn. If any ally is at 0 hp when you sound, they stabilise automatically.

## Hibernal
hp: 10
imp: 1
languages: Common, Primal

Hibernals are the largest of the common races — nine feet on the rare occasions they stand fully upright, and heavy enough that most inn floors are a genuine question. Thick pelts, blunt claws, small deep-set eyes, and a general demeanour that outsiders read as slow and their own people read as correctly paced.

They sleep for a third of the year, and they do not consider this rest. A Hibernal in the deep sleep is *working* — banking what the waking months put into them, converting a good season into something that can be spent later. A Hibernal who has had a hard year wakes diminished. A Hibernal who has had a magnificent one wakes as something you would not want to disagree with.

Hibernal settlements are built around the winter: enormous shared sleeping halls, elaborate guardianship rotas for the few who stay awake, and a legal tradition that treats anything done to a sleeping Hibernal as the single worst crime in their code. They speak Common and Primal, and their winters are when most of their poetry gets written, by the guards.

### Deep Sleep

Each year you must sleep for three months or begin taking cumulative penalties. When you wake, you gain a pool of Stored dice based on how the previous year went — the DM assigns between 2d6 and 6d6. You may spend one Stored die at any time to add it to any roll or to any damage you deal.

### Bulk

You cannot be moved, grappled or knocked prone by anything smaller than you. You may use two-handed weapons in one hand. You automatically fail all stealth checks in enclosed spaces and cannot fit through anything built for a human.

### Feat 3: Rouse
**Unlock:** Wake mid-hibernation to defend the hall, and survive it.

You may enter a waking version of the deep sleep for a single turn, taking no action. On your next turn, you gain 2d6 temp hp and may spend up to three Stored dice at once. Usable once per combat.

### Feat 4: The Long Year
**Unlock:** Give your entire Stored pool away to another creature in a single act.

Your Stored pool no longer empties at the end of the year — unspent dice carry over, to a maximum of 12d6. Additionally, you may now share the pool: any ally who rests beside you during your hibernation gains 1d6 of it for their own use.

## Deepmaw
hp: 5
imp: 4
languages: Common, Primordial

Deepmaw come from below the trenches, where the water is black and the pressure is a physical fact and nothing has needed eyes for a very long time. On land they are unsettling in a way people find hard to place — too thin, too long in the jaw, skin like wet slate, and a mouth that does not fully close.

Above their brow sits a lure: a single filament ending in a pale light that they can dim, brighten, or shape. In the deep, it is how they eat. On the surface, it turns out to work on almost everything with a mind, and Deepmaw have had to learn a great deal of etiquette about when to switch it off. Most of them keep it hooded. The ones who do not are usually not being polite by accident.

They are patient in a way that reads as menacing and is mostly just how you end up when your ancestors waited weeks between meals. They speak Common and Primordial, and their own tongue is pressure-based and cannot be produced in air at all.

### The Lure

As an action, unhood your light. All creatures within 30ft who can see it must make a wisdom roll or move toward you on their next turn and be unable to target anyone else while doing so. You may maintain it, but you may take no other action while it burns.

### Pressure-Born

You are immune to cold and to crushing damage of any kind, and you can see perfectly in total darkness. Bright light gives you disadvantage on all rolls until you can hood your eyes.

### Feat 3: Swallow
**Unlock:** Take a creature larger than you, alone, using the Lure.

Your bite deals 2d6 and, on a creature at or below half hp that is your size or smaller, you may attempt to swallow them whole. A swallowed creature takes 1d6 per turn and must break out with a strength roll. You may hold one at a time and it is very obvious that you are doing so.

### Feat 4: Silent Light
**Unlock:** Extinguish your lure and hunt blind for a full month.

Your Lure no longer requires an action or your attention, may be maintained indefinitely, and no longer emits visible light — the compulsion arrives without a source. Affected creatures do not know why they are approaching and cannot be warned off by an ally pointing at you.

# Prime Variants

Every race has an origin event. For most, that event happened once, long ago, and everything since has been descent — each generation a slightly weaker copy. A Prime is what happens when the origin event repeats at full strength, or when a bloodline runs backward far enough to arrive at its own source.

They are not a separate people. A Prime can be born to entirely ordinary parents. This is the part that frightens everyone.

## Human (Prime)
base: Human

The other races have a joke about humans: they are what is left when a species refuses to decide what it is. Prime Humans are that refusal taken to its conclusion.

They are not born so much as they fail to finish being born. Midwives report nothing unusual until the child is a few weeks old and the parents begin quietly disagreeing about its face. By adulthood a Prime Human has spent decades borrowing — a dwarf's stubbornness worn for a season, an elf's night-sight, a goblin's flinch. Each is genuinely theirs while they hold it. None of them stay.

Human scholars celebrate them as the pinnacle of adaptability. Every other race finds them deeply upsetting, because a Prime Human who has spent a month with you will begin, unmistakably, to become slightly you.

### Physical
Their face is not fixed. It shifts by fractions toward whoever they spend time near, and nobody who meets them twice describes them the same way.

### Borrowed Blood
After a week alongside any creature, permanently gain one of its feat-1 traits. Taking a new one discards the old. Adrenaline Surge becomes unlimited, but each use past the first costs a quarter of max HP.

## Dwarf (Prime)
base: Dwarf

The dwarves did not come from the mountains. They came from beneath them, from pressure, and a Prime is what the pressure makes when it is not interrupted.

They are born in the oldest and deepest holds, always to parents who worked the low seams. The child is small, dense, and immediately a structural concern — nurseries in the deep holds have reinforced floors for exactly this reason. Prime Dwarves cannot swim, cannot be carried by one person, and cannot be moved against their will by anything short of siege equipment.

Their grudges are institutional. A carved name is a legal document in the deep holds, and a Prime with an open forearm has standing to pursue that name across any border, into any nation, for as long as the scar stays open. Most kingdoms have quietly agreed it is easier to extradite.

### Physical
4ft and weighing as much as a warhorse. Skin has visible stone grain, floors crack under them, and they sink like an anvil in water.

### Grudgestone
Carve a name into your own forearm. You know that target's direction and distance anywhere in the world, and gain a cumulative +1 to all rolls against them per day since the carving, uncapped. The scar only closes when they die.

## High Elf (Primal)
base: High Elf

High elven society is built on the conviction that magic is *studied*. Primals ruin this.

A Primal is born with the ears already elongated, and within a year they are twitching independently toward every casting in the building. They do not read magic. They overhear it. A Primal child raised near a tower will have absorbed a working library by the age of nine without opening anything, which is precisely the problem — the High Elves' entire hierarchy is a ladder of earned access, and Primals climb nothing.

They are treated with enormous public honour and quietly excluded from every archive that matters. Most leave. A significant number end up teaching in places where nobody cares how the knowledge was acquired.

### Physical
Ears elongated into blade-thin antennae, twitching independently toward magic.

### Spellthief's Ear
Sense any casting within 100ft through solid matter, including tier and school. Hear the same spell cast three times and you learn it permanently, regardless of tier or whether you have the class for it.

## Half-elf (Prime)
base: Half-elf

Half-elves were never meant to breed true. Somewhere, several centuries back, one line did, and its descendants are no longer half of anything.

A Prime Half-elf has no fixed appearance. The face resolves into whatever the observer finds least threatening, which means they have never once been described accurately by two people. This is not a disguise and cannot be switched off. Many have never seen themselves — mirrors show them what *they* find least threatening, which is usually a stranger.

The compensation is total. Nothing that targets lineage can find them. Inherited curses slide off, bane-workings fail to bind, and no oracle has ever seen one in a vision. There is a standing theory among diviners that Prime Half-elves are not absent from prophecy but *edited out of it*, and that this is being done deliberately, and that nobody knows by whom.

### Physical
Features rearrange toward whatever the viewer finds least threatening. Two people in the same room will argue about what they look like.

### No One's Kin
Immune to anything targeting a race, bloodline or lineage — bane spells, inherited curses, divine judgement, prophecy. You cannot be located by divination and do not appear in any oracle's vision.

## Orc (Prime)
base: Orc

Every orc clan keeps a story about the First Warband — four-armed, head and shoulders above everything, so consumed by fighting that they never got around to building anything. The story is usually told as a warning. Primes are the story arriving in person.

They are born in the middle of clan wars, disproportionately, which nobody has explained. The extra arms are present at birth and the rage arrives around adolescence, uninvited and without training. A Prime does not learn to rage; a Prime learns, very slowly and with help, to stop.

Modern orc society is mercenary, disciplined, and profitable. Primes are extremely bad for business and extremely good at the actual work. Most clans keep exactly one and are careful about which contracts they take.

### Physical
2–3ft taller with four arms.

### Innate Rage
Berserker rage from creation with no class requirement. Both WAAAAHs can run simultaneously and stack with every other Prime Orc present. Every ally who dies while you are raging grants a permanent +1 damage.

## Half-orc (Prime)
base: Half-orc

Orc armies used half-orcs as cavalry for centuries. A few of those lines stopped dismounting.

The fusion was not deliberate and took generations. What emerged is a people who are their own mount — hooved, digitigrade, and faster over broken ground than anything with a rider on it. They have two hearts, spaced far enough apart that a single wound cannot reach both, and a Prime who has been run through will simply keep coming with the second one, crits landing double, entirely aware that this is the last part of the fight.

Orc clans revere them and find them awkward to house. Half-orc settlements that have gone Prime tend to be built around wide, low, doorless structures.

### Physical
The rider and mount fused generations back. Digitigrade, hooved, and no longer needs anything to ride.

### Two Hearts
You have two separate HP pools. When the first empties you do not drop — the second takes over, and every attack you make while running on it is an automatic critical.

## Goblin (Prime)
base: Goblin

Goblins tell no origin story. Prime Goblins are the reason.

There is no fixed skeleton in a Prime — the joints move in every direction, and the body settles into whatever shape the gap requires. The eyes have no whites at all, which is how they are identified, usually far too late. A Prime that has not been directly observed during a turn cannot be reliably fought; they arrive from a direction that was not there.

Cities do not know they have them. That is generally the arrangement. A Prime Goblin who is discovered has failed at the only thing Primes are for, and their own kind will not shelter them afterward.

### Physical
No fixed skeleton. Joints bend in every direction, and the eyes have no whites at all.

### Nobody's Watching
If no enemy directly observed you during your last turn, every attack you make this turn automatically crits. Trip triggers free on any successful hit.

## Half-elemental (Prime)
base: Half-elemental

If the half-elementals really were an elven experiment on humans, the Primes are the version the experiment was actually after.

There is no flesh in a Prime — a membrane of skin over raw element that moves visibly beneath it, and enough ambient bleed that they cannot stand near anything flammable, freezable, or fragile depending on their type. They do not resist their element. They *drink* it. Every point of it dealt within thirty feet, by anyone, to anyone, flows to the Prime as healing, and they can hold it and let it back out all at once.

This makes them catastrophic to field against an army of matching type, and it makes them unwelcome at any hearth, forge, or well. Primes tend to be solitary by necessity rather than temperament.

### Physical
No flesh. A thin outline of skin containing raw, visibly moving element.

### Elemental Tide
Every point of your element's damage dealt within 30ft — by anyone, to anyone — redirects to you as healing instead. You may hold it and release the accumulated total as a single burst.

## Cat-folk (Prime)
base: Cat-folk

The trading caravans are a late development. Primes are what cat-folk were before the wealth.

Eight feet, built as a predator, and marked along the spine with nine visible burns. Each burn is a life. When a Prime dies, one mark goes out and the last few seconds rewind, and everybody present sees the count drop. There is no hiding it and no faking it, which has made Prime Cat-folk the most trusted contract enforcers on the continent — you can look at one and know exactly how much risk they can still absorb.

A Prime with two marks left commands enormous respect and enormous prices. A Prime with none is simply a very large cat-folk who is about to die like anyone else, and they never announce it.

### Physical
8ft, fully digitigrade, built as a predator rather than a merchant. Nine marks burned into the fur along the spine.

### Nine
Nine times in your life, negate an event that would kill you and rewind to the start of that turn. One mark burns away each time. Everyone can see how many you have left.

## Reptile-people (Prime)
base: Reptile-people

Ordinary reptile-folk shed skin. Primes shed *themselves*.

Once a season the entire body comes away intact — hollow, whole, and unnervingly complete down to the fingernails. It stays animate but empty for about a week, and the Prime can step into it across any distance. Villages in the deep desert keep sheds the way other places keep horses: stationed at wells, watchtowers, and border posts, waiting.

The theological problem is obvious and reptile-folk have argued about it for centuries. If the shed can hold you, and the body can hold you, which one is you? The prevailing answer is that the question is rude.

### Physical
Sheds the entire body seasonally, leaving a hollow, intact duplicate behind.

### The Old Skin
Your most recent shed husk stays animate but inert for a week. You can move your consciousness into it at any distance as an action — a spare body, a scout, or a decoy waiting somewhere safe.

## Dragonkin (Prime)
base: Dragonkin

Dragonkin are diluted dragons. A Prime is a dragon that has not yet noticed it is standing upright.

Nine feet, fully scaled, winged, and bound to a hoard from the moment they first take something and refuse to give it back. The binding is not greed and cannot be reasoned out of them. A Prime's power scales directly with what they have gathered — a Prime with nothing is barely stronger than a normal dragonkin, and a Prime with a well-stocked hoard is a regional problem.

The practical consequence is that Prime Dragonkin are the easiest of all Primes to defeat. You do not fight them. You find the hoard.

### Physical
9ft, full scale coverage, functional wings. Reads as a small drake standing upright rather than a person with claws.

### Hoard-Bound
Designate a hoard. Gain +1 to every stat per major treasure in it, and Dragon Breath scales with its total worth. If the hoard is taken or scattered, you lose all of it until you reclaim it.

## Ratmen (Prime)
base: Ratmen

The joke is that ratmen are cowards. The truth is that ratmen are plural and have simply never mentioned it.

A Prime is a swarm holding a shape, and the shape slips at the edges when they are tired or hurried. Single blades do not kill them; you cannot stab a crowd. Only fire, flood, and area magic have ever put one down, which means every hunter who has gone after a Prime with a sword has come back either empty-handed or not at all.

Ratmen do not consider a Prime an individual and get visibly uncomfortable when asked to. The common tongue has no word for what a Prime is, and rat does, and they will not translate it.

### Physical
Not one creature. A swarm holding a person's shape, and it shows at the edges.

### Not One Rat
Single-target damage cannot reduce you below 1 HP — only area effects can kill you. You may disperse to squeeze through anything and reform within 60ft.

## Demonborn (Prime)
base: Demonborn

Not descendants. Not touched. Demons, born in the material world to mortal parents, with all the paperwork that implies.

Their blood is demonfire and their wounds vent flame rather than bleed, which makes injury a spectacle. What matters far more is the Contract: a Prime can speak a deal aloud, and if the other party agrees, both are bound — no ritual, no signature, no witnesses required. Breaking it kills you wherever you are.

This has made Prime Demonborn the most sought-after neutral arbitrators in the world. Treaties signed through one hold. Merchants pay enormously for their attendance. Nobody has ever successfully cheated one, and roughly a dozen people a year die learning that the offer was genuine.

### Physical
A true demon rather than a descendant. Blood is demonfire, and wounds vent flame rather than bleed.

### Contract
Offer any creature a spoken deal. If accepted, both parties are magically bound to it. Whoever breaks it takes damage equal to your maximum HP, wherever they are.

## Dweller (Prime)
base: Dweller

The dwellers went down and kept going. Primes are what was at the bottom.

Nine feet, eight arms, and the eye sockets sealed over entirely — they have no use for them and stopped growing them somewhere back down the line. What surrounds a Prime instead is thirty feet of total silence and total darkness, absolute, permanent, and unremovable. Inside it they see perfectly by tremor. Everything else, including their own allies, is blind and deaf.

A Prime cannot be part of a formation, cannot be signalled, and cannot signal. Dweller communities house them at the outer edges of a settlement, facing outward, which is exactly what they are for.

### Physical
9ft, eight arms, and no eyes at all — the sockets have sealed over.

### Deep Silence
A permanent 30ft radius of absolute darkness and total soundlessness. You navigate it perfectly by tremorsense. Everything else inside it is blinded and deafened, allies included.

## Lost (Prime)
base: Lost

Every Lost is a soul that refused the crossing. A Prime is a soul the crossing has stopped arguing with.

They wear the wounds that killed them, permanently and without healing, and they do not die again until the thing they stayed for is finished. Damage that ought to kill a Prime instead does something to the world around them — the clock jumps, a night passes in a step, a season ends. Nobody has satisfactorily explained where the time goes, and Primes remember all of it.

The Lost regard Primes with something between reverence and pity. Every Prime is proof that the goal can be held onto. Every Prime is also proof of how long that can take.

### Physical
Visibly, unmistakably dead, still wearing the wounds that did it.

### Unfinished
You cannot die while your goal remains incomplete. Damage that would kill you instead advances time around you — hours, days, occasionally longer — at the DM's discretion. Necrotic damage heals you.

## Slimefolk (Prime)
base: Slimefolk

Slimefolk take a humanoid shape out of politeness. Primes have found the politeness costs more than it returns.

Vastly greater volume, no fixed outline, and a body that is functionally a container. Anything a Prime has fully absorbed stays available — a key swallowed forty years ago comes back out clean. From anything they have consumed that was alive, they can wear a trait, one at a time, swapping as needed.

Slimefolk communities are entirely comfortable with this. Everyone else has questions about what is in there, and Primes have learned not to answer them at dinner.

### Physical
Enormous volume, and no more pretence at a humanoid outline unless it is actively useful.

### Everything I've Eaten
Anything you have fully absorbed can be re-manifested out of your body at will — weapons, tools, keys. From any creature you have consumed, you may also wear one of its traits at a time.

## Forsaken (Prime)
base: Forsaken

The gods cursed the Forsaken to suffer forever as an example. Primes are the example working correctly and everyone hating it.

Half a Prime is already marble before they turn thirty, and the stone visibly advances during combat and retreats afterward, so anyone fighting one can watch the curse working in real time. The Prime feels every inch of it.

What makes them Prime is the ending. When a Prime dies, whatever killed them becomes Forsaken too. Not cursed by proxy — genuinely, fully Forsaken, with all of it. Word of this has spread. Primes are among the most heavily avoided beings alive, and a handful have leveraged that into remarkably peaceful lives.

### Physical
Half of them is already marble, and the stone visibly spreads during combat before receding.

### Shared Eternity
When you die, you drag whatever killed you into the curse with you. They become Forsaken. They will not thank you, and they will find you.

## Fallen (Prime)
base: Fallen

The Fallen are descendants. A Prime is not a descendant. A Prime is an angel that was born here, and the higher planes have not commented.

Full wings from birth that do not fold away, which means a Prime cannot pass for anything and never has. What they carry is Judgement: name a crime aloud, and everyone in sight who committed it takes the weight of exactly how guilty they were. The Prime feels each measurement precisely, including for people they love, including for themselves.

Most Primes use it once and then never again. The ones who use it regularly are, without exception, worse people than they were when they started.

### Physical
An actual angel, not a descendant. Full wings from birth, and they do not fold away.

### Judgement
Once per long rest, name a crime aloud. Every creature in sight who has committed it takes damage proportional to their guilt, as adjudicated by the DM. You will know exactly how guilty each of them was.

## Wood Elf (Prime)
base: Wood Elf

Wood elves protect forests. Primes are *part of the network*, and it took a long time for anyone to work out what that meant.

Antlers, bark grain, moss for hair, and no ageing at all inside a wood. The critical part is invisible: every forest a Prime has slept in is linked, permanently, and they can step between any two and bring whoever is holding on. A Prime who has travelled widely is a transit system.

High elves consider this a vulgar shortcut around properly earned magic. Wood elves consider high elves to be people who have never walked anywhere. The argument is old.

### Physical
Antlers, bark-grained skin, hair that is genuinely moss. Does not age at all while inside a forest.

### The Wood Remembers
Every forest you have spent a night in is permanently linked. You may step from any one of them into any other, and bring anyone touching you.

## Dryad (Prime)
base: Dryad & Treekin

Forests make dryads when they are threatened. A Prime is what a forest makes when it is threatened and has *time*.

Permanently in flower, faintly lit, bark smoothed to something closer to polished heartwood. Somewhere within a mile a Prime has planted a single seed, and if she dies near it she regrows there over a month, whole. She feels it constantly, the way you feel a held breath, and she knows the instant anything happens to it.

Dryads consider the seed intensely private. Asking a Prime where hers is planted is roughly equivalent to asking where she keeps her heart, and is answered about as warmly.

### Physical
Permanently flowering and faintly luminous, with bark polished smooth.

### Second Root
Plant a seed anywhere. If you die within a mile of it, you regrow from it in a month with everything intact. You may only have one planted at a time and you will feel it if it is destroyed.

## Treekin (Prime)
base: Dryad & Treekin

Fifteen feet of old growth, too wide for any door built by people who did not expect them.

Primes are made by forests that intend to *hold ground* rather than reclaim it. Where an ordinary treekin roots to heal, a Prime roots to make a position — everyone within twenty feet grows thorn cover and shares the regeneration, and nothing hostile inside the radius gets to leave.

The tradeoff is that a rooted Prime has made a permanent decision about where the fight happens. Treekin do not root casually and Primes root once, at the moment they judge to be the one that matters, and every Prime has an opinion about which moment that will be.

### Physical
15ft of old growth. Wider than a doorway and aware of it.

### Standing Grove
When you root, everyone within 20ft gains your temp HP regeneration and grows thorn cover of their own. You may not move, and neither can anything hostile that tries to leave.

## Half Blood (Prime)
base: Half Blood

Vampirism has two forms: the ritual, which makes pure bloods, and the disease, which makes half bloods. Everyone assumed the disease was the lesser version. The Primes are the disease deciding otherwise.

No reflection, no shadow, and blood moving visibly beneath the skin that is frequently not their own. A Prime's bite spreads the condition on a failed save, and they retain up to three stolen traits at a time from everyone they have drained, swapping them out like coats.

Pure bloods find this obscene. The disease does not need a ritual, a lineage, a master, or permission, and a single Prime with no self-control can produce more half bloods in a season than a vampire court manages in a century. There have been purges. They have not worked.

### Physical
The disease perfected rather than cured. No reflection, no shadow, and the blood visible moving under the skin is not always theirs.

### Contagion
Anyone you blood drain becomes a half blood on a failed save. You permanently retain one stolen trait per drained target, up to three at once, and may swap them out with a fresh drain.

## Siren Spawn (Prime)
base: Siren Spawn

The survivors of siren encounters had children. Some of those children were not diluted at all.

Full siren anatomy — gills, taloned feet, and a second throat that lets a Prime hold two notes at once, which is what makes the difference. Hearing anyone speak tells a Prime what that person actually wants, underneath whatever they said. The song no longer stuns. It instructs, one action per listener, and the listener will remember it as their own idea and defend it.

Coastal law in most nations treats an unregistered Prime as a standing hazard. Several courts employ one anyway, and are not especially subtle about why.

### Physical
Full siren anatomy — gills, taloned feet, and a second throat that lets them hold two notes at once.

### The Name
Hearing any creature speak reveals their truest want. Siren's Song no longer stuns — instead you command one action from each affected target, and they will believe it was their idea.

## Dark Gnome (Prime)
base: Gnome (Dark Variant)

No researcher has come back from a dark gnome settlement. The few reports that exist are from people who met a Prime somewhere else and did not stay long.

Mandibles, chitin across the back and forearms, eyes arranged in a pattern that is definitely not two. The hyperactivity that costs ordinary dark gnomes a stunned turn simply does not end in a Prime, and every kill buys another action, and there is no ceiling on it. A Prime in a large enough fight becomes, briefly, difficult to perceive as taking turns at all.

Gnome tribes that revere insects treat Primes as the pattern completed. Everyone else has stopped sending researchers.

### Physical
Mandibles, chitin plating across the back and forearms, and eyes arranged wrong.

### Swarm Logic
Hyperactive is permanent with no ending stun. Every kill you land grants an additional action next turn, and this does not cap.

## Ashen (Prime)
base: Ashen

All Ashen trace back to five men. What the records do not say is whether those five were Primes or simply the first, and Primes have not settled the question by existing.

Flame feathers along the arms and spine, coals for eyes, and a death that is not a death. A Prime who is killed detonates, and the blast revives every ally within sixty feet to full, and the Prime reforms from their own ash within a few days regardless of what did it.

The effect on tactics is total. Parties who field a Prime fight differently, and Primes know exactly how they are being used, and most of them have made peace with it. A few have not, and those ones are careful never to tell anyone what they are.

### Physical
Feathers of open flame along the arms and spine. The eyes are coals.

### The Pyre
When you die you detonate. Every ally within 60ft is revived to full HP by the blast, and you reform from your own ash in 1d4 days regardless of what killed you.

## Echoforged (Prime)
base: Echoforged

Ordinary Echoforged hold what was meant to be remembered. A Prime *is* the archive, and the chest cavity is open, and other people can reach into it.

They form where erasure was most thorough — the libraries burned to ash rather than merely burned, the massacres where even the names went. Standing anywhere, a Prime can replay what happened there as a visible scene, centuries deep. Once a day they can reach into one of those echoes and pull out a dead person's skill and wear it for an afternoon.

Historians hire them. Governments try to prevent this. Several nations have laws against Primes standing in specific rooms.

### Physical
Fully hollow, the chest cavity an open and readable archive that others can reach into.

### Recitation
Replay any event that happened where you stand as a visible illusion, centuries back if need be. Once per long rest, pull a dead person's skill out of the echo and use it as your own for the scene.

## Mosskin (Prime)
base: Mosskin

Ten feet of walking grove. Things live in them. Primes are aware of this and have decided it is fine.

A Prime is made by woodland that has been neglected for a very long time and has stopped waiting. Everything a Prime plants grows a century in a day, which means a Prime can raise a defensible wood overnight where there was bare stone in the evening.

This has made them enormously valuable in reconstruction and enormously contentious in border disputes, since a forest that was not there yesterday raises awkward questions about whose land it is. At least two wars have started this way and the Primes involved were not consulted before or after.

### Physical
10ft of walking grove. Birds nest in them, and they do not consider this a problem.

### Old Growth
Anything you plant grows a century in a day. Over a single long rest you can raise a defensible woodland where there was bare ground.

## Tideborn (Prime)
base: Tideborn

Tideborn are the ocean's memory, thinned by generations of living where the light reaches. Primes are trench-born and remember all of it.

Bioluminescent, half-translucent, and visibly in pain in open air, which limits how long they can be useful on land. In exchange, a Prime can be asked anything about any event that happened on, in, or near water at any point in history, and will answer, and will be right.

Every maritime power wants one. Very few can keep one alive above the surface long enough to matter. The going arrangement is a tank, a contract, and a great deal of guilt.

### Physical
Trench-adapted — bioluminescent, half-translucent, and in visible pain on dry land.

### Ancestral Tide
The ocean's full memory is open to you. Ask the DM any question about any event that occurred on, in, or near water, at any point in history. Call of the Abyss from creation.

## Dreamstrider (Prime)
base: Dreamstrider

A vision imprints on an unborn child and makes a Dreamstrider. A Prime is what happens when the vision does not stop.

Barely corporeal, no shadow, and a reflection that lags half a second behind. What sets a Prime apart is the ability to declare the last ten minutes a dream and have that be true. It did not happen. Only the Prime remembers it, and only the Prime can act on what they learned in it.

The obvious question is how often this has already been done to the world, and by whom, and Primes are the only people who could answer and they do not.

### Physical
Barely corporeal. Casts no shadow and appears in mirrors a half-second late.

### The Waking Dream
Once per long rest, declare the last ten minutes a dream. They did not happen. Only you remember them, and only you can act on what you learned.

## Cinderfray (Prime)
base: Cinderfray

Cinderfrays come from volcanoes that are dying. Primes come from volcanoes that are not finished.

Molten rather than cooled, with no igneous plating containing anything, and a fissure across the chest that widens every round of a fight. Ten rounds and it goes — sixty feet, allies included, no exceptions. A Prime can vent early and eat the damage themselves to reset it, and most do, and most Primes are covered in the evidence of having done so.

Cinderwalk clans do not exile their Primes. They build differently around them: wide camps, long sightlines, and a standing rule that the Prime fights at the edge.

### Physical
Still erupting. Molten rather than cooled, with no igneous plating containing any of it.

### Countdown
A fissure across your chest widens every combat round. On round ten you erupt for catastrophic damage in 60ft, allies included. You may vent early as an action to reset the count and take the damage yourself instead.

## Huskling (Prime)
base: Huskling

A caster dies mid-ritual and leaves a Huskling. A Prime is left by a ritual that was *large*, and it is still going.

There are no bones in a Prime at all. What holds the silhouette together is the unfinished working itself, suspended and audible if you stand close enough. Once a day a Prime can let a piece of it complete — casting any spell, at any tier, without knowing it, without a slot, without any idea in advance which one it will be.

Hucklings regard Primes as walking unexploded ordnance and are entirely correct. No Prime has ever learned who they used to be, and most have stopped looking.

### Physical
No bones at all — suspended arcane energy holding the silhouette of the person it used to be.

### Unfinished Ritual
The spell that killed your originator is still casting inside you. Once per long rest, finish it: cast any single spell at any tier, free, without knowing it.

## Galeborn (Prime)
base: Galeborn

Galeborn are born from storms that lingered. Primes are born from storms that never left, and they have never touched the ground.

Nothing below the knee but a sustained vortex. The storm that made them is still with them — a permanent thirty-foot weather system that batters everything hostile inside it and leaves allies entirely alone, which nobody has ever explained and Primes cannot control.

Sky Trail navigation is trivial for them. Ordinary Galeborn tribes treat a Prime's arrival as good fortune and terrible logistics, since nothing in the camp stays where it was put.

### Physical
Nothing below the knee but a sustained vortex. They have never touched the ground.

### Eye of the Storm
A permanent personal storm follows you. Enemies within 30ft take 1d6 per turn and have disadvantage on ranged attacks. Allies are untouched. Permanent flight.

## Rimeblood (Prime)
base: Rimeblood

The first Rimeblood was made by a killing frost. A Prime is made by a winter that killed a great many people at once.

Eight feet, with ice growing outward in spurs that regrow within hours of being broken off. Anything a Prime kills freezes solid and never rots. Shattering one grants the Prime a permanent increase — but a frozen kill can also be thawed, later, by anyone with enough heat, alive and entirely aware of how long they were in there.

Prime Rimebloods therefore keep collections, and their collections are political. More than one northern territory is governed by the fact that a Prime is holding somebody's heir in a cave.

### Physical
8ft with ice growing outward from the body in spurs that regrow when broken.

### The Long Winter
Anything you kill freezes solid and never rots. Shatter one for a permanent +1 to max HP — or leave it, and it can be thawed later by a strong enough heat source, alive and extremely aware of how long it was in there.

## Hollow Veil (Prime)
base: Hollow Veil

Ordinary Hollow Veils are severed shadows that woke up. Primes are inverted: they cast a shadow rather than being one, and from certain angles they have no depth at all.

Every light a Prime consumes is stored rather than spent. They can release the accumulated total at once as raw radiance, or spend it to remove themselves from a witness's memory entirely — which is why nobody can produce a reliable account of one.

The uncomfortable theory, held by Luminkin and denied everywhere else, is that a Prime Hollow Veil is not a severed shadow at all but a Luminkin who broke the taboo and kept going. The taboo says light made through cruelty dims a Luminkin irrevocably. It does not say what happens if they do not stop.

### Physical
Inverted. They cast a shadow rather than being one, and read as two-dimensional from the wrong angle.

### Eaten Light
Every light source you consume is stored. Release the whole stored total at once as radiant damage — or spend it to erase yourself from a witness's memory entirely.

## Verdant Warden (Prime)
base: Verdant Warden

Wardens are made when a grove fuses its oldest trees into a soldier. A Prime is made when the grove has correctly assessed the size of the thing coming.

Twenty-five feet. Built to the scale of the threat rather than the forest. And when a Prime falls, it does not fall — it bursts into a full ironwood thicket that keeps fighting for several rounds while the Prime regrows inside it. If the thicket holds, so does the Prime.

Nobody has ever confirmed a Prime Warden dying permanently. There are three or four standing groves that people believe are Primes that never finished regrowing, and nobody has been willing to test it.

### Physical
25ft. Built at the scale of the thing that threatened the forest, not the forest.

### Felling
At 0 HP you burst into a full ironwood thicket that keeps fighting for 1d6 turns while you regrow inside it. If the thicket survives the fight, so do you.

## Luminkin (Prime)
base: Luminkin

Every Luminkin carries a fragment of the first spark. A Prime carries an everfire, openly, in the chest cavity, and the wicksoul never dims with age.

An old Prime is dim. This is not decay. It is a record. The Lamplighter Saints are almost all Primes, and the powder ground from their bones burns longer than anything else known.

An everfire burns openly in the chest cavity, and the wicksoul never dims with age.

### Kindling

## Pebblekin (Prime)
base: Pebblekin

A wandering stone that has seen enough history grows legs. A Prime is a stone that swallowed a geode and let it win.

Crystal has grown through the entire body and the Prime has stopped resisting it, which is what separates a Prime from a Pebblekin who is simply dying. Touch any rock and a Prime sees what happened near it — and can pull one of those witnessed lives out and wear the skill of whoever they saw for about an hour.

Conglomerations treat Primes as archives with legs and consult them constantly. A Prime who has walked a long road is worth more than most libraries and moves considerably slower.

### Physical
Geode-bodied. The crystal has grown through them completely and they have stopped resisting it.

## Tideripples (Prime)
base: Tideripples

Ordinary Tideripples can dissolve. A Prime never solidified in the first place, and the shape slips when they are tired.

They can pour themselves into any body of water and travel its entire length in an instant, emerging anywhere it reaches — which means a Prime who has found a river system has found a country. What they do to people is worse: a touch pulls the moisture out, and there is no defence against it that anyone has published.

The Currents treat Primes as navigators and keep them at the front. Nobody has yet mapped how far a single Prime can actually get through connected water, and several nations would prefer not to find out.

### Physical
Never solid. Permanently water held in a shape by intent alone, and the shape slips when they are tired.

## Hearthlings (Prime)
base: Hearthlings

Seven generations of continuous fire makes a Hearthling. Centuries makes a Prime, and the smoke coming off one carries memories that belong to people nobody alive has met.

Ember-cored and warm to stand near. What makes a Prime is the table: a meal they cook grants everyone who eats it the full benefit of a long rest plus a shared blessing that lasts a day. It takes four hours and the Prime must know every diner's name, which is not a mechanical requirement so much as the whole point.

A Prime who has been displaced from their fire is one of the saddest things in the world, and other Hearthlings will go a very long way to prevent it.

### Physical
Born of a fire that burned for centuries rather than generations. Ember-cored, and the smoke carries other people's memories.

### The Long Table

## Dapplehooves (Prime)

Dapplehooves know every shortcut. Primes make them.

Full centaur scale at 7ft, without losing a step of speed.

## Bloomsprites (Prime)

The assumption was that a Prime Bloomsprite would be larger. It is smaller — thumb-sized, nearly invisible among flowers, and able to unfold into twelve feet of layered petal and heartwood when it decides to stop being subtle.

### Physical
Smaller, not larger — thumb-sized. But they can unfold into a 12ft blossom-construct of layered petal and heartwood.

## Mistweavers (Prime)
base: Mistweavers

Ordinary Mistweavers can become fog. Primes never left it — the humanoid shape is the effort and they cannot hold it while sleeping.

Never solid at any point. The humanoid shape is the effort and the fog is the resting state.

Duskpaws watch thresholds. Primes hold them.

Larger, and able to drop into a true quadrupedal hunting form that leaves no trace at all.

## Clockwork Cherubs (Prime)

Adult-sized with six wings. Built to seraph specification, not cherub.

## Parchmentkin (Prime)
base: Parchmentkin

A spellbook takes too much energy and stands up. A Prime is an entire library doing it, and the text stands off the skin in three dimensions.

### Physical
An entire library rather than a single book, with text standing off the skin in three dimensions.

## Gorgonblood (Prime)

## Aesirkin (Prime)

## Fablehoof (Prime)
base: Fablehoof

A Fablehoof manifests when a heroic tale grows strong enough. A Prime manifests from a tale that everyone already knows, and strangers recognise them on sight without being able to say from where.

## Chimera-Touched (Prime)
base: Chimera-Touched

The Chimera-Touched are stitched. A Prime is a true chimera — three heads, three sets of instincts, and one body they have to negotiate over.

## Rookborn (Prime)
base: Rookborn

Parliaments have never had leaders and have always reached decisions anyway. Nobody outside a Parliament has ever been able to describe the process. Prime Rookborn are the reason, and they are why the description was never going to work.

An ordinary Rookborn shares what they know. A Prime does not share, because there is no boundary across which sharing would happen. What one bird sees, they have seen. A Prime standing in a room in one city is also standing on a rooftop four hundred miles away and has been for their entire life, and they have never experienced this as remarkable. They use the plural for themselves without noticing they are doing it, and correct themselves only when they see the other person's face change.

The difficulty is legal rather than martial. A Prime cannot testify, because there is no way to establish which of them witnessed anything. A Prime cannot be imprisoned in any meaningful sense. At least two cities have quietly rewritten their statutes around this, and both times the Prime knew about the rewrite before the clerks did.

### Physical
Larger than their kin and moulting constantly, with feathers that never fully settle. Birds gather wherever they stop and do not disperse when they leave.

### Parliament
You perceive everything perceived by any bird within a mile, simultaneously and without effort. You may speak through any of them. You cannot be surprised, ambushed, or followed. In any settlement you have been in for a day, there is nothing happening outdoors that you do not know about.

## Chrysalid (Prime)
base: Chrysalid

Cocooning twice is dangerous. Three times is not survivable. Primes did not stop at three.

A Prime Chrysalid never finished emerging and never will. Their plating is always partially soft, always resetting somewhere on the body, and they run several degrees too warm to the touch. They can go down and come back up in hours rather than weeks, which means a Prime who is losing a fight can leave it and return to the same fight built specifically to win it.

Chrysalid crèches regard Primes with open unease. The entire structure of their society rests on the idea that emergence is final — that the larval years are a question and the adult form is the answer, and that you then live with the answer. Primes do not live with anything. Older Primes have been observed to lose track of which form was originally theirs, and the crèches consider this the actual horror, rather than the survival rate.

### Physical
Never finished. Wings, plating and limb structure visibly reforming somewhere on the body at all times, and no two descriptions of the same Prime a year apart will match.

### Unset
You may cocoon in one hour and emerge with any combination of forms, traits, resistances or physical capabilities suited to a problem you have personally encountered. You may do this once per long rest. Each time, the DM removes something from you at random — a trait, a skill, or a memory — and does not tell you which.

## Bayborn (Prime)
base: Bayborn

Ordinary Bayborn find a pack. Primes arrive as one.

If you meet one Prime, you have met all of them, and the others already know your face and are already moving.

### Physical
Larger and heavier than their kin, and marked identically to their littermates down to the scarring, including scars they did not personally receive.

### One Animal
You share sensation, position and condition with every member of your litter at any distance, permanently. Any of you may act on what any other knows. Damage dealt to one may be voluntarily distributed across all. If you are the last of your litter, you permanently gain the full Shoulder bonus regardless of who is beside you, and you never stop looking.

## Hibernal (Prime)
base: Hibernal

Every Hibernal banks the year. A Prime banks the decade, and there is no reliable way to wake one early.

They go down when they judge it is time, which may be after two ordinary winters or after thirty, and they do not announce it. What comes back up is carrying more than a body has any business holding — Primes have been recorded waking with reserves that took a season to spend and that they spent, in one case, on a single afternoon that is still in the regional histories.

The practical problem is that a sleeping Prime does not stop growing. Halls built around one have to be rebuilt around it, then abandoned, then rebuilt further out. There are at least four mountains on the northern range that local people will tell you are Hibernal, and will decline to elaborate, and will not build on.

Waking one deliberately is the gravest crime in the Hibernal code and the only one for which they have no stated punishment, on the grounds that the Prime will handle it.

### Physical
No fixed size. A Prime is as large as the length of their last sleep made them, and they continue growing while under.

### The Deep Year
Your Stored pool has no cap and does not expire. Every full year spent hibernating adds 4d6. You may spend any number of Stored dice at once on a single roll, action or attack. You cannot be woken by any means before you are finished, and you do not know in advance how long that will be.

## Deepmaw (Prime)
base: Deepmaw

An ordinary Deepmaw's lure is attached to them. This is the only thing that makes them survivable to be near.

A Prime's is not. Primes project the light, and they can put it anywhere they have personally been, and it stays after they have gone. There are pale lights burning at the bottom of wells, in unused stairwells, at the far end of quiet roads, in parts of the world where nobody has reported a Deepmaw in living memory. Most of them are not attached to anything yet. The Prime knows where every one of them is and what has walked toward it.

They are patient beyond the point where patience is a virtue. A Prime who has decided on something will set a light and then not think about it for years. Coastal authorities in the deep-water ports have an informal register of known lights and a standing policy of leaving them entirely alone, on the reasoning that the alternative is finding out who they belong to.

### Physical
Longer and thinner than their kin, with no lure filament at all — the brow is smooth. Their mouth does not close even slightly.

### Set a Light
You may place a lure at any location you have physically stood. It persists indefinitely, requires no attention, and emits no visible source. Any creature that comes within 30ft of one must make a wisdom roll or begin travelling toward it, and will not be able to explain why. You know the position and status of every light you have ever set, and you may see and speak through any of them.
