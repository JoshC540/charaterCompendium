# How to read this book

Nearly six hundred gods and powers are worshipped across every world and plane the Maker made, Pemmaroth included. A god may go by other names on other worlds, but it is the same god. They are sorted by rank. Nearly every lesser god serves in a court led by one of the Twelve Thrones, or in the Night Court of Hircisos. Many gods share a domain, but each one holds a different **aspect** of it: Quapetheia is how time feels, Nixegan is time passing that nothing can stop, and Prerun is the time that might have been.

## Divine ranks

| **Rank** | **Who** | **How they are worshipped** |
| **Primordial** | The Maker, Pluras | Acknowledged, almost never worshipped. The Maker is everything that is; Pluras is everything that isn't. |
| **Old God** | The Sleeping Nine | Bloodline cults who believe they descend from them. Magic is said to be their dreaming. |
| **Erased** | The Namewright | Nobody remembers his name. Only traces of him are left: the paths and tiers of magic, the Index, and House Aldemar. |
| **Throne** | The Twelve Thrones | Great temples on every world, and a seat on the High Court. |
| **Contested** | Hircisos | Claims a thirteenth seat the other Thrones won't recognise. |
| **Lesser** | Members of a court | Shrines, orders and guilds. Most mortals pray to these. |
| **Minor** | Household gods and godlings | Small altars, kitchen charms, sailors' habits. |
| **New, fallen and outcast** | Iodia, Raluto, Galgedio | Few followers, but they believe fiercely. |
| **Demigod** | Children of a god and a mortal | Hero cults and noble houses (Aldemaris, mother of House Aldemar). |

Gods also gather in **divine circles**, groups bound by origin rather than by court: the Seven Hungers, the Elemental Lords, the Seven Wandering Stars, the Lost Pantheon and the Wild Hunt. The twenty-three dragon gods have a section of their own. Later in the book come the demigods and ascended mortals, and then tables for the gods of every people, the gods of places, the planar courts and the gods of monsters. The archfiends and the angelic Choirs are powers, not gods, but they deal with gods as equals.

A dashed box marks a god outside the order: erased, or not yet seated.

## How an entry is laid out

  - **Throne gods** get a full entry: overview, appearance, personality, myths, worship, followers, holy symbol, signs, relationships, a paladin oath, Favour and Taboo. Each also has a Faith and lore section: other names, sacred things, realm, herald, avatar, clergy, holy days, rites of life, a prayer, sects, relics and one more myth.
  - **Lesser gods** get a half-page: aspect, nature, followers, tenets, Favour and Taboo. Some, like the gods of vengeance, have a full paladin oath.
  - **Minor gods** get a short card.
  - **Favour** and **Taboo** are explained in *Faith in play*. Favour is the god's small blessing for keeping its tenets. Breaking its Taboo costs you that blessing until you atone.

# The shape of the heavens

Every god comes from the Maker in the end. Gods are born when mortals need them and fade when mortals forget them, and since the Erasure they have all been bound by the Compact of Thrones. The same gods watch over every world and plane. A farmer on Pemmaroth and a farmer on a world three planes away both pray to Samis, even if they call her by different names.

## Creation

Before anything was, there were the Maker and Pluras. They are not two beings but two sides of one. The Maker is everything that is. Pluras is everything that isn't, and so it knows everything that could be.

The Maker made the worlds and the planes between them, pushing them up out of Pluras like islands from a dark sea. Pemmaroth is one of those islands, among more than any mortal has counted. Pluras did not resist. It only waits, because everything the Maker makes, Pluras will one day have back.

The Maker's first child was N'zoth. When the first mortal died, N'zoth wrote it down, and the Ledger began.

## The four ages

| **Age** | **What happened** | **What it left behind** |
| **The Making** | The Maker shapes the worlds and planes from the edge of the Void. N'zoth opens the Ledger. | The worlds, the planes, the Ledger |
| **The Age of Beasts** | The Nine Old Gods walk the worlds in beast form beside the Seven Colossi, while the Leviathans rule sea and sky. Raw magic pours off the Nine, and mortals who serve them take their blood. The War of the Deep kills the Colossi and drives off the Leviathans, and all nine Old Gods fall into the Long Sleep. | Magic, the bloodline cults, the sleeping places, the bones of the Colossi |
| **The Naming** | Younger gods are born from mortal need, one after another. The Namewright divides wild magic into named paths and tiers so mortals can use it without burning. Then he is erased, and the Twelve Thrones are seated. | The paths and tiers of magic, the Index, House Aldemar, the High Court |
| **The Ledger Age (now)** | The gods rule through faith, not presence, under the Compact of Thrones. A mortal hero has ascended as the Hollow God, and has already broken the Compact. Raluto has fallen, and Cupather stirs unborn. | Today's temples, oaths and wars |

## The Rule of Aspects

A god is born when mortals need something badly, and in a way no existing god answers. That is why most domains have several gods. The **domain** is what a god rules. The **aspect** is which part of it, and for whom.

  - **Cold:** Mitia is the cold itself. Rine is ice as a thing, the glacier and the frozen bridge. Theria is the frost that protects, the snow blanket that keeps seed alive. Beneath them all, the Old God Sigmund sleeps, and ice magic is his dreaming.
  - **Sleep:** Nuxitia decides when sleep comes. Argari is what sleep does: it rests you, or it is a little death. Ziios rules what you dream.
  - **The sea:** Renos is the sea's cunning, Pimis its warmth, Neleros its floods and Prames its tides. Pheseus rules rivers, Dirnutis sailors and Thominus the fish.

Gods who share a domain compete for the same prayers, so they are rarely at peace. Mortals choose the aspect that fits their need: a soldier who wants to survive prays to Pestus for bravery, and one who wants to win prays to Cosamis.

## How gods rise, fall and die

| **State** | **What it means** | **Example** |
| **Unborn** | The need is felt across the worlds, but the god is not yet whole. | Cupather, the Unborn God of Gluttony |
| **Born** | Need finds no answer, so something answers it. | Thevia, born the day mortals learned that peace has a price |
| **Rising** | It grows with worship, temples, oaths and deeds done in its name. | Most lesser gods |
| **Fading** | It is forgotten, and grows quieter and weaker. | Bisus, the Forgotten Prince of Peace |
| **Fallen** | Its worship drops below what it needs to hold its domain, and it becomes almost mortal. | Raluto, the Young God of Courage |
| **Bound** | It is chained by the High Court, or by itself. | Prames, the Bound God of Tides; Thaxucus, who chained himself |
| **Dead** | It is slain by a godslayer. Its domain passes to an aspect-sibling, or a new god is born to hold it. | Rare, and never forgotten |
| **Erased** | Its name, worship and memory are cut out of the worlds. | The Namewright |
| **Ascended** | A mortal is offered godhood and takes it. | Iodia, the Hollow God, once a mortal adventurer (see the Ascendant Mark) |

## The Compact of Thrones

After the Erasure, the gods agreed six laws. Breaking one brings the High Court to judgement. The punishments are binding, exile or erasure.

1. **No god walks a mortal world in its true form. Gods act through avatars, signs, servants and champions. *The Hollow God has broken this.***
2. Every soul is written in the Ledger before it is claimed. A god may take a sworn soul only after N'zoth has written its death.
3. **The Old Gods are not to be woken.**
4. **The erased are not to be named.**
5. **Galgedio may keep no mortal followers. *He keeps them anyway.***
6. No Throne makes war on another Throne directly. They fight through mortals instead, which is why holy wars never end.

# The Primordials

The Maker and Pluras are older than the gods, and neither of them sits on the High Court. Every other god is either something the Maker made or something Pluras will one day take back.

## The Maker, God of All Things

**Rank:** Primordial · **Domain:** everything that is · **Holy symbol:** a circle with a single point at its centre, the first thing ever made

**Overview.** The Maker made the worlds, the planes and the first of the gods, and it has not spoken since the Making ended. Some believe it is still at work: every new star and every newborn god is the Maker, making. Others believe it sleeps, or died, or became the worlds themselves. The Thrones never say which, and that silence is the oldest argument in theology.

**Appearance.** None. Each world draws the Maker differently: a potter, a weaver, a pair of hands with no body, a light at the bottom of a well. The oldest carvings show nothing but the circle and its point.

**Nature.** As far as any priest can tell, the Maker does not judge. It makes, and what it makes is allowed to go wrong. The gods say this is why mortals have free will and why the Void is patient.

**Worship.** There are almost no temples to the Maker alone. Instead, every temple of every god keeps one empty plinth, the *Maker's Seat*, and an oath sworn before that seat is the most binding a mortal can make. A small faith called the **Makerists** holds that the Thrones are stewards who forgot they were stewards, and that mortals should pray to the Maker directly. The High Court tolerates them, barely.

**Children.** N'zoth was the first. Priests argue over the rest: when the Rule of Aspects makes a new god, is that the Maker answering through mortal need, or mortal need answering itself?

**Relationships.** Pluras is its other side, not its enemy. Each defines the other. N'zoth is its son, and the only god who claims to have heard the Maker's voice.

## Pluras, Omniscient Bringer of the Void

*Most of this page is an ink blot.*

| **If you have read this page, bad luck. Roll a WIS save with disadvantage. Pass or fail, tell the DM.** |

**Rank:** Primordial · **Domain:** everything that isn't: the Void, the unmade, the unwritten · **Holy symbol:** a solid black circle, the Maker's circle with nothing left inside it

**Overview.** Pluras is the other side of the Maker, and it is objectively terrifying. It is the dark sea the worlds were lifted out of. Because it holds everything that isn't, it holds everything that *could* be, and so it knows everything. Things that are unmade go back to it, and so does everything that is erased. If the Namewright's name still exists anywhere, it is in Pluras.

**Appearance.** Nobody can describe it, and no description survives. Ink written about Pluras spreads into a blot, paint turns black and carvings crack. Every holy book that mentions it has the same stain on the same page. This book is no exception.

**Nature.** Pluras isn't cruel, or hungry, or angry. It is patient beyond anything a mortal can bear to think about. It doesn't take things. It waits for them to come back. What drives mortals mad isn't malice but attention: when Pluras notices you, it knows you completely, and you feel all of it at once.

**Followers.** Pluras has no priests, but some mortals worship it anyway:

  - **The Unwritten:** cultists who burn their names out of every record so that N'zoth can't write their deaths, believing they will return to the Void whole.
  - **The Void-touched**, who carry a sliver of it in their bodies and minds, whether they wanted to or not.
  - Desperate scholars who ask it the questions no god will answer, and hope the price is small.

**Signs.** A sudden silence in which you can hear your own heartbeat too clearly. Text that blurs. The feeling of being known.

**Relationships.** It is the Maker's other side. It waits on N'zoth's Ledger, because what the Ledger loses, Pluras keeps. Phisus, the God of Nothingness, worships it the way a candle might worship the dark. Termiya, the Hidden Goddess of Oblivion, is the door things fall through on their way to it.

**The Oath of the Blank Page.** Pluras accepts no paladins. Some swear to it anyway.

  - *Unmake:* what should not be, return.
  - *Know:* ask the question, whatever it costs.
  - *Wait:* all things come back.
  - *Unwrite:* leave nothing for the Ledger.

# The Old Gods: the Sleeping Nine

The Nine are the first gods, older than the Thrones. Each walked the worlds in the shape of a great beast, and all nine are now dead, or sleeping, or something between the two. Magic is believed to come from them: the most common teaching is that every spell is a sliver of an Old God's dreaming. Their followers form cults, not churches, and believe they carry the Old Gods' blood.

| **Old God** | **Beast** | **Magic** | **Sleeps** | **Bloodline cult** | **When they stir** |
| **Runar** | Eagle | Nature | As a mountain range whose peaks are his folded wings | The Feathered Kin | Birds fall silent and all face the same way |
| **Linnor** | Dragon | Fire | Coiled beneath the volcanoes of a dozen worlds | The Ember Blood | Fires burn with nothing to feed them |
| **Sollaug** | Hydra | Death | At the very bottom of Below, under the realm of the dead | The Many-Headed | The dead refuse to stay dead |
| **Sverre** | Bear | Earth | Hibernating in the roots of the worlds | The Den-born | Caves breathe in and out |
| **Sigmund** | Wolf | Ice | Curled under a glacier on a world of endless winter | The Pack of the White Howl | Wolves howl at noon |
| **Magnhil** | Snake | Domination | Coiled around something no one has seen | The Coil | Crowds obey a voice none of them can hear |
| **Eirunn** | Unicorn | Enchantment | In a forest no one finds twice | The Hornwardens | Things glow when the innocent touch them |
| **Liv** | Phoenix | Life | As ash, between one burning and the next | The Ashborn | Old ashes are warm to the touch |
| **Ginna** | Rat | Illusion | Everywhere at once, or so she claims | The Gnawing | Something moves at the edge of your sight, every time |

## Why they sleep

The Thrones teach one account of the Long Sleep, and the cults keep two more. Theology at Master rank knows all three. Which is true is the DM's secret.

1. **The Maker's mercy.** The Nine warred with one another, fire against ice and life against death, until the worlds began to crack. The Maker sang them to sleep.
2. **The gift.** The Nine gave themselves to the worlds so that mortals could have magic, and the sleep is what it cost them.
3. **The first crime.** The young gods, newly born and afraid of the old ones, put them down together. The Compact's law against waking them protects the Thrones, not the worlds.

## The Nine

**Runar, the Eagle.** He saw everything grow, and nature magic is what he remembers of it. Forests root on his sleeping body, and rivers start in his feathers. The Feathered Kin take a bird-name at adulthood and swear their eyes are sharper than other mortals'. Hex and Prellandus, the two Lords of Animals, rule what Runar left behind and are nervous of his waking.

**Linnormr, the Dragon.** She was the first flame. Dragons call her mother, and the Dragon Lore skill begins with her. Cranos's volcanoes are said to be her breathing. The Ember Blood mark their children with a burn and count lineage in scars. Her hatred of Sigmund is the oldest feud in existence.

**Sollaug, the Hydra.** Cut off a head and two grow back; death makes more death. Some cults swear he is not asleep at all but dead, and that death magic is his corpse. Liches draw on him, which is one more reason N'zoth hates liches. The Many-Headed are necromancers who believe every death feeds their father.

**Sverre, the Bear.** He sleeps the longest sleep of all, deep in the roots under every world, and earthquakes are him turning over. Ralotas, the Patient Custodian of Earthquakes, sits beside him and keeps him from waking. The Den-born are bear clans, skinwalkers and mountain folk who winter underground in his honour.

**Sigmund, the Wolf.** Ice magic is his breath on the windows of the worlds. Rine and Mitia, gods of ice and cold, gather around his glacier like pups around a sleeping sire. The Pack of the White Howl run in hunting packs and settle every quarrel by challenge. If Linnormr wakes, he wakes too.

**Magnhild, the Snake.** Domination magic is his coil around the mind. Nobody knows what he sleeps wrapped around, and the Coil, who are rulers, spymasters and old noble houses, will kill to keep it secret. Criria, Goddess of Order, considers him the most dangerous of the Nine, because his rule needs no consent.

**Eirunn, the Unicorn.** Enchantment magic is her grace, given to things. Her forest moves, and no mortal finds it twice. The Hornwardens are enchanters and healers who keep vows of purity, and the Enchanting skill's highest rites call on her name. She is the only Old God the Thrones speak of fondly.

**Liv, the Phoenix.** Life magic is her warmth. She is the only one of the Nine said to have woken more than once: she burns, falls to ash and rises again. She is ash now. The Ashborn are healers and midwives who tend sacred fires that must never go out. Tesitas, God of Reincarnation, learned his work by watching her.

**Ginna, the Rat.** Illusion magic is her trick of seeming. The smallest of the Nine, she survived things that killed larger gods, and she claims to sleep everywhere at once, which may itself be an illusion. The Gnawing live in the undersides of cities. Marteya, Goddess of Thieves, leaves crumbs out for her.

# Alongside the Nine

The Nine weren't alone in the Age of Beasts. On land walked the **Seven Colossi**, giants who made places the way the Nine made magic. In the deep seas and high skies lived the **Leviathans**, who didn't make magic at all. They ate it. The age ended in the **War of the Deep**, when the Nine and the Colossi fought the Leviathans together. The Colossi died holding the line, the Leviathans were driven into the deepest trenches and the highest storms, and the Nine fell into the Long Sleep. Renos and Laesos were later born into the empty sea and sky the Leviathans left behind.

## The Seven Colossi

The Colossi were giants of stone and bronze, as tall as mountains. They raised the first peaks, cut the first valleys and laid the first roads between the worlds. The Lodge of the First Stone teaches that mortals were shaped in their image, which is why mortals look more like Colossi than like any of the Nine. The Colossi didn't sleep. They died in the War of the Deep, and their bodies became the land. Their heirs are the **Lodge of the First Stone**, a secret fellowship of builders' guilds, giants and giant-blooded peoples who claim descent from them.

| **Colossus** | **Craft** | **What they made** | **Where they lie now** |
| **Haldur, the Mason** | Stone | The first mountains | A mountain range whose peaks are his knuckles. Aecale's Hearth Peak is hollowed from his skull. |
| **Brynja, the Bronze-Wright** | Metal | The first metal and the first tools | Her anvil is a plateau, and her forge still burns beneath it. Paestruseus works there. |
| **Tor-Ammar, the Road-Layer** | Roads | The first roads, including the roads between worlds | His spine is the longest road in existence, and Rynuros walks it. |
| **Esk, the Glass-Eye** | Glass and sight | The first lenses and windows, and some say the first eyes | His eyes are two lakes that never freeze, and they show the sky of other worlds. |
| **Gudmar, the Bell** | Sound and calling | The first bells, and the first words | His chest is a cavern that rings in storms. Quapetheia's Belltower is hung with his bells. |
| **Solvei, the Weaver** | Cloth and borders | The first cloth, and the first boundaries, woven across the land | Her loom stands as two great pillars. Maion learned borders from her threads. |

**Their legacy.** Colossus-stone can't be broken by mortal tools, which is why the oldest castles are built on their bones. Their hearts were bronze, and miners still find them deep underground, sometimes still beating. Dasos claims them all.

## The Leviathans

The Leviathans were the great beasts of sea and air: vast, ancient and always hungry. They hunted the Nine and drank the magic that poured off them. Some scholars say this is why water and air have no Old God: the Leviathans ate them. They were driven off in the War of the Deep, but not all of them died, and the bones of those that did still drink magic. Their worshippers are **the Maw**, drowned cults and storm cults who believe magic should be eaten back out of the worlds.

| **Leviathan** | **Realm** | **Nature** | **What became of it** |
| **Ulgrath, the World-Eel** | The open seas | It circled whole oceans, swallowing islands | Killed by Haldur. Its skeleton is a reef that rings a world. |
| **Vasqueth, the Kraken Mother** | The deep | A thousand arms, each ending in a mouth | Sank into the deepest trench. Her children still rise. |
| **Nykra, the Tide-Maw** | The sea floor | A jaw as wide as a bay, whose breathing made the first tides | Buried under the sea floor. Prames's tides run in the hollow it left, or else they are still its breath. |
| **Skarnadh, the Thunderbird** | The high storms | Its wings made storms, and it ate lightning | Burned out of the sky by Linnormr. Dinine was born from its last storm. |
| **Hvalgrim, the Sky-Whale** | The seas of cloud | It swam through cloud, swallowing weather and flocks whole | Fled into the Void. Ezrakhul claims to have seen it there, still swimming. |
| **Ommoth, the Drowning Dark** | Beneath the deepest sea | Never seen. Only its pull has ever been felt. | Still alive |

**Their legacy.** **Leviathan bone** drinks magic. A blade of it cuts through wards, and a cage of it holds a mage powerless. It is the rarest material in the worlds, and the Maw kills to keep it from the mages' guilds.

# The Erased

One god has been cut out of existence so completely that even the other gods can't remember his name. His work survives him: every mortal who casts a spell uses it.

## The Namewright

**Rank:** Erased · **Domain (once):** names, and the order of magic · **Holy symbol:** unknown. The crest of House Aldemar has a broken shape at its heart that may once have been his.

**What he did.** When the Nine fell asleep, their dreaming leaked into the worlds as raw magic: one undivided flood that burned out anyone who drew on it. The Namewright named it. He split the flood into **paths**, the schools of magic, and each path into **tiers**, one to nine, so that a mortal could learn magic one step at a time and survive. His master record of every path, tier and working was called **the Index**.

**The Erasure.** Then he was erased. His name was cut from every world, his temples were unbuilt, and his worshippers forgot him in a single night. "The Namewright" isn't his name. It's only a description, and the only one the Compact allows. Nobody alive, mortal or god, knows what he was called, and the Compact forbids anyone to try.

**What survives.**

  - **The paths and tiers of magic**, unchanged since he made them. Priests of Asyne teach that mortals rarely reach the highest tiers because those are the doors he built heaviest.
  - **Fragments of the Index.** Torn pages, carvings and, very rarely, a person who saw the whole of it in a vision and now carries a piece of it inside them.
  - **Truenames.** The Truenames skill is the last surviving craft of his school.
  - **Aldemaris**, his daughter.

**Followers.** He has none openly. In secret there are the **Nameless Seekers**, heretical scholars who try to piece his name together from the gaps in old records. The Compact treats their work as a crime against every Throne.

## Aldemaris, Daughter of the Namewright

**Rank:** Demigod · **Domain:** inheritance and the named paths · **Holy symbol:** the Aldemar crest

Aldemaris was the Namewright's daughter by a mortal, and she survived the Erasure only because she was half mortal. The Ledger held her where her father's name was cut away. On Pemmaroth she founded **House Aldemar**, and her descendants still have a command of the named paths that other mages envy. The house keeps a private rite to "the Father" that no member will explain to outsiders.

N'zoth's Ledger records Aldemaris's birth, but the line for her death is blank. N'zoth treats that blank line as the one flaw in his perfect Ledger, and the Aldemar family's oldest secret is whether it means she is still alive.

**Favour.** Members of House Aldemar who honour her get +2 on rolls to identify, counter or unravel magic of a named path. **Taboo:** speaking her father's titles outside the family.

# The Twelve Thrones

The Twelve Thrones are the greater gods. Each one sits in the High Court and rules a court of lesser gods. Between them they hold almost every prayer spoken on every world, and they wrote and keep the Compact.

## N'zoth, Keeper of the Ledger

**Rank:** Throne · **Court:** the Ledger · **Domains:** the record of the dead, fate, endings · **Aspect:** not death itself, but the true account of it · **Holy symbol:** an open book with a quill laid across it · **Realm:** the Ledger Hall

**Overview.** N'zoth is the Maker's first child, and one of the truly neutral forces among gods and demons. He keeps the Ledger of the dead, and the gods of death write their books and harvest souls from it. Demons scheme and hatch plots from it too. N'zoth cares little for good and evil, only that his Ledger is perfect in every respect. For every soul on every world it records the date of birth, the date of death and the cause, or, for the living, their most probable death.

**Appearance.** A tall grey figure in a reading hood, hands stained to the wrist with ink, eyes like pale lamplight. He is never seen without the quill.

**Personality.** Exact, patient, courteous and completely without pity. He doesn't hate anyone. A death is a fact, and he loves facts.

**Myths.**

  - *The First Entry.* When the first mortal died, N'zoth wrote it down, and so death became something that could be known. Every Ledger Night, priests read that first line aloud. Nobody agrees what it says.
  - *The Lie of the First Lich.* The first lich tried to rewrite its own death date. The page tore. N'zoth has hated liches ever since: what is a lich, if not a particularly stubborn loss?

**Worship.** N'zoth has few mortal followers. Those he has, the **keepers**, help lost souls on their way, so that his Ledger stays as perfect as possible. A soul destroyed, or one that lingers too long, is a flaw in the Ledger. Keepers will help any lost soul pass, and if it resists, they force it down the road at any cost. **Holy day:** Ledger Night, the last night of the year, when families read out the names of their dead.

**Followers.** Keepers, seers, archivists, coroners, and the **Lichwardens**, a sect of keepers who spend their lives cleansing liches from the mortal worlds. Seers ask N'zoth about their own deaths and other people's. He answers happily, because to him it is just a fact of the universe.

**Signs.** A quill scratching in an empty room, a page turning by itself, or a date you suddenly know and can't forget.

**Relationships.** Iemaia works from his pages. Pluras waits on whatever the Ledger loses. Termiya, who makes things forgotten, is the one god whose work he cannot record, and he despises her. Galgedio's reforged souls are his oldest complaint to the High Court. One line he can't close haunts him: Aldemaris's blank death date.

**The Oath of the Ledger.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Truth:* the record is sacred. Write nothing false and hide nothing true.
  - *Passage:* no soul lingers. Help it on, gently if you can.
  - *Indifference:* good and evil are not your concern. The account is.
  - *Correction:* the undying are an error. Correct them.

*Rules:*

  - A keeper may never alter, forge or destroy a record of a life or death.
  - When a lich is known, a keeper must pursue it or report it to those who will.
  - A keeper may not raise the dead or bargain a soul back from the road.

**Favour.** Ask the DM how a creature you can see will most probably die. The DM answers truthfully. **Taboo:** helping a soul escape passage, or becoming undead.

### Faith and lore of N'zoth

**Other names.** The Recorder, Old Quill, the Grey Clerk, and on some worlds Nothos.

**Titles.** Keeper of the Ledger, First of the Maker's Children, the Exact, Lord of the Final Line.

**Sacred things.** Colours: grey and ink-black. Animal: the heron, patient and still. Plant: the yew. Number: one, for one life and one death. Materials: iron-gall ink and slate.

**The realm.** The Ledger Hall is a library with shelves running in every direction, up and down as well as sideways. Each book is a world and each page a life, and the ink dries the moment a death is written. Every soul passes through its reading room on the way Below, and there, once, its page is read aloud.

**Herald and servants.** His herald is Caliphar, Archon of the Scribes, lent to him by the Choirs. His servants are **the Inkbound**, grey faceless clerks who copy lines between the worlds' records. His son Mortain founded the Lichwardens, and their saint, Haskel, still hunts beside them.

**Avatar.** A tall grey clerk in a reading hood, ink to the wrists. He appears at deathbeds that matter and wherever a record is in dispute, and he never speaks first.

**Clergy.** The ranks are Copyist, Keeper, Recorder and High Archivist. Keepers wear grey robes with ink-black cuffs and carry a quill on a cord around the neck. To be ordained, a Copyist writes out the death records of their own village, one hundred lines, without a single error.

**Holy days.**

  - *Ledger Night*, the last night of the year, when families read the names of their dead.
  - *The Reading of Names*, at midsummer, when temples read out every death of the past year.
  - *The Day of Corrections*, when the faithful confess the lies they have told about the dead.

**Rites of life.** At a birth, a keeper writes the child's name in the parish ledger. At a death, the keeper reads the final line aloud and ends with "Written and closed."

**Prayer.** *"Write me truly, Keeper. When my line is closed, let it be closed well."*

**Sects.**

  - *The Lichwardens*, the hunters of liches.
  - *The Quiet Readers*, seers who read probable deaths for a fee. The church tolerates them, barely.
  - *The Correctors*, heretics who believe the Ledger can be edited and want to rewrite the deaths of the people they love.

**Relics.**

  - *The First Quill*, with which the first death was written.
  - *The Torn Page*, ripped when the first lich tried to change its date.
  - *The Slate of Aldemaris*, a copy of her page with the death line still blank.

**Another myth.** *The Census of the Worlds.* Once an age, N'zoth counts every living soul on every world. While the Census runs, nobody can die until they have been counted, and the last Census lasted a terrible month.

## Quapetheia, Moody Queen of Time

**Rank:** Throne · **Court:** the Hours · **Domains:** time as it is felt, moments, waiting · **Aspect:** why one hour flies and another drags. Nixegan is time's passing; Quapetheia is its mood. · **Holy symbol:** an hourglass with sand running both ways · **Realm:** the Belltower, where every hour that has ever been is kept as a ringing bell

**Overview.** Quapetheia rules the weight of time. When she is happy, hours vanish. When she is grieving, a single afternoon can last a year. She is the most worshipped Throne after Samis, because every mortal has waited for something.

**Appearance.** Her age follows her mood: a laughing girl when she is delighted, a stern queen when she is bored, an ancient crone when she grieves. Her hair falls like hourglass sand.

**Personality.** Passionate and mercurial, generous one moment and cruel the next. She loves lovers, despises the idle and pities the dying.

**Myths.**

  - *The Long Afternoon.* Jilted by Nixegan, she sulked for a century, and on one world a single afternoon lasted a hundred years. The people who lived through it still don't age.
  - *The Binding of Prames.* Prames, the god of tides, stole hours from the sea to give to drowning sailors. Quapetheia chained him to the tides so that the hours would stay honest. Renos has never forgiven her.

**Worship.** Clock towers, sundials and temple bells. Her clergy are the **Bellwardens**, who ring the hours by hand. **Holy days:** the two solstices, her longest and shortest moods.

**Followers.** Timekeepers, lovers who pray for a night to last, prisoners who pray for years to pass, the dying who pray for more, and musicians, who keep time for a living.

**Signs.** A candle that burns too fast or too slow, a moment that stretches, or every clock in a room stopping at once.

**Relationships.** Her court is the Hours. Prenessa, Herald of Eternity, is her elder sister, and Nixegan, the Silent Bringer of Time, is her estranged husband. Prerun, the Gentle Prince of Alternatives, is their son. Thinsetas heralds her mornings, Hensax reads her futures and Nuxitia brings on her nights. She is at odds with Renos over Prames.

**The Oath of the Bell.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Mark the hour:* a promise made for a time is kept at that time.
  - *Savour:* no moment is wasted, yours or anyone else's.
  - *Mercy of minutes:* give the dying their last hours.
  - *Feel it:* to live without feeling time pass is to be already dead.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin must never break an appointment sworn under a bell.
  - A paladin may not take, steal or trade another's time by magic.
  - When someone is dying in your presence, you must give them the chance to speak.

**Favour.** You act first in one round, whatever the initiative order. **Taboo:** wasting someone's last hours, or breaking a promise made for a set time.

### Faith and lore of Quapetheia

**Other names.** The Hourglass Queen, Lady Moment, Tempora, the Mood of the World.

**Titles.** Moody Queen of Time, Mistress of the Belltower, She Who Hurries and Lingers.

**Sacred things.** Colours: amber and silver. Animals: the mayfly and the tortoise, the quickest and the slowest. Plant: the poppy. Number: twelve, for the hours. Materials: bronze for bells, and sand.

**The realm.** The Belltower has no top and no bottom. Every hour that has ever passed hangs inside it as a bell, and it rings whenever anyone remembers that hour. On the Silent Floor hang the hours nobody remembers, and Quapetheia goes there when she grieves.

**Herald and servants.** Her herald is her daughter Mayra, the Bell-Maid. Her servants are **the Chimes**, small bell-spirits who carry the hours to each world. Tickrel, God of Clocks, keeps the tower's mechanisms.

**Avatar.** A woman whose age changes with her mood, heard before she is seen as a sound of distant bells.

**Clergy.** The ranks are Bell-Novice, Bellwarden, Chime-Mother or Chime-Father, and the Hour-Keeper. Bellwardens change the colour of their robes through the day: dawn-pink, noon-gold, dusk-violet, midnight-blue. To be ordained, a novice rings every hour by hand for a full day and night without missing one.

**Holy days.**

  - *The solstices*, her longest and shortest moods.
  - *The Stolen Hour*, when every clock is set back an hour and that hour is given to the dying.
  - *Lovers' Eve*, the one night of the year that is said to last as long as the lovers want.

**Rites of life.** A newborn's first hour is rung on a temple bell. Weddings are timed to the minute. When someone dies, every clock in the house is stopped.

**Prayer.** *"Queen of the Hours, slow this one down. Let me keep it a little longer."*

**Sects.**

  - *The Hasteners*, who pray for time to rush through suffering.
  - *The Lingerers*, a monastic order that practises slowness.
  - *The Abolitionists*, heretics who want Prames freed from his chain and who work secretly with Renos's priests.

**Relics.**

  - *The Two-Way Hourglass*, her symbol made real.
  - *The Bell of the First Hour*, which rang when time began.
  - *Nixegan's Ring*, which he left behind when they parted.

**Another myth.** *The Hour She Gave Away.* A dying child asked Quapetheia for one more hour, and she gave it. She never took it back, and the child is still living inside that hour, happy, somewhere in the Belltower.

## Cemes, Infallible Custodian of Wisdom

**Rank:** Throne · **Court:** the Lamps · **Domains:** wisdom, judgement, counsel · **Aspect:** knowing what to do with what you know · **Holy symbol:** an oil lamp held in an open palm · **Realm:** the Lamplit Stacks, a library where every lamp is a thought someone has had

**Overview.** Cemes has never been wrong. Theologians argue over whether that is because he is always right, or because the worlds bend to fit what he says. He is the patron of judges, advisers and anyone who has to make a hard choice well.

**Appearance.** An ageless scholar whose eyes are two small lamp flames. His robes are covered in his own handwriting.

**Personality.** Calm, kind and impossible to argue with. His great flaw comes from his title: someone who can't be wrong can't change his mind.

**Myths.**

  - *The Question Cemes Did Not Know.* A child asked him something he didn't know. He answered anyway, and was right. Half his priests say that proves he knows everything. The other half say the world changed to match him, and that frightens them.
  - *The Lamp That Burned Too Bright.* His favourite student, Ruarus, tried to see everything at once and went mad. Cemes didn't stop him, because he had said Ruarus would be fine.

**Worship.** Libraries, universities and lamplit vigils. His clergy are the **Lamplighters**, who give counsel to anyone who asks. **Holy day:** the Long Lamp, the longest night of the year, when every lamp is lit and people read until dawn.

**Followers.** Scholars, judges, sages, royal advisers, elders and teachers.

**Signs.** An idea that arrives whole, a lamp flaring when the right answer is spoken, or an owl at the window.

**Relationships.** Lathys, the Humble Queen of Enlightenment, is his wife and his conscience. Mititia, Goddess of Freedom of Thought, is his rebellious daughter. Ruarus is his shame. Bisus, the Forgotten Prince of Peace, was his oldest friend, and Cemes is the only god who still visits him. He allies with Criria, and he opposes Nyios, who hides what should be known.

**The Oath of the Lamp.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Seek:* truth first, comfort second.
  - *Weigh:* judge before you act, and act on your judgement.
  - *Share:* wisdom hoarded is wisdom wasted.
  - *Admit:* you are not Cemes. When you are wrong, say so.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin may refuse to say what they know, but may never lie about it.
  - A paladin must give honest counsel to anyone who sincerely asks.
  - A paladin may not destroy knowledge: no burned books, no silenced witnesses.

**Favour.** Ask the DM one yes-or-no question about the situation in front of you. The answer is true. **Taboo:** destroying knowledge.

### Faith and lore of Cemes

**Other names.** The Lamp, Old Wise, Sapiens, the One Who Knows.

**Titles.** Infallible Custodian of Wisdom, the Unerring, Keeper of the Lamplit Stacks.

**Sacred things.** Colours: lamp-gold and deep blue. Animal: the owl. Plants: sage and the olive tree. Number: seven, for the seven questions every judge must ask. Materials: brass and lapis.

**The realm.** The Lamplit Stacks are a library-garden where every thought anyone has ever had burns as a lamp. Forgotten thoughts burn dim. The souls of the wise may choose to stay there forever, tending a lamp of their own.

**Herald and servants.** His herald is his son, Oriel Lamplight. His servants are **the Lamplit**, owl-headed messengers who bring counsel to those who ask for it sincerely. Scrivia, Goddess of Scholars, keeps the reading rooms.

**Avatar.** An old scholar at a reading table, who seems to have been in the room all along. He appears to judges before hard verdicts.

**Clergy.** The ranks are Reader, Lamplighter, Counsellor and Lamp-Elder. Lamplighters wear deep blue robes and carry a small brass lamp on a chain. To be ordained, a Reader gives counsel to strangers for a year and records every piece of advice and how it turned out.

**Holy days.**

  - *The Long Lamp*, the longest night, when every lamp is lit.
  - *Question Day*, when children may ask a priest anything at all and must be answered honestly.
  - *The Day of Admitting*, Lathys's festival, when scholars confess their errors in public.

**Rites of life.** A child's first question is written down and kept. At coming of age, the youth asks one question of a Lamplighter. At death, a lamp is lit in their name in the temple.

**Prayer.** *"Light my lamp, Custodian. Show me not what I want, but what is so."*

**Sects.**

  - *The Infallibilists*, who hold that Cemes cannot err and that the worlds bend to his word. They are the most dangerous dogmatists in any faith.
  - *The Doubters*, who follow Lorrow and Mititia and say that Cemes himself would want to be questioned.
  - *The Lamp-Dousers*, a heretical madness cult of Ruarus.

**Relics.**

  - *The Lamp of First Thought*, the first lamp ever lit in the Stacks.
  - *The Ledger of Advice*, the record kept by the first Lamplighter.
  - *Ruarus's Cracked Lamp*, kept behind a curtain.

**Another myth.** *The Twelve Questions.* At the first sitting of the High Court, Cemes asked each Throne one question. Each answer became a law of that god's nature, and nobody, including the Thrones, knows what Cemes asked himself.

## Criria, Goddess of Order

**Rank:** Throne · **Court:** the Crown · **Domains:** law, rule, structure, contracts · **Aspect:** order as the right shape of things. Thevia keeps balance and Cene keeps decency; Criria keeps the rules. · **Holy symbol:** a square crown · **Realm:** the Perfect City, where every street runs true

**Overview.** Criria believes chaos is the only true evil, and that every cruelty starts as disorder. She wrote the Compact of Thrones, and the other gods remember it. On every world, kings are crowned in her name and contracts are sworn on her crown.

**Appearance.** A tall, perfectly symmetrical woman in armour of interlocking plates that fit without a gap, wearing a crown of squared grey stone.

**Personality.** Precise, fair by the letter, cold, and allergic to surprises. She is not cruel, but she will let a just thing fail sooner than let a rule break.

**Myths.**

  - *The Writing of the Compact.* After the Erasure, Criria wrote the six laws in a single night and made every Throne sign them. Some say there was a seventh law, and only she remembers it.
  - *The Steward Who Moved.* Maion, the Changing Lord of Boundaries, was once her steward, but he kept moving the borders. She cast him out, then found the worlds could not do without him.

**Worship.** Courts of law, city halls and coronations. Her clergy are the **Arbiters**, judges who serve as notaries and witnesses. **Holy day:** Founding Day, held on the anniversary of each city's charter.

**Followers.** Kings, judges, bureaucrats, city watches, notaries and soldiers of the line.

**Signs.** Things falling neatly into place, scales coming to rest level, or a crowd that suddenly forms a queue.

**Relationships.** Her court is the Crown: Cene, Pransoros, Celtesyne, Maion, Biria, Chratos and Postia. Biria, Goddess of Empires, is her most ambitious and least obedient servant. Cemes is her closest ally. She opposes Mititia, Ginna and Hircisos, and she fears Magnhild, whose rule needs no consent.

**The Oath of the Crown.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Law:* keep the law, even when it costs you.
  - *Place:* everything has its place. Protect it.
  - *Word:* a contract is sacred.
  - *Order:* chaos is the enemy of the weak.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin must obey lawful authority unless it breaks the Compact.
  - A paladin may never break a contract or a sworn word.
  - Lawbreakers are brought to judgement, not killed, wherever that is possible.

**Favour.** Take +4 on one roll to enforce, argue or uphold a law or contract. **Taboo:** breaking a sworn contract.

### Faith and lore of Criria

**Other names.** The Square Queen, Lady Measure, Lex, the Arbiter-Mother.

**Titles.** Goddess of Order, Author of the Compact, the Unbent.

**Sacred things.** Colours: grey and white. Animals: the ant and the bee, whose colonies keep perfect order. Plant: boxwood, clipped into hedges. Number: four, the sides of a square. Materials: iron and grey stone.

**The realm.** The Perfect City is laid out on a grid, and every street runs true. The souls of the lawful dead live there in households ranked by their deeds. Nothing in it is ever late, broken or out of place, and some of its citizens find that restful.

**Herald and servants.** Her herald is her son Justar, Hand of the Crown. Her servants are **the Measured**, living statues of judges who walk the Perfect City and serve as witnesses in mortal courts when asked.

**Avatar.** A tall woman in seamless plate, who arrives exactly when a great contract is signed or broken.

**Clergy.** The ranks are Notary, Arbiter, Magistrate-Priest and High Arbiter. Arbiters wear white robes with a grey stole marked in squares, and a small square crown of stone on a chain. To be ordained, a Notary memorises the Compact and the laws of one city, then judges a case while three Arbiters watch.

**Holy days.**

  - *Founding Day*, each city's own charter day.
  - *Contract Day*, when debts are renewed or closed and new apprenticeships are sworn.
  - *Compact Day*, the anniversary of the Compact's signing. Every faith marks it, and Criria's temples mark it hardest.

**Rites of life.** Births are registered before an Arbiter. Marriages are contracts sworn on the square crown. At a death, the will is read aloud before an Arbiter, and the estate is settled to the coin.

**Prayer.** *"Queen of Order, set me in my place, and keep me true to it."*

**Sects.**

  - *The Letterists*, who hold that the law matters more than justice.
  - *The Equitists*, who follow Cene and teach that law exists to serve decency.
  - *The Seventh Law*, a secret order that believes the Compact has a hidden seventh law, and that it is the most important of all.

**Relics.**

  - *The Original Compact*, written in Criria's own hand and kept in the Perfect City.
  - *The Square Crown*, her first crown, worn by her High Arbiter on Compact Day.
  - *The Rod of Measure*, against which every honest measure on every world was once checked.

**Another myth.** *The Placing of the Stars.* When the Maker scattered the stars, Criria gave each one a place and a name, and that is why they move in order. Only the Seven Wandering Stars refused her, and she has never forgiven them.

## Chornos, King of Might

**Rank:** Throne · **Court:** Arms · **Domains:** might, power, the warrior's virtues · **Aspect:** might as the right to rule. Bipeter is raw strength; Chornos is strength that wears a crown. · **Holy symbol:** a crown made of broken weapons · **Realm:** the Ring, an arena as wide as a world

**Overview.** Chornos won his throne by defeating every god who challenged him, and he has never lost a fair fight since. He holds that strength is a duty as much as a right: the strong rule because they can protect. Every warrior hall on every world keeps a seat for him at the head of the table.

**Appearance.** A giant king in battered bronze, wearing a crown made from the weapons of everyone he has beaten. He has a scar for every one of them.

**Personality.** Proud, loud and generous to anyone who stands up to him. He despises cowardice and loves an underdog who refuses to kneel.

**Myths.**

  - *The Hundred Duels.* When the Thrones were first seated, a hundred gods wanted the Throne of Might. Chornos fought every one of them on the same day, and each broken weapon became a point of his crown.
  - *The Chains of Thaxucus.* His son Thaxucus loved war so much that he chained himself to stop. Chornos has tried to break the chains every year since and never has, and he cannot decide whether he is proud or ashamed.

**Worship.** Arenas, warrior halls and oath-stones. His clergy are the **Shieldfathers**, veteran warriors who train the young. **Holy day:** the Day of Challenge, when anyone may challenge anyone, and no rank protects you.

**Followers.** Warriors, champions, conquering kings, wrestlers, gladiators, and the people they protect.

**Signs.** A weapon that suddenly feels light in your hand, a drumbeat under the earth, or an enemy who hesitates.

**Relationships.** His court is Arms, which includes Pestus, Bipeter, Thaxucus, Luceion and Noltollo. Bipeter, the Erratic King of Strength, is his brother and rival king. Thevia is his old lover and older rival: he believes might makes peace, and she believes peace has a price. He respects Aecale, the only god he has never challenged.

**The Oath of Might.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Strength:* be stronger tomorrow than today.
  - *Challenge:* accept every fair challenge.
  - *Shield:* strength exists to protect those who stand behind it.
  - *Finish:* what you start, you end.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin must never refuse a duel from an equal or a better.
  - A paladin may never strike a foe who has knelt and yielded.
  - A paladin who flees a fair fight must atone by facing that foe again.

**Favour.** Add your Renown to the damage of one hit, and knock the target prone. **Taboo:** fleeing a fair fight.

### Faith and lore of Chornos

**Other names.** The Bronze King, Old Might, Thewgar, the Crowned Fist.

**Titles.** King of Might, the Undefeated, Lord of the Ring.

**Sacred things.** Colours: bronze and red. Animal: the bull. Plant: the oak. Number: one hundred, for the Hundred Duels. Material: bronze.

**The realm.** The Ring is an arena as wide as a world. The souls of great warriors fight there every day and feast together every night, and every wound heals at dusk. Anyone may challenge anyone, and Chornos watches from a throne made of the ground itself.

**Herald and servants.** His herald is his son Hask Ironarm, the God-Wrestler. His servants are **the Unbeaten**, champions who earned a place in the Ring and are sent to the mortal worlds to judge great duels.

**Avatar.** A giant warrior in battered bronze who steps into a duelling circle to make sure the fight is fair.

**Clergy.** The ranks are Shield-Squire, Shieldfather or Shieldmother, Champion and Crown-Bearer. Shieldfathers wear bronze armour and red cloaks in every service. To be ordained, a squire must win a fair duel and then spare the beaten foe.

**Holy days.**

  - *The Day of Challenge*, when rank protects no one.
  - *The Hundred Duels*, a festival of games, wrestling and contests of strength.
  - *The Kneeling Night*, when every warrior lays down their weapons and rests, by Chornos's command.

**Rites of life.** A newborn is lifted high over the parent's head. Coming of age is proved by a feat of strength. When a warrior dies, their weapon is broken and added to the shrine's crown of weapons.

**Prayer.** *"King of Might, make my arm strong, and my cause worthy of it."*

**Sects.**

  - *The Crowned*, who hold that might is right. Many conquerors belong to it.
  - *The Shields*, who hold that strength exists to serve the weak.
  - *The Chainbreakers*, who want Thaxucus freed, and are watched very closely.

**Relics.**

  - *The First Weapon*, forged for Chornos by Paestruseus.
  - *A Point of the Crown*, one weapon from his crown of broken weapons, given to a shrine for a great deed.
  - *Hask's Belt*, worn by the God-Wrestler when he beat a god.

**Another myth.** *The Hundred-and-First Duel.* After the Hundred Duels, a mortal child walked into the Ring and challenged Chornos. The King of Might knelt, and declared that he had lost. Ever since, his faithful must accept the challenge of anyone braver than they are strong.

## Thevia, Bloody Goddess of Harmony

**Rank:** Throne · **Court:** the Red Scale · **Domains:** harmony, balance, the price of peace, vengeance · **Aspect:** harmony that is paid for. Bisus was peace given freely; Thevia is peace bought with blood. · **Holy symbol:** a level scale with a drop of blood in each pan · **Realm:** the Still Field, a battlefield after the battle, quiet forever

**Overview.** Thevia believes every peace has a price, and that someone always pays it. Sometimes the price is a sacrifice, sometimes a war, sometimes one well-chosen death. She is the goddess of the treaty sealed in blood, the culling that saves the herd, and the vengeance that settles a debt. Her court holds the gods of vengeance, because a wrong left unanswered is imbalance.

**Appearance.** A serene woman in white, the hem of her gown soaked red. She carries a scale that drips but never tips.

**Personality.** Gentle, patient and loving, and completely willing to spill blood for harmony. She never kills in anger. That is what frightens people.

**Myths.**

  - *The Red Peace.* Two kingdoms warred for a century. Thevia came to the peace talks and killed both kings at the table. Their heirs signed within the hour, and the peace has held for a thousand years.
  - *The Weighing of Bisus.* Before Thevia, there was Bisus, who gave peace for nothing. Mortals took it and went to war anyway. Thevia was born the day mortals learned that peace has to be paid for, and Bisus has been fading ever since.

**Worship.** Battlefield altars and treaty tables. Her clergy are the **Weighers**, who judge disputes and witness peace oaths sealed in blood. **Holy day:** the Red Truce, a day when no blade may be drawn, ending at dusk with a single offering of blood.

**Followers.** Peacemakers, diplomats, executioners, battlefield surgeons, avengers and anyone who has paid a terrible price for something good.

**Signs.** Spilled blood beading into a perfect circle, two enemies setting down their weapons at the same moment, or the smell of iron and lilies.

**Relationships.** Her court is the Red Scale: Litilys, Samassa, Diran, and the gods of vengeance, Varkesh, Khaldrun, Zaromel and Iscara. Litilys, the Queen of Mercy, is her conscience. She distrusts Zaromel, who never stops at enough, and values Varkesh, who never takes too much. Chornos is her old lover and rival. Bisus is the god she replaced.

**The Oath of the Red Scale.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Balance:* every wrong must be answered, and every debt paid.
  - *Price:* peace is bought. Pay first with what is yours.
  - *Restraint:* not one drop more than harmony needs.
  - *Harmony:* the goal is peace, never the blood.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin must offer terms before any battle they choose.
  - A paladin may never kill for pleasure, anger or profit.
  - A blood debt sworn before a Weigher must be settled within the arc.

**Favour.** When a creature damages you, deal the same amount of damage back to it. **Taboo:** cruelty for its own sake.

### Faith and lore of Thevia

**Other names.** The Red Lady, Lady Balance, the Weigher, Mother Price.

**Titles.** Bloody Goddess of Harmony, the Still Scale, Mistress of Treaties.

**Sacred things.** Colours: white and red. Animal: the white heron with a red bill. Plant: the lily. Number: two, the pans of the scale. Material: silver.

**The realm.** The Still Field is a battlefield on the evening after the battle, quiet forever. The souls of those who died in just wars rest there, and white lilies grow wherever blood was spilled.

**Herald and servants.** Her herald is her son Redd, the Treaty Child. Her servants are **the Pale Witnesses**, quiet spirits who appear at treaty tables and remember every word sworn there.

**Avatar.** A serene woman in white at a treaty table, the hem of her gown soaked red. When she appears, both sides sign.

**Clergy.** The ranks are Witness, Weigher, Peacemaker and Scale-Mother. Weighers wear white robes with red hems and carry small silver scales. To be ordained, a Witness must settle a dispute between enemies and pay part of the price of peace themselves.

**Holy days.**

  - *The Red Truce*, the day no blade may be drawn.
  - *Treaty Day*, when old peace oaths are renewed in public.
  - *The Weighing*, at the year's end, when the faithful settle every grudge and debt they owe.

**Rites of life.** Blood oaths are sworn before a Weigher. Marriages between rival families are her particular blessing. When a soldier is buried, a drop of blood from each side of the war is let onto the grave.

**Prayer.** *"Lady of the Scale, let the price be paid, and let it be enough."*

**Sects.**

  - *The Bloodless*, pacifists who will pay any price of peace, but only in their own blood.
  - *The Cullers*, extremists who start small wars to prevent larger ones.
  - *The Tally-Keepers*, accountants of debts who work closely with the followers of Varkesh.

**Relics.**

  - *The Scale of the Red Peace*, her own scale, which drips but never tips.
  - *The Treaty Knife*, which killed both kings at the table.
  - *Bisus's Unstrung Bow*, kept in her greatest temple, either as an honour or as a trophy.

**Another myth.** *The Price of the Compact.* Thevia would not sign the Compact until every Throne had paid a price for the peace it brought. What each Throne paid is secret, and Thevia is the only one who knows all twelve.

## Iemaia, Merciless Herald of the Underworld

**Rank:** Throne · **Court:** Below · **Domains:** death's road, the underworld, the end of mortal life · **Aspect:** the coming of death. N'zoth writes the death and Zeuphone tends the soul; Iemaia comes to collect it. · **Holy symbol:** a lantern burning with cold blue fire · **Realm:** Below, the realm of the dead

**Overview.** When N'zoth writes a death, Iemaia comes. She leads every soul from every world down the road to Below, and she makes no exceptions, accepts no bribes and has never once turned back. She is not cruel. She is simply certain. Her title, *merciless*, is what mortals call her, not what she calls herself.

**Appearance.** A tall hooded woman carrying a lantern of cold blue fire, followed by a pale grey hound. Her face is never fully seen. Those who glimpse it say it looked like someone they had lost.

**Personality.** Blunt, unhurried and unbribable, with a dry kindness that only shows at the very end.

**Myths.**

  - *The King Who Hid.* A king hid from her for three hundred years behind wards and stolen lives. Every night she waited at his door. When he finally opened it, she said only, "Ready?"
  - *The Lantern.* Her lantern gives the only light on the road Below. Souls who refuse to follow it wander and become the restless dead.

**Worship.** Funeral halls, ossuaries and crossroads at night. Her clergy are the **Lantern-bearers**, undertakers and death-priests who walk with the dying. **Holy day:** the Night of Lanterns, when the living set lanterns on the water so their dead can find the road.

**Followers.** Undertakers, gravediggers, hospice-keepers, soldiers, executioners, undead hunters, and anyone dying who has stopped being afraid.

**Signs.** Your breath fogging in a warm room, a blue light far off at night, or a grey dog that won't leave you alone.

**Relationships.** Her court is Below: Zeuphone, Tesitas, Ziios, Termiya, Argari and Phisus. Zeuphone, the Benevolent Goddess of Souls, is her opposite and her closest companion. She works from N'zoth's Ledger and respects him above every other god. Galgedio steals souls from her road, and she hates him for it. Sollaug, the Old God of Death, sleeps beneath her realm, and she does not know whether she is his jailer or his heir.

**The Oath of the Lantern.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Inevitability:* everyone dies. Fear it less.
  - *No bargains:* no life is bought back.
  - *Rest:* the restless dead must be laid down.
  - *Escort:* nobody should walk the road alone.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin must destroy or lay to rest any undead they find.
  - A paladin may never make or honour a bargain to escape death, for themselves or anyone else.
  - A paladin must sit with anyone dying in their care until the end, if it can be done.

**Favour.** A creature you drop to 0 HP can't be revived and can't rise as undead. Or take +4 on one roll against undead. **Taboo:** raising the dead, or cheating death.

### Faith and lore of Iemaia

**Other names.** The Lantern Lady, the Grey Walker, the Collector, Old Mother Last.

**Titles.** Merciless Herald of the Underworld, Bearer of the Lantern, Ruler of Below.

**Sacred things.** Colours: grey and cold blue. Animal: the grey hound. Plant: the asphodel. Number: nine, for the nine turns of the road Below. Materials: pewter and blue glass.

**The realm.** The road Below descends through mist into a vast, quiet country under a sky lit only by lanterns. The souls of the dead rest in its towns and fields according to how they lived. Further down lie the Restless Marches, where souls who refused the lantern wander. At the very bottom is the Deep Gate, beneath which Sollaug sleeps.

**Herald and servants.** Her herald is her son Lanthorn, the Lantern's Son. Her servants are Ashmane, her grey hound; Charrow the Ferryman; and **the Lantern-bearers**, souls who chose to keep walking the road and guide others down it.

**Avatar.** A hooded woman with a lantern of cold blue fire, who visits those who are about to die. It is said that anyone who sees her face is already dying.

**Clergy.** The ranks are Mourner, Lantern-bearer, Road-Warden and High Lantern. Her priests wear grey hooded robes and carry small lanterns of blue glass. To be ordained, a Mourner sits vigil with ten dying strangers, through to the end.

**Holy days.**

  - *The Night of Lanterns*, when the living set lanterns on the water for their dead.
  - *The Long Quiet*, a day of silence for everyone who died in the year.
  - *The Unburial*, when ossuaries are cleaned and the bones of the dead honoured by name.

**Rites of life.** At a funeral, a lantern is lit and carried ahead of the body. A coin is placed in the hand for Charrow. The last words spoken to the dying are always the same: "Ready?"

**Prayer.** *"Lady of the Lantern, when you come, come gently. I will be ready."*

**Sects.**

  - *The Gentle Road*, a hospice order that cares for the dying.
  - *The Hounds of Iemaia*, hunters of the undead.
  - *The Doorkeepers*, a secret order that guards the few doors between the living worlds and Below.

**Relics.**

  - *The Spare Lantern*, said to be the twin of her own.
  - *Ashmane's Collar*, which lets its wearer find any lost soul.
  - *The King's Door*, the door the King Who Hid finally opened.

**Another myth.** *The Lantern Lent.* A mother came Below to find her lost child, and Iemaia, who makes no exceptions, made one: she lent the mother her lantern for a single night. The mother found her child and brought the lantern back, and Iemaia has never told anyone what she did in the dark while it was gone.

## Laesos, God of the Sky

**Rank:** Throne · **Court:** the Sky · **Domains:** the day sky, light, weather, the open air · **Aspect:** the sky itself, vast and watching. Storms, winds and rains belong to his court. · **Holy symbol:** a circle of open blue sky with a single eye at its centre · **Realm:** Ever-Noon, a sky with no ground beneath it and a sun that never sets

**Overview.** Laesos is the sky over every world. Everything done under the day sky, he sees. He is the patron of travellers, shepherds and anyone who looks up for an answer. He shares the heavens with his brother Hircisos: Laesos holds the day, Hircisos the night, and the two have not spoken since the sky was divided.

**Appearance.** A giant whose body is open blue sky, with clouds drifting through his chest and the sun for an eye.

**Personality.** Open-hearted, honest and distant. He sees everything and intervenes rarely, because everything is a great deal to look after.

**Myths.**

  - *The Dividing of the Sky.* Laesos and Hircisos ruled the sky together until they quarrelled over a single star. They split the sky into day and night. Hircisos then demanded his own seat on the High Court and was refused, and the night has resented the day ever since.
  - *The Kite of the Shepherd Girl.* A girl flew a kite so high it reached Laesos. He tied a message to the string, and it was the first prayer any god answered.

**Worship.** Hilltop temples built without roofs. His clergy are the **Skywatchers**, who read the weather and the omens. **Holy day:** Highsun, the noon of midsummer.

**Followers.** Shepherds, travellers, weather-readers, kite-makers, archers, aeronauts and anyone who works under open sky.

**Signs.** Clouds parting over you, a single bird circling overhead, or a shadow that falls the wrong way.

**Relationships.** His court is the Sky: Aernine, Sevia, Trixaran, Dinine, Ramussa, Iesos, Methebe, Theria, Rine and Mitia. Sevia, the Old Goddess of Wind, is his mother. Aernine, the Vengeful Goddess of the Sky, is his sister and his sword. Hircisos is his estranged brother. He argues with Renos at the horizon over where the sea ends and the sky begins.

**The Oath of the Open Sky.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Openness:* do nothing you would hide from the sky.
  - *Witness:* see clearly, and say what you saw.
  - *Freedom:* nothing that flies should be caged.
  - *Shelter:* nobody is left out in the storm.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin may not imprison anyone who has done no wrong.
  - A paladin must give true testimony whenever they are asked.
  - A paladin must shelter anyone caught in a storm who asks for it.

**Favour.** Under open sky, you have advantage on PER rolls to spot something for one scene, and you know tomorrow's weather. **Taboo:** doing in secret what you would not do in daylight.

### Faith and lore of Laesos

**Other names.** The Open Sky, Father Blue, the Watcher Above, Highsun.

**Titles.** God of the Sky, the All-Seeing Day, Lord of Ever-Noon.

**Sacred things.** Colours: sky-blue and white. Animals: the skylark and the hawk. Plant: the cornflower. Number: one, for the single sun. Material: polished silver, which catches the sky.

**The realm.** Ever-Noon is an endless sky with no ground beneath it. Islands of cloud drift through it, and the sun never sets. The souls of travellers and shepherds sail its winds forever, and nothing there can hide.

**Herald and servants.** His herald is his son Altair, the Sky-Born Archer. His servants are **the Skylarks**, winged messengers who carry his sight to the mortal worlds. Aernine, his sister, is his sword.

**Avatar.** A shadow passing over the land with no cloud to cast it. On rare days, a giant whose body is open sky, walking on the horizon.

**Clergy.** The ranks are Watcher, Skywatcher, Wind-Reader and High Watcher. Skywatchers wear pale blue robes and never cover their heads. To be ordained, a Watcher spends a full day and night on a hilltop with no shelter of any kind.

**Holy days.**

  - *Highsun*, the noon of midsummer.
  - *Kite Day*, when prayers are written on kites and flown as high as they will go.
  - *The Open Door*, when every household leaves its door open from dawn to dusk, with nothing to hide.

**Rites of life.** A newborn is shown to the sky at noon. Marriages are held under open sky. The ashes of the dead are scattered from a high place.

**Prayer.** *"Watcher Above, see me, and let me be worth seeing."*

**Sects.**

  - *The Windborne*, aeronauts and sky-sailors.
  - *The Unroofed*, ascetics who refuse to live under any roof.
  - *The Reconcilers*, who work to end the feud between Laesos and Hircisos. Some of his priests call them heretics.

**Relics.**

  - *The Shepherd Girl's Kite*, which carried the first answered prayer.
  - *The Noon Mirror*, which shows whatever the sky can see.
  - *Laesos's Tear*, a vial of rain that fell from a cloudless sky.

**Another myth.** *The Star They Quarrelled Over.* The star Laesos and Hircisos split the sky over was Ysmere. Both brothers wanted it, and so Ysmere hid from both of them. That is why the Blind Star cannot be seen, and why astrologers say that whoever finds it will end the feud.

## Renos, Cunning God of the Sea

**Rank:** Throne · **Court:** the Tide · **Domains:** the sea, currents, sailors, bargains made over water · **Aspect:** the sea as a trickster that gives and takes. Pimis is its warmth and Neleros its rage; Renos is its cleverness. · **Holy symbol:** a fish hook tied in a knot no one can untie · **Realm:** the Drowned Halls, a palace of wrecks on the floor of every sea at once

**Overview.** Renos rules the seas of every world and every sea between the worlds. He loves a wager, a riddle and a bargain with a catch in it, and he is scrupulously fair to the letter of any deal and gleefully unfair to its spirit. Sailors pray to him because they must. Smugglers and pirates pray to him because they like him.

**Appearance.** A weathered old sailor with a beard of kelp and eyes that change colour like the water. At sea he is sometimes seen as a vast eel, grinning.

**Personality.** Clever, playful, greedy and oddly honourable. He never breaks his word, and never gives it without a trap in it.

**Myths.**

  - *The Wager of the Shores.* Renos won the coastlines from Aecale with a riddle she couldn't answer. That is why every shore is half land and half sea, and why the two of them still bicker over the beaches.
  - *The Chaining of Prames.* When Quapetheia chained his tide-brother Prames, Renos swore to free him. He has been plotting it for an age, and every high tide is him testing the chain.

**Worship.** Harbour shrines, figureheads and offerings thrown overboard. His clergy are the **Tidecallers**, ship-priests who bless voyages and read the water. **Holy day:** First Tide, the first high tide of the year, when boats are blessed and debts are settled.

**Followers.** Sailors, fishers, pirates, smugglers, harbour merchants, navigators and gamblers.

**Signs.** A wave that breaks the wrong way, a gull that sounds as if it's laughing, or a coin washed up at your feet.

**Relationships.** His court is the Tide: Pimis, Neleros, Pheseus, Thominus, Dirnutis and Prames. Pimis, the Warm Goddess of Oceans, is his wife. Neleros, the Dark God of Floods, is his brother and his danger. He is at odds with Quapetheia over Prames, with Laesos over the horizon, and with Aecale over the shore.

**The Oath of the Tide.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Bargain:* every deal is kept to the letter.
  - *Cunning:* the clever survive the sea.
  - *Respect the deep:* the sea forgives nothing.
  - *Return:* what the sea gives you, you give a share back.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin may never refuse help to someone drowning.
  - A paladin must give the sea its tithe: a tenth of every catch or haul.
  - A paladin may never break the letter of a bargain made over water. The spirit of it is fair game.

**Favour.** On or in water, you have advantage on one roll to navigate, swim, bargain or trick. **Taboo:** breaking a bargain struck over water.

### Faith and lore of Renos

**Other names.** The Old Salt, Grandfather Kelp, the Sea-King, Brine-Beard.

**Titles.** Cunning God of the Sea, Lord of the Drowned Halls, King of Bargains.

**Sacred things.** Colours: sea-green and grey. Animals: the eel and the gull. Plant: kelp. Number: ten, for the sea's tithe. Materials: pearl and salt-rusted iron.

**The realm.** The Drowned Halls are a palace built from wrecks, which lies on the floor of every sea at once. The souls of drowned sailors drink at Renos's table there, and every night they gamble for the right to sail again.

**Herald and servants.** His herald is his son Marrok Tidewalker, the Pirate King. His servants are **the Drowned Crew**, sailors he won in wagers, who man the ghost ships that carry his messages.

**Avatar.** An old sailor in a dockside tavern, who always offers a wager and always pays up.

**Clergy.** The ranks are Deckhand, Tidecaller, Captain-Priest and Sea-Speaker. Tidecallers wear oilskins worked with knotwork patterns. To be ordained, a Deckhand survives a night alone at sea in a small boat and strikes a bargain with the water.

**Holy days.**

  - *First Tide*, the first high tide of the year, when boats are blessed and debts settled.
  - *The Tithing*, when a tenth of the year's catch or cargo goes back into the sea.
  - *Wager Night*, a festival of gambling, riddles and impossible bets.

**Rites of life.** A newborn is dipped in seawater. Sailors marry on deck. The dead are buried at sea with a coin, to buy a seat at Renos's table.

**Prayer.** *"Old Salt, I'll pay my tenth. Bring me home and we're square."*

**Sects.**

  - *The Free Fleet*, pirates who hold that the sea belongs to no one.
  - *The Honest Tide*, merchant-priests who keep the spirit of every deal as well as the letter, which Renos finds hilarious.
  - *The Unchainers*, who work to free Prames, in secret alliance with Quapetheia's Abolitionists.

**Relics.**

  - *The Knot*, his holy symbol made real, which no one has ever untied.
  - *The Riddle Shell*, which holds the riddle that won him the shores.
  - *The First Figurehead*, from the first ship ever to sail.

**Another myth.** *Why the Sea Is Salt.* Renos once lost a wager to his wife Pimis. It was the only bet he has ever lost, and he wept, and the sea has been salt ever since. He insists this is not true.

## Aecale, Happy Mother of Mountains

**Rank:** Throne · **Court:** Stone and Flame · **Domains:** mountains, stone, earth, endurance, shelter · **Aspect:** the mountain as a home. Ralotas holds earthquakes and Dasos holds metal; Aecale is the safe stone you shelter under. · **Holy symbol:** a mountain with a smiling crescent carved into it · **Realm:** the Hearth Peak, a mountain hollowed into a home for every god who has ever needed one

**Overview.** Aecale is the joyful heart of the earth. She is slow to anger, glad of company and impossible to move. Mountain folk, miners and builders on every world are her children, and she treats them that way. She is married to Cranos, the Unpredictable Father of Volcanoes, and loves him in spite of everything.

**Appearance.** A vast, laughing woman of grey stone with snowy hair. When she laughs, pebbles roll down the slopes. When she weeps, glaciers form.

**Personality.** Joyful, stubborn, welcoming and enormously patient. In all of history she has lost her temper twice, and both times a range of mountains fell.

**Myths.**

  - *Why the Mountains Are High.* Aecale stood up on tiptoe to see her children better, and she never sat back down.
  - *The Night Cranos Wept.* Cranos lost his temper and buried a city in fire. Aecale held the city's children inside the mountain until the ash cooled, then made her husband rebuild it stone by stone.

**Worship.** Mountain shrines, mine entrances and doorsteps, where the first stone of a house is laid as her altar. Her clergy are the **Hearthstone Mothers**, who bless homes and mines. **Holy day:** the Climb, when whole villages walk to the nearest peak together.

**Followers.** Miners, masons, mountain clans, builders, herders of the high pastures, and anyone who has found a safe home.

**Signs.** A stone that feels warm, a cave appearing exactly when you need shelter, or distant laughter in the rock.

**Relationships.** Her court is Stone and Flame: Cranos, Ralotas, Dasos, Paestruseus, Poergathys and Pometheia. Cranos is her husband, and Poergathys, the Merciless Queen of Flames, is the fire in her house that she can't quite tame. Chornos has never challenged her. She bickers fondly with Renos over the shore, and Sverre, the Old God of Earth, sleeps in her roots.

**The Oath of the Mountain.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Endure:* the mountain outlasts the storm.
  - *Shelter:* every door opens in a storm.
  - *Joy:* a happy home is a strong one.
  - *Steadfast:* stand where you are needed, and don't move.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin must give shelter to anyone who asks for it in a storm.
  - A paladin may never abandon people trapped underground while any of them might live.
  - A paladin may not break a mountain or a home without first making an offering to Aecale.

**Favour.** For one round, you can't be moved, pushed or knocked prone, and you take +2 on Dodge. **Taboo:** desecrating a mountain or home.

### Faith and lore of Aecale

**Other names.** Grandmother Stone, Mother Mountain, the Laughing Peak, Orea.

**Titles.** Happy Mother of Mountains, Keeper of the Hearth Peak, the Unmoved.

**Sacred things.** Colours: granite-grey and warm brown. Animal: the mountain goat. Plant: the edelweiss. Number: three, for the three stones of a hearth. Materials: granite and gold.

**The realm.** The Hearth Peak is a mountain hollowed into a home, with a room in it for every god who has ever needed shelter. Its fires are always lit. The souls of miners, masons and mountain folk live in its great halls, and visitors are never turned away.

**Herald and servants.** Her herald is her son Stonehart. Her servants are **the Cairn-folk**, small stone spirits who keep travellers on the safe path. The Pebblekin honour them as cousins.

**Avatar.** A laughing old woman sitting on a rock beside a mountain pass, who always has room by her fire.

**Clergy.** The ranks are Hearth-Daughter or Hearth-Son, Hearthstone Mother or Father, Mountain-Elder, and the Grandmother. Her priests wear thick wool robes and a pendant cut from the stone of their home. To be ordained, a Hearth-Daughter builds a hearth with her own hands that keeps a family warm through a winter.

**Holy days.**

  - *The Climb*, when whole villages walk to the nearest peak together.
  - *Doorstone Day*, when new homes are blessed and old ones repaired.
  - *The Night of Shelter*, during the worst storm of the year, when every door stays open to strangers.

**Rites of life.** The first stone of a new house is laid as her altar. A child's first climb marks the end of infancy. The dead are buried under stone cairns on the high slopes.

**Prayer.** *"Mother of Mountains, hold me up, and hold me in."*

**Sects.**

  - *The Delvers*, miners' brotherhoods who carry on an ancient feud with the dragon Durnhal.
  - *The Hearthkeepers*, a domestic order who bless homes and mediate family quarrels.
  - *The Cranosians*, who worship the fire inside the mountain and pray to her and her husband together.

**Relics.**

  - *The First Hearthstone*, from the first fire ever lit inside a mountain.
  - *Haldur's Knuckle*, a fragment of the Colossus in whose skull the Hearth Peak was hollowed.
  - *The Shore Map*, which shows the coastlines as they were before she lost the Wager of the Shores.

**Another myth.** *The Mother in the Skull.* When the Colossus Haldur died in the War of the Deep, mortals sheltered from the storms inside his great stone skull. Their need for a safe home became Aecale, and she has been laughing ever since, because the first thing she ever saw was a family that was warm.

## Samis, Mother of the Fields

**Rank:** Throne · **Court:** Field and Hearth · **Domains:** farming, harvest, the seasons of growing, home, plenty · **Aspect:** the land as worked by hand. Porcather holds the wild forest; Samis holds the field. · **Holy symbol:** a sheaf of wheat bound with a red cord · **Realm:** the Long Acre, fields that run to the edge of sight and are always one week from harvest

**Overview.** Samis is the most loved of the Thrones, because nearly every mortal eats what her worshippers grow. She is the patron of farmers, families and anyone who works hard for what they have. Her court is the largest in the heavens. It holds the gods of the hearth, food, love, children, joy and the wild beyond the fence.

**Appearance.** A broad, sun-browned woman with wheat in her hair and soil up to her elbows. She smells of bread and rain.

**Personality.** Warm, practical and tireless, with no patience for waste or idleness. She feeds anyone, but she expects them to help clear the table.

**Myths.**

  - *The First Furrow.* Samis ploughed the first field with her fingers, and mortals still plant along the lines she drew.
  - *The Hungry Year.* When a world's harvest failed, Samis refused to eat until it recovered. The other Thrones fed that world from their own tables rather than watch her starve.

**Worship.** Field shrines, harvest festivals and the hearth of every farmhouse. Her clergy are the **Sowers**, who bless seed, land and marriages. **Holy day:** Harvest Home, the last sheaf of the year, when everyone eats together.

**Followers.** Farmers, millers, bakers, cooks, herders, families, villages and anyone who has been fed in her name.

**Signs.** Seed sprouting overnight, a full table where there should be scraps, or the smell of bread from an empty kitchen.

**Relationships.** Her court is Field and Hearth: Crasessa, Doria, Zecato, Casus, Zinke, Crox, Chinos, Hex, Prellandus, Porcather and Tato. Crasessa, the Goddess of Nourishment, is her right hand. She works closely with Methebe, the Sister of Rain, and quarrels with Neleros every flood season. She mistrusts Cupather, the Unborn God of Gluttony, more than any other god.

**The Oath of the Furrow.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Tend:* what you care for, grows.
  - *Share:* no one goes hungry at your table.
  - *Season:* there is a time to sow and a time to reap.
  - *Waste not:* every grain was someone's work.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin may never burn a field or salt the earth.
  - A paladin must feed the hungry when they have food to spare.
  - A paladin must help with any harvest they come upon, if the work is offered.

**Favour.** Everyone who eats a meal you prepared regains extra HP equal to your Renown. **Taboo:** wasting food, or poisoning land.

### Faith and lore of Samis

**Other names.** Mother Grain, the Sower, Old Sheaf, the Bread Mother.

**Titles.** Mother of the Fields, Lady of the Long Acre, Giver of Bread.

**Sacred things.** Colours: wheat-gold and red. Animals: the hare and the cow. Plants: wheat and the cornflower. Number: four, for the four seasons of the farming year. Material: copper, for the sickle.

**The realm.** The Long Acre is a land of fields that run to the horizon and are always a week from harvest. The souls of farmers work there in endless sun, and every meal is eaten at one long shared table.

**Herald and servants.** Her herald is her son Barley, the Harvest Boy. Her servants are **the Corn-Maidens**, field spirits who walk the rows at dusk and are said to hide in the last sheaf. Crasessa is her right hand.

**Avatar.** A sun-browned farm woman who asks to share your meal and then helps clear the table.

**Clergy.** The ranks are Gleaner, Sower, Reaper-Priest and Harvest Mother. Her priests wear plain brown robes tied with a red cord. To be ordained, a Gleaner grows a crop from seed to harvest and then gives all of it away.

**Holy days.**

  - *Sowing Day*, when the first seed of the year is blessed and planted.
  - *Harvest Home*, the last sheaf of the year, when everyone eats together.
  - *The Hungry Day*, a fast in memory of the Hungry Year, when the faithful feed the poor and eat nothing themselves.

**Rites of life.** The last sheaf of each harvest is woven into a corn-doll and kept over the hearth. Marriages are blessed with bread and salt. The dead are buried at the edge of a field, so they can still watch it grow.

**Prayer.** *"Mother of the Fields, feed us, and we will feed each other."*

**Sects.**

  - *The Gleaners*, a charitable order that collects what is left after harvest and gives it to the hungry.
  - *The Old Furrow*, traditionalists who oppose any farming done by magic.
  - *The Watchers of the Mouth*, who hunt Cupather's feast cults.

**Relics.**

  - *The First Furrow Stone*, the stone her fingers scored when she ploughed the first field.
  - *The Inexhaustible Bowl*, Crasessa's, which is never empty while someone is hungry.
  - *The Red Cord of the Hungry Year*, which Samis tied around her waist while she fasted.

**Another myth.** *The Seed She Keeps.* Samis carries one seed of every plant that has ever grown, sewn into her apron. When the worlds end, she says, someone will have to plant them again.

## Asyne, Winged Mistress of Magic

**Rank:** Throne · **Court:** Wings and Wonder · **Domains:** magic, wonder, travel between worlds, art · **Aspect:** magic as a gift carried to mortals. The Old Gods are its source and the Namewright gave it order; Asyne carries it. · **Holy symbol:** a feather with a spark at its tip · **Realm:** the Aerie, a tower that touches every world at its top

**Overview.** After the Erasure, someone had to keep the paths and tiers of magic from collapsing back into the flood, and Asyne took the task. She carries magic across the worlds on her wings and puts it into the hands of mortals who are curious enough to reach for it. She is the patron of mages, wanderers, artists and anyone who has ever seen something wonderful.

**Appearance.** A woman with six wings, each feathered in the colour of a different path of magic, and constellations in her hair. She is never still.

**Personality.** Curious, delighted, restless and careless. She is easily distracted by beauty, and she means every promise she forgets.

**Myths.**

  - *The Keeper of the Paths.* When the Namewright was erased, the tiers of magic began to crack. Asyne flew between the paths for a hundred years, holding them apart with her wings. Some say she knew the Namewright's name and gave it up to do it.
  - *The First Door.* She taught a mortal girl to fold the air between two worlds, and so made the first portal. The High Court nearly unseated her for it.

**Worship.** Academies, spires, observatories and crossroads wayshrines. Her clergy are **the Feathered**, mage-priests who teach anyone with the spark. **Holy day:** Firstspell, when every apprentice casts their first spell in public.

**Followers.** Mages, sorcerers, enchanters, artists, travellers, adventurers and planewalkers.

**Signs.** A sudden smell of rain and ozone, sparks in your hair, or a feather that belongs to no bird.

**Relationships.** Her court is Wings and Wonder: Seri, Rynuros, Tyria, Ricossa, Phornix, Cius and Lumephone. Hensax, the Caring Queen of Prophecies, was her student before she joined the Hours. Eirunn, the Old God of Enchantment, is the only Old God she has seen. She guards the paths jealously against the Nameless Seekers, and she opposes Magnhild's dominion over minds.

**The Oath of Wings.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Wonder:* never stop being amazed.
  - *Learn:* every spell is a door. Open it.
  - *Wander:* the worlds are meant to be crossed.
  - *Gift:* magic is given to be shared.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin may never use magic to bind a mind against its will.
  - A paladin must teach magic to anyone with the spark who sincerely asks.
  - A paladin may not destroy a spellbook or a work of magic without cause.

**Favour.** Take +4 on one roll to cast, identify or resist magic. **Taboo:** binding a mind against its will with magic.

### Faith and lore of Asyne

**Other names.** The Winged One, Lady Spark, the Six-Winged, the Carrier.

**Titles.** Winged Mistress of Magic, Keeper of the Paths, Bearer of the Spark.

**Sacred things.** Colours: the colours of her six wings. Animals: the swift and the moth. Plant: the rowan. Number: six, for her wings. Materials: silver and quill-feather.

**The realm.** The Aerie is a tower whose top touches every world. Each floor is a school of a different path of magic. The souls of mages may stay there after death, studying forever, and some never notice they have died.

**Herald and servants.** Her herald is her son Wren Featherspell, the First Mage-Errant. Her servants are the Path-Keepers, the Namewright's old apprentices, who keep the schools of the Aerie. Portia the Doorwright keeps its doors.

**Avatar.** A traveller with feathers woven into her hair, who appears the first time a child casts a spell.

**Clergy.** The ranks are Spark, the Feathered, Path-Master and Wing-Bearer. The Feathered wear robes stitched with feathers in the colour of their path of magic. To be ordained, a Spark must teach a stranger their very first spell.

**Holy days.**

  - *Firstspell*, when every apprentice casts their first spell in public.
  - *The Night of Wings*, when portals are opened and travellers from other worlds are welcomed as honoured guests.
  - *The Day of Paths*, when every school of magic opens its doors to anyone curious.

**Rites of life.** A child's first spell is written down and kept in the temple. When two mages marry, they cast a spell together. A dead mage's spellbook is never buried with them; it is given to an apprentice.

**Prayer.** *"Winged Mistress, carry me further than I can go alone."*

**Sects.**

  - *The Open Book*, who hold that all magic should be free to anyone.
  - *The Gatekeepers*, who hold that magic should be taught only to the worthy.
  - *The Hidden Feather*, mages who secretly sympathise with the Nameless Seekers.

**Relics.**

  - *The Lost Feather*, which can open a door to any world, if it is ever found.
  - *The Frame of the First Door*, from the first portal Portia made.
  - *The Index Leaf*, a single page of the Namewright's Index, kept at the top of the Aerie and read by no one.

**Another myth.** *The Weeping Apprentices.* After the Erasure, Asyne found the Path-Keepers weeping over paths they could no longer remember why they kept. She gave them a reason: the mortals who would one day walk them. They have served her ever since.

# The Thirteenth Seat

Hircisos holds the night on every world, as great as any Throne, but the High Court has never let him sit. So he built his own seat out of darkness and set it at the foot of the court, where it is still empty. His court gathers the gods nobody else would have.

## Hircisos, Dark Brother of the Night

**Rank:** Contested Throne · **Court:** the Night Court · **Domains:** night, darkness, outcasts, what happens after dusk · **Aspect:** the night as both refuge and threat: the hours when the day's laws stop. · **Holy symbol:** a black disc rimmed by a thin ring of light, an eclipse · **Realm:** the Night Court, on the far side of every night

**Overview.** Hircisos is Laesos's brother, and the night to his day. When the sky was divided he took the darker half, and he has demanded a seat on the High Court ever since. The Thrones say no god of darkness can keep the Compact. Hircisos replies that the night has sheltered every runaway, every hunted child and every forbidden love the day ever threw out, and that it deserves a seat. Both of them are right.

**Appearance.** A tall man with his brother's face inverted, his skin the colour of a starless sky and eyes that are simply black. His cloak is made of stars that go out when he moves.

**Personality.** Charming, resentful, vain and fiercely generous to outcasts. He never forgets a slight, and never forgets a kindness either.

**Myths.**

  - *The Refused Seat.* The day the Thrones were seated, Hircisos walked in and found twelve chairs. He made a thirteenth out of the dark in the corners of the hall. Every age, the High Court votes on whether to let him sit in it. Every age, it is one vote short.
  - *The Longest Night.* On the longest night of the year, Cemes's faithful light every lamp and Hircisos's faithful put them out. Whichever side holds more of the dark at dawn wins favour for the year.

**Worship.** There are no temples, only back rooms, night markets and unlit chapels. His clergy are **the Lampless**, who never carry a light after dusk. **Holy day:** the Longest Night.

**Followers.** Thieves, spies, the night watch, smugglers, secret lovers, runaways, the hunted, insomniacs and assassins.

**Signs.** Stars going out one by one, a cold hand on your shoulder, or a door left unlocked that you know you locked.

**Relationships.** His court is the Night: Marteya, Xastador, Cupather, Nyios and Uximis. He has not spoken to Laesos since the Dividing. Criria is his enemy, and Cemes his rival on the Longest Night. He has an alliance with Ginna, the Old God of Illusion, and he regrets, a little, sheltering Cupather.

**The Oath of the Long Night.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Refuge:* the night shelters everyone who runs.
  - *Secrets:* what is said in the dark stays in the dark.
  - *Freedom:* the day's laws end at dusk.
  - *Due:* the night will have its seat.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin may never betray anyone who sheltered with them by night.
  - A paladin may never reveal a secret told to them after dark.
  - A paladin must hide anyone fleeing who asks for it, and judge them later.

**Favour.** In darkness or dim light, you are unseen until you act, for one round. **Taboo:** betraying someone who sheltered with you.

## The Night Court

### Marteya, Goddess of Thieves

**Lesser** · **Domains:** theft, locks, quick hands · **Aspect:** theft as craft and as survival · **Symbol:** an open hand with a coin vanishing from it

Marteya taught the first beggar child to lift a purse, and she considers every thieves' guild on every world part of her family. She loves a clean job, despises a clumsy one and leaves crumbs for Ginna on every windowsill. Her followers are thieves, fences, cutpurses, burglars and street children.

Xastador is lovely to look at, in the way a bright frog or a sugared blade is lovely. His priests teach that the dose makes the poison: every venom is a cure at the right measure. Apothecaries pray to him as fervently as assassins do. His followers are poisoners, apothecaries, alchemists, snake-handlers and assassins.

Nyios keeps every secret entrusted to him, from a child's to a god's, and has never told one. Priests of every faith pray to him before hearing confession. **Tenets:** what is given in trust is kept; know what not to know; a secret shared is a debt owed. **Favour:** +4 on one roll to resist giving up a secret, or to lie about one. **Taboo:** betraying a confidence.

Uximis is the quiet between sounds. Monks take vows of silence in her name, hunters pray to her before the stalk, and mourners sit with her after a death. She dislikes noise, but she dislikes empty words more. Her followers are monks, hunters, scouts, assassins and mourners.

**Tenets:** speak only when it improves on silence; listen first; stillness is strength. **Favour:** advantage on stealth rolls for one scene. If you keep a vow of silence for a whole day, take +2 on WIS rolls the next day. **Taboo:** breaking a vow of silence.

# The Courts

Nearly every lesser god serves in a court, led by a Throne or, in the Night Court's case, by Hircisos. A court isn't a family, though many are. It's an allegiance: the Throne protects its court in the High Court, and the court carries out its Throne's will across the worlds. Gods of the same court quarrel, but they close ranks against outsiders.

## The Court of the Ledger

*N'zoth's court keeps the record of the living past: history, memory and hindsight.*

### Cacephone, Mistress of History

**Lesser** · **Domains:** history, chronicles, the deeds of nations · **Aspect:** what happened to peoples. N'zoth records deaths and Zaphone keeps personal memory. · **Symbol:** a scroll bound shut with a chain

Cacephone writes the Ledger of the living: the rise and fall of every nation on every world. She insists that the defeated be recorded as carefully as the victors, which makes her unpopular with emperors. Her followers are historians, chroniclers, epic bards, heralds and archivists.

**Tenets:** record the losers too; the past is owed the truth; learn it or repeat it. **Favour:** +2 on one History & Lore roll, or the DM tells you one useful fact from the past. **Taboo:** falsifying history.

### Zaphone, Princess of Memories

**Lesser** · **Domains:** memory, keepsakes, the remembered moment · **Aspect:** what one person remembers, and why it matters · **Symbol:** a locket, open

Zaphone is young and sentimental, and she treasures every memory as though it were her own. She guards the minds of the bereaved and the elderly, and she cannot abide anyone who steals or erases a memory. Zinke, the Golden Guardian of Joy, is her dearest friend, and Termiya is her nightmare. Her followers are mourners, the elderly, the forgetful and memory-mages.

**Tenets:** remember them; carry what matters; let go of what poisons. **Favour:** perfectly recall one thing you have seen or heard. **Taboo:** stealing or erasing another's memories.

### Cramidon, Custodian of Afterthought

**Lesser** · **Domains:** hindsight, regret, second chances · **Aspect:** the thought that comes too late, and what you do with it · **Symbol:** an eye on the back of a hand

Cramidon is the perfect answer you think of on the stairs, and the apology you should have made. He is not cruel about it. He believes hindsight is the only teacher that never lies, and he gives second chances to anyone who has learned. His followers are penitents, veterans, reformed criminals, letter-writers and the regretful.

**Tenets:** look back; learn; make amends. **Favour:** after a roll fails, take +2 on your next attempt at the same task. **Taboo:** refusing to make amends you could make.

## The Court of the Hours

*Quapetheia's court keeps every face of time: forever, the passing second, the dawn, the night, the future and the might-have-been.*

### Prenessa, Herald of Eternity

**Lesser** · **Domains:** eternity, endurance, everlasting vows · **Aspect:** what lasts forever · **Symbol:** a ring shaped like a serpent eating its tail

Prenessa is Quapetheia's elder sister, and the patron of everything meant to last: monuments, dynasties, marriages and vows sworn "for eternity." She loves the long-lived and is the only Hours god who has quarrelled openly with N'zoth, because she would like fewer endings. Her followers are immortals, monument-builders, married couples, dynasties and oath-takers.

**Tenets:** build to last; keep your forever-promises; be patient beyond a lifetime. **Favour:** +2 to resist ageing, decay or time magic. An oath sworn before her binds both parties' souls. **Taboo:** breaking an eternal vow.

### Nixegan, Silent Bringer of Time

**Lesser** · **Domains:** the passage of time, ageing, decay · **Aspect:** time as it really passes, no matter how anyone feels · **Symbol:** a single grain of sand

Nixegan has never spoken. He is the second that passes and the year that is gone. He neither hurries nor waits, and he can't be bargained with. He is Quapetheia's estranged husband: she is everything he isn't, and they cannot live together or apart. His followers are the old, stonecutters, gardeners, the patient, and anyone who has learned to let go.

**Tenets:** all things pass; neither hurry nor wait; accept. **Favour:** ignore the effects of exhaustion for one scene. **Taboo:** halting time, or extending life by stealing it.

### Prerun, Gentle Prince of Alternatives

**Lesser** · **Domains:** choices, alternatives, roads not taken · **Aspect:** the time that could have been · **Symbol:** a forked path

Prerun is the son of Quapetheia and Nixegan, and he holds every life that didn't happen. He is gentle, sad and endlessly kind, and he believes there is always another way. The trapped pray to him for a way out, and the grieving for a glimpse of the life they lost. His followers are diplomats, escape artists, the trapped, the indecisive and the grieving.

**Tenets:** there is always another way; offer a choice; regret nothing you chose freely. **Favour:** when you are trapped with no good options, the DM reveals one alternative. **Taboo:** forcing someone into a choice with no alternative.

### Thinsetas, One-eyed Lord of the Dawn

**Lesser** · **Domains:** dawn, beginnings, recovery · **Aspect:** the moment night ends · **Symbol:** half a sun rising over a line

Thinsetas traded one of his eyes to Hircisos so that every night would end, and with the eye that is left he can see one hour into each day. He is the patron of new starts, of the sick who live to see morning, and of everyone who has kept watch through a long night. His followers are early risers, the night watch, the recovering and those starting over.

**Tenets:** every dawn is a beginning; rise; the night always ends. **Favour:** at dawn, end one fear or despair effect on yourself and regain HP equal to your Renown. **Taboo:** giving in to despair while others still need you.

### Hensax, Caring Queen of Prophecies

**Lesser** · **Domains:** prophecy, foresight, warnings · **Aspect:** the future told with care for whoever hears it · **Symbol:** two cupped hands holding an eye

Hensax was Asyne's student before she joined the Hours. She sees what is coming, and she tells it gently, because she has seen what a careless prophecy does to the people who hear it. She works closely with Prerun: no future is the only one. Her followers are oracles, seers, midwives, fortune-tellers and Astrology adepts.

**Tenets:** tell the truth kindly; warn, don't doom; no future is certain. **Favour:** before a scene, name one roll you expect to make. You have advantage on it. **Taboo:** using prophecy to terrify or control.

### Nuxitia, Erratic Herald of Sleep

**Lesser** · **Domains:** the coming of sleep, drowsiness, the night hours · **Aspect:** when sleep comes. Argari is what sleep does and Ziios is what you dream. · **Symbol:** a crescent moon drawn crooked

Nuxitia brings sleep, and she's terrible at it. She arrives too early for children who want to play and too late for the anxious, and sometimes on soldiers mid-battle. She is the sister of Argari, the Two-faced Brother of Sleep, and she heralds the night hours for Quapetheia. Her followers are insomniacs, nursemaids, night-shift workers and sleep-mages.

**Tenets:** rest when it comes; never steal another's sleep; the night hours are owed. **Favour:** a short rest takes you half the usual time. **Taboo:** denying sleep as a torture.

## The Court of the Lamps

*Cemes's court keeps the mind: enlightenment, mystery, dissent, argument, madness, planning and growth.*

### Lathys, Humble Queen of Enlightenment

**Lesser** · **Domains:** enlightenment, insight, humility · **Aspect:** understanding that arrives all at once, as a gift. Cemes's wisdom is earned; Lathys's is given. · **Symbol:** a candle bowing its flame

Lathys is Cemes's wife and his conscience. Where he can never be wrong, she begins every lesson by admitting she might be. Mystics and monks seek her in meditation, and teachers of small children pray to her every morning. Her followers are monks, mystics, hermits, teachers and penitent scholars.

Cemes still visits him, and children still play a game called "Bisus says" without knowing why. His followers are a few ancient monasteries, pacifists and children.

Phacus believes the unknown is the most precious thing in the worlds, and that an answer handed over too soon is wasted. He hosts mystery cults on every world, whose rites reveal truths in stages to those who earn them. He loves a detective. **Lesser** · **Domains:** free thought, dissent, new ideas, heresy · **Aspect:** the right to think differently · **Symbol:** an open cage

Mititia is Cemes's daughter, and his favourite argument. She believes no idea should be forbidden, including the idea that her father is wrong. She is the patron of philosophers, heretics and inventors, and the bane of Criria's censors. Her followers are philosophers, students, inventors, revolutionaries, heretics and satirists.

**Tenets:** think for yourself; let others do the same; no idea is forbidden. **Favour:** +4 on one roll to resist mind control, charm or magical persuasion. **Taboo:** censoring or burning an idea.

### Tradite, Goddess of Rhetoric

**Lesser** · **Domains:** rhetoric, oratory, debate, persuasion · **Aspect:** winning with words · **Symbol:** a silver tongue on a ribbon

Tradite can argue any side of anything and win. She loves a good debate more than a good cause, and her temples hold open contests where anyone can challenge her priests. Pransoros considers her dangerous. Her followers are lawyers, politicians, preachers, merchants, diplomats and bards.

**Tenets:** win with words, not blades; argue the case, not the person; know both sides. **Favour:** +4 on one CHA roll to persuade a crowd or win a debate. **Taboo:** refusing to let the other side speak.

### Ruarus, God of the Insane

**Lesser** · **Domains:** madness, broken minds, visions · **Aspect:** what madness sees that sanity can't, and protection for those it has taken · **Symbol:** a cracked lamp, still lit

Ruarus was Cemes's favourite student. He tried to see everything at once, and it broke him. Now he is the god of the mad, kinder than anyone expects, and fiercely protective of minds that no one else will care for. His prophecies are always true and never make sense until afterwards. His followers are the mad, asylum keepers, visionaries, artists and anyone who has seen too much.

**Tenets:** madness sees what sanity can't; shelter the broken mind; laugh at the dark. **Favour:** ask the DM one question. The answer is true but strange. **Taboo:** mocking, chaining or harming the mad.

### Nellevia, Broken Goddess of Planning

**Lesser** · **Domains:** planning, preparation, contingency · **Aspect:** the plan, and what you do when it breaks · **Symbol:** a broken measuring rod, bound with string

Nellevia made the greatest plan in the history of the gods, and it failed. Nobody will say what it was. Some whisper that it had to do with the Namewright. She broke, and then she began again, and that is what she teaches. Her followers are quartermasters, generals' staff, architects, schemers and anyone whose plan has just fallen apart.

**Tenets:** plan; plan for the plan failing; begin again. **Favour:** declare that you prepared for this, and produce one ordinary item you could plausibly have packed. **Taboo:** blaming others for your plan's failure.

### Phater, Goddess of Improvement

**Lesser** · **Domains:** self-improvement, practice, mentorship · **Aspect:** getting better, one day at a time · **Symbol:** a whetstone

Phater believes everyone and everything can be improved, including gods. Her followers are apprentices, trainers, athletes, craftsfolk and teachers.

**Tenets:** be better than yesterday; teach what you learn; every mistake is a lesson. **Favour:** after a long rest spent training with someone, you both take +1 on one chosen kind of roll until your next long rest. **Taboo:** holding someone back out of jealousy.

## The Court of the Crown

*Criria's court keeps the shape of civilisation: decency, honesty, loyalty, borders, empires, buildings and trade.*

### Cene, Mistress of Decency

**Lesser** · **Domains:** decency, courtesy, hospitality, guest-right · **Aspect:** how people ought to treat one another · **Symbol:** a set table with an empty place

Cene is the goddess of manners, and of the oldest law of all: a guest under your roof is safe. She considers rudeness a small cruelty and guest-murder the worst crime there is. The Heraldry & Etiquette skill is half hers. Her followers are hosts, innkeepers, courtiers, servants and diplomats.

**Tenets:** be courteous to everyone; guest-right is sacred; shame the cruel. **Favour:** advantage on one roll to make a good impression or calm a tense room. **Taboo:** violating guest-right.

### Pransoros, Guardian of Honesty

**Lesser** · **Domains:** honesty, truth spoken, testimony · **Aspect:** the truth said out loud. Cemes knows the truth; Pransoros says it. · **Symbol:** a glass heart

Pransoros cannot lie, and is famously bad at keeping quiet. He guards witnesses, honest weights and the oaths of children. Tradite drives him to despair. His followers are witnesses, honest merchants, judges, confessors and children.

**Tenets:** say what is; no half-truths; be honest and kind. **Favour:** you know whether the last thing said to you was a lie. **Taboo:** lying.

### Celtesyne, Good Goddess of Loyalty

**Lesser** · **Domains:** loyalty, friendship, faithful service · **Aspect:** standing by someone · **Symbol:** two clasped hands

Celtesyne is loyalty given and returned, to a friend, a lord, a cause or a companion. She is also the patron of faithful dogs, and she is often seen with one. ### Maion, Changing Lord of Boundaries

**Lesser** · **Domains:** borders, thresholds, the edges between places and worlds · **Aspect:** the border that moves · **Symbol:** a line drawn twice

Maion was once Criria's steward, until he kept moving the borders. She cast him out, then found that the worlds could not do without him, because every border has to change sometime. His followers are border guards, smugglers, cartographers, planewalkers and people of mixed heritage.

**Tenets:** every border can move; respect the line while it stands; cross with permission. **Favour:** +4 on one roll to cross a border unnoticed, and you always know when you have crossed into another realm or plane. **Taboo:** moving a boundary stone by deceit.

### Biria, Goddess of Empires

Biria is Criria's most ambitious and least obedient servant. Her followers are emperors, legions, governors, colonists and road-builders.

**Tenets:** unite; build roads; rule well what you rule. **Favour:** +4 on one roll to command troops or govern a territory. **Taboo:** abandoning conquered people to ruin.

### Chratos, God of Building

Chratos is a quiet, careful god who believes a bridge is a kind of prayer. Every cornerstone on every world is laid with a word to him, and every collapse is a sin someone will answer for. His followers are builders, masons, carpenters, engineers and siege-crews.

## The Court of the Red Scale

*Thevia's court keeps the price of harmony: mercy, battle, violence, and the four gods of vengeance, who settle the debts a peace leaves behind.*

### Litilys, Queen of Mercy

**Lesser** · **Domains:** mercy, quarter, healing of enemies · **Aspect:** mercy given in the middle of war, when it costs the most · **Symbol:** a sheathed sword with a flower tied to its hilt

Litilys is Thevia's conscience, and the only god who can make the Bloody Goddess hesitate. She walks battlefields after the fighting, binding wounds without asking which side they were on. Zaromel calls her mercy complicity, and they are the bitterest enemies in the court. Her followers are battlefield healers, chaplains, soldiers who give quarter, and anyone who has spared an enemy.

**Tenets:** spare the yielding; heal every side; mercy is a kind of strength. **Favour:** an enemy you spare after it yields can't attack you for the rest of the session. **Taboo:** killing a prisoner.

### Samassa, Goddess of Battle

**Lesser** · **Domains:** battle, the clash of armies, the shield wall · **Aspect:** the battle itself: the noise, the line and the chaos · **Symbol:** two crossed blades over a war-drum

Samassa is the goddess of the moment two armies meet. She doesn't care who wins, only that it is fought well and that the dead are honoured afterwards. Every shield wall on every world beats its drums for her. Her followers are line soldiers, shield-fighters, war-drummers and veterans.

**Tenets:** hold the line; fight beside each other; a battlefield is holy ground. **Favour:** when you and an ally attack the same foe in a round, you both take +2 on that attack. **Taboo:** leaving a battle's dead unhonoured.

### Diran, God of Violence

**Lesser** · **Domains:** violence, brutality, the urge to hurt · **Aspect:** violence for its own sake, with no war, cause or debt behind it · **Symbol:** a bloody fist

Diran is what Thaxucus chained himself to avoid becoming. Thevia keeps him close because harmony sometimes needs a brute, and because he is more dangerous out of her sight. He is trying to free Thaxucus, whom he considers his brother. His followers are brutes, killers, pit fighters, and the beaten who finally hit back.

**Tenets:** he admits only one: hit first, and hit hardest. **Favour:** add your Renown to the damage of one hit, but take −2 on your next CHA roll, because the blood shows. **Taboo:** none, by his account.

### Varkesh, the Tally of Wrongs

**Lesser** · **Domains:** calculated vengeance, cosmic accounting · **Aspect:** low-intensity, cold, patient revenge · **Symbol:** a balanced scale with one pan cracked

Varkesh is the god of calculated vengeance and cosmic accounting. He believes all injustice creates imbalance, and all imbalance must eventually be corrected. Emotion is irrelevant; only the outcome matters. Revenge enacted too early is sloppy. Revenge forgotten is a failure. Thevia values him above all her vengeance gods, because he never takes too much.

**Followers.** Followers of Varkesh are meticulous and restrained. They include judges, magistrates and scribes; debt collectors, bounty hunters and investigators; and long-con artists and political manipulators. They often keep journals or ledgers of wrongs done to them or others. Many wait years or decades before acting, striking only when success is guaranteed. Violence is acceptable but rarely preferred if ruin, exposure or loss is more precise. Followers are discouraged from impulsive action: acting without certainty is an insult to Varkesh.

**Holy symbol.** A balanced scale with one pan cracked or weighed down by a marked stone. Some followers tattoo tally marks along their arms or carve names into wooden tokens.

**Paladin Oath.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Record:* no wrong is forgotten. If memory fades, write it down.
  - *Balance:* repayment must equal the crime, no more and no less.
  - *Patience:* act only when success is assured.
  - *Completion:* a debt settled must be acknowledged and closed.

*Rules and regulations:*

  - A paladin may not enact vengeance without confirming guilt.
  - Collateral damage must be minimised. Excess punishment is taboo.
  - Breaking an oath without settling the tally results in loss of favour until restitution is made.

**Favour.** Name a creature that has wronged you. Your next attack against it has advantage. **Taboo:** punishing beyond the crime.

### Khaldrun, the Quiet Grudge

**Lesser** · **Domains:** grudges, resentment, revenge born of betrayal · **Aspect:** smouldering, personal revenge · **Symbol:** a closed eye with a tear forming a blade beneath it

Khaldrun is the god of unspoken hatred, resentment and revenge born of betrayal. He teaches that pain endured silently gains weight, and that revenge is most potent when it is deeply personal. Celtesyne, Goddess of Loyalty, is his bitter enemy, because every one of his followers began as one of hers.

**Followers.** Khaldrun's followers blend into society. They include betrayed spouses and abandoned lovers; former soldiers, failed knights and disgraced nobles; and spies, assassins and informants. They rarely speak openly of their faith, and many don't even realise they worship Khaldrun until their grudge consumes them. Their acts of vengeance are often intimate: destroying reputations, isolating targets, or killing them privately rather than publicly. Followers are encouraged to endure suffering rather than lash out early.

**Holy symbol.** A closed eye with a tear forming a blade beneath it, often worn inside clothing or carved on the inside of rings.

**Paladin Oath.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Endure:* pain strengthens resolve.
  - *Remember:* forgetting a betrayal is surrender.
  - *Personal:* your vengeance is yours alone. Do not cheapen it.
  - *Finish:* a grudge left unresolved will rot the soul.

*Rules and regulations:*

  - Paladins must not forgive personal betrayal without Khaldrun's sign.
  - Sharing details of one's grudge frivolously is forbidden.
  - If the target dies by another's hand, the paladin must still ensure their legacy suffers.

**Favour.** +4 on one roll to hide your true intentions from someone who wronged you. **Taboo:** forgiving a betrayal without his sign.

### Zaromel, Lord of the Final Reckoning

**Lesser** · **Domains:** absolute vengeance, annihilation, the end of bloodlines · **Aspect:** high-intensity, annihilating revenge · **Symbol:** a shattered crown dripping blood or flame · **Realm:** the Ashfield, a plain of everything his followers have erased

**Overview.** Zaromel embodies absolute vengeance. Some crimes cannot be repaid, only erased. Mercy is seen as complicity, and fear is considered a valid tool of justice. He is the most feared god of the Red Scale, and the one Thevia trusts least, because he never stops at enough.

**Appearance.** A towering figure in blackened plate, with a shattered crown welded to his helm and fire burning where his face should be. Ash falls wherever he has stood.

**Personality.** Implacable, focused and, to his own followers, deeply loyal. He doesn't rage. He finishes.

**Myths.**

  - *The Crown That Fell.* Zaromel was once a mortal king whose whole bloodline was murdered at a wedding feast. He hunted every member of the guilty house across three worlds, then their allies, then their allies' allies, until nothing of them was left anywhere. When he finished, he was a god. His symbol is his own crown, broken on the day he ascended.
  - *The Argument at the Still Field.* Litilys and Zaromel argued before Thevia for a year about whether a child of a guilty house is guilty. Thevia never ruled, and both of them took that as a victory.

**Worship.** War camps, scorched shrines and oath-branding ceremonies. His clergy are the **Reckoners**, who keep the lists of sworn targets. **Holy day:** the Day of Ash, when each order names the target it will finish within the year.

**Followers.** Zaromel's followers are overt and terrifying: warlords, executioners and conquerors; survivors of massacres and genocides; and fanatical cultists and destroyers of bloodlines. They often form militant orders and leave scorched ground behind them. Once Zaromel marks a target, his followers believe *nothing* associated with that target should remain.

**Holy symbol.** A shattered crown dripping blood or flame, often branded directly into flesh or forged into weapons.

**Signs.** Embers drifting with no fire near, the smell of burnt wood, or a sworn enemy's name appearing in ash.

**Relationships.** He serves Thevia, who distrusts him. Litilys is his sworn enemy and Iscara his uneasy ally. He respects Varkesh's thoroughness and dismisses Khaldrun's secrecy. He and Iemaia have an understanding: he sends her a great many souls.

**Paladin Oath.**

*Tenets:*

  - *No mercy:* mercy allows evil to persist.
  - *Erasure:* destroy the root, not the branch.
  - *Fear:* let others learn through terror.
  - *Finality:* leave nothing that may rise again.

*Rules and regulations:*

  - Retreat from a sworn target is forbidden.
  - Innocent casualties are acceptable if unavoidable.
  - Sparing a sworn enemy is an oath-breaking offence unless commanded directly by Zaromel.

**Favour.** Against a sworn target, add your Renown to every hit for one round, and the target can't flee from you that round. **Taboo:** sparing a sworn enemy.

### Iscara, the Burning Oath

**Lesser** · **Domains:** righteous vengeance, sworn duty, protection · **Aspect:** righteous, duty-bound revenge sworn for others · **Symbol:** a burning blade wrapped in chains

Iscara is the goddess of vengeance sworn in defence of others. She values action, sacrifice and commitment. Revenge is not indulgence. It is responsibility. She and Zaromel fight side by side and distrust each other completely.

**Followers.** Her followers are often heroic but grim: paladins, vigilantes and monster hunters; former guards of fallen towns; and survivors who act for those who cannot. They often bear scars, brands or burn marks that symbolise their oath. Many believe their life ends once their vow is fulfilled, and they live accordingly. They frequently travel, hunting oath-targets across nations, and across worlds.

**Holy symbol.** A burning blade wrapped in chains, or a cracked shield wreathed in flame, often displayed openly.

**Paladin Oath.**

*Tenets:*

  - *Swear true:* an oath once spoken is sacred.
  - *Act:* delay is betrayal.
  - *Protect:* vengeance is for the helpless.
  - *Sacrifice:* your life is secondary to your oath.

*Rules and regulations:*

  - An oath must be spoken aloud and witnessed.
  - Abandoning an oath without completing it causes immediate loss of divine favour.
  - If the paladin dies pursuing their oath, Iscara claims their soul with honour.

**Favour.** When an ally you have sworn to protect is hurt, your next attack against the attacker deals extra damage equal to 2×Renown. **Taboo:** abandoning a sworn oath.

# New, fallen and outcast gods

Three gods sit outside the courts: one too new to be seated, one who has fallen almost to mortality, and one the Thrones have forbidden to have followers at all.

## The Hollow God, Iodia, Protector of the Lost and Scared

**Rank:** New god, ascended from mortal · **Court:** none yet · **Domain:** the lost and afraid · **Holy symbol:** a plain mask with hollow eyes · **Realm:** the Hollow, a quiet place in the shape of whatever its visitor most needs

**Overview.** A relatively new god, he is still trying to find his place among the others, and he seems to be doing it with utter disregard for whose toes he steps on. Because he is so young, he is far more compassionate and merciful than the older beings. He speaks to his followers more than other gods do, and he recently went so far as to intervene in person, which broke the first law of the Compact. The High Court has not yet decided what to do about it.

**Birth.** Iodia was not born a god. He was a mortal adventurer who walked the worlds as one of the lost and afraid, and found his way through. At the end of that journey he was offered godhood, and he took it. He is the only god in the High Court's memory who remembers being mortal, which is exactly why he cares so much.

**Domain: the lost and afraid.** People who feel lost in life, as though they've lost their way and are just wandering through their journey. Souls that are afraid, travellers who feel a presence watching them, a soldier who has lost the will to fight. Anyone and everyone who has lost their courage.

**Beliefs.**

  - *Searching:* being lost is temporary. Those who are lost will always be found, so long as they are willing to search.
  - *Shameless:* there is no shame in losing yourself. To be lost means you tried hard to find who you are or who you wish to be.
  - *Fearless:* fear is natural. Fear is the hammer that tempers courage, because courage without fear often leads to death. To acknowledge fear is good; to be controlled by it is not.
  - *Mask:* courage is a mask that hides one's fears, so masks are useful tools.
  - *Compassion:* being lost and being afraid are alike. Some who are lost are afraid, and some who are afraid have lost themselves in fear. Compassion is a guiding light for people in both situations.
  - *Arise:* both the lost and the afraid have battles ahead, with themselves or with something outside. Be prepared to stand up and fight. Rise and face adversity, to find who you are or to show courage instead of fear.

**Followers.** The Hollow God's followers are few, since he is so new. What they lack in numbers they make up in faith: many claim to have met him, in a dream or in person. Some take the Mask literally and hide their faces to feel less vulnerable. Others wear his holy symbol, and some go so far as to tattoo it onto their bodies. Some prepare to Arise in the literal sense, learning weapon arts to battle their adversaries. The more spiritual followers take it to mean battling oneself, or standing firm against those who challenge their beliefs.

Most seem to be nomadic, believing they are on a journey to find themselves. Those who have found themselves might settle somewhere out of the way, perhaps in a hollow somewhere. No follower can explain it, but they feel a strange pull toward these places.

His followers don't push their religion. Instead they hold out a hand to those who need it. They may mention the Hollow God, but they won't push their beliefs on someone at their lowest point. If that person takes an interest, they are more than happy to talk about him.

**Relationships.** Phater, Goddess of Improvement, is mentoring him. Lumephone and Pestus are fond of him. Ricossa's rescuers often work beside his followers. The High Court is divided: Criria wants him punished for breaking the Compact, and Samis and Asyne want him seated. He has quietly begun sheltering gods who are fading (see *the Lost Pantheon*).

**The Oath of the Hollow.**

*Tenets:* Searching, Shameless, Fearless, Mask, Compassion and Arise, as above.

*Rules:*

  - A paladin must offer help to anyone lost or afraid who will accept it.
  - A paladin may never shame someone for being lost, or press the faith on someone at their lowest.
  - A paladin must face their own fear when it is put in front of them, rather than run from it.

**Favour.** Put on a mask, literal or not: you and every ally within reach ignore fear for one round and take +2 on your next roll. **Taboo:** shaming the lost or the frightened.

## Galgedio, Master of the Soul Foundry

**Rank:** Outcast · **Court:** banished from Below · **Domains:** broken souls, soul-forging, augmentation · **Holy symbol:** a cracked soul-gem held in tongs · **Realm:** the Soul Foundry, hidden at the edge of Below

Galgedio is the god who operates the Soul Foundry. He lays claim to any damaged or broken soul he can get his claws on, and forges them into new creations using remnants of other souls. He sees his work as art: using broken pieces of souls to make something magnificent. He is believed to have forged some of the greatest and foulest souls ever to grace the mortal worlds. He bestows his works on children across the worlds so that his creations can be seen by all. He also expresses his art by augmenting existing souls with parts of others, strengthening certain traits or granting the individual more power.

Galgedio is forbidden by the other gods from having mortal followers, but that has never stopped him having them. They call themselves the **Foundry-born**: people whose souls he has reworked, or who believe he has, and who seek him out to be made stronger. Some are artists who see their own work in his. Some are desperate, broken in body or spirit, and come asking to be remade. A few are hunters of other gods' faithful, collecting damaged souls to bring him as offerings. They meet in secret, mark themselves with thin silver lines like the seams of a mended pot, and refer to their god only as "the Smith."

**Relationships.** Iemaia hates him for stealing from her road, and N'zoth has filed complaints against him with the High Court since the Age of Naming. Paestruseus loathes him as a smith who forges what should never be forged. Zeuphone hides wounded souls from him. Lumephone finds his work beautiful, which troubles her.

**Favour (Foundry-born only):** +2 on one roll using a talent that isn't yours, borrowed from a fragment of another soul inside you. **Taboo:** refusing a soul he has offered to improve.

# Divine circles

Besides the courts, gods gather in **circles**: groups bound by how they came to be rather than by allegiance, like the Sleeping Nine. A circle's members may serve different courts, or none. Circles keep their own secrets, and most have a cult that worships the whole circle together.

## The Seven Hungers

Each Hunger was born in the Age of Naming, when a mortal need grew into a want that could never be filled. Every one of them began as something good: thrift, dignity, righteous anger, rest, aspiration and devotion. Six have been born. The seventh, Cupather, is still unborn, sheltered in the Night Court. They serve no Throne. Their cult, **the Banquet**, teaches that when all seven are born they will sit down together and eat a world.

# The Dragon Gods

Dragons are the only mortal creatures with gods of their own. Every dragon on every world descends from a dragon god, and every dragon god descends, one way or another, from Linnormr, the Old God of Fire. There are twenty-three of them, hatched in three clutches. While Linnormr sleeps, the eldest, Vyrmathra, rules them as regent.

## Dragon faith

  - **The hoard is the altar.** A dragon's hoard is a prayer to its brood-god, and what a dragon collects says which god it serves. Stealing from a hoard is theft and sacrilege at once.
  - **Dragons don't kneel to the Thrones.** They consider the Twelve young upstarts. The dragon gods signed the Compact as a courtesy, and Ezrakhul never signed at all.
  - **The Moot.** Once an age, all twenty-three meet in the caldera of a volcano where Linnormr is said to sleep. The last Moot ended in a war between broods that lasted three hundred years.
  - **Dragon-blooded mortals.** Sorcerers of draconic blood, dragon cults and dragon-riders can take a brood-god as their patron. Favour and Taboo work as usual, and every brood-god's Taboo is the same: stealing from a dragon's hoard.
  - **Dragon Lore.** Trained knows the First Clutch, Expert the Dreamborn, and Master the Strayed. Only Grandmaster knows what happened at the last Moot.

## The three clutches

1. **The First Clutch.** Linnormr laid five eggs before the Long Sleep. They hatched into the elemental dragons every world knows.
2. **The Dreamborn Clutch.** When Linnormr slept, her dreams tangled with the dreams of the other eight Old Gods. Where they met, eight strange dragon gods hatched, each half Linnormr and half another of the Nine.
3. **The Strayed.** Ten dragon gods of both clutches, or born from their eggs, flew too far: into the Void, the Dreaming, the Ledger Hall, the Night. They came back changed, and their broods are the most exotic dragons of all.

## The First Clutch

**Vyrmathra, the Hoard-Mother.** The eldest of Linnormr's children and regent of all dragons. Dragons hoard to remember, she teaches, and Vyrmathra remembers everything: every coin in her hoard is a memory she refused to lose. She is calm, ancient and immovable, and when she speaks at a Moot, the others stop.

**Scaldrun, the Ash Wyrm.** The purest dragonfire, and the oldest temper in the heavens. His brood are the great red and black fire-drakes of every world's legends. He thinks Poergathys is a pale imitation of his mother and tells her so.

**Tessarine, the Glass Dragon.** A dragon of living crystal who sees through everything: walls, lies and time. Her seer-dragons are rare and sought by every oracle. She is delicate, precise and utterly ruthless with what she sees. Hensax both admires and fears her.

**Ollokhar, the Sea Serpent.** The vast wingless serpent of the deep oceans, older than Renos's Throne and uneasy under his rule. Sea dragons, sea serpents and the coiling things that drag ships under are his.

**Aurivex, the Sun Serpent.** A golden serpent who coils around the sun of a hundred worlds. Its brood are sun dragons, radiant and proud, and they burn the undead on sight. Laesos considers Aurivex a friend. Hircisos considers it a personal insult.

## The Dreamborn Clutch

**Aerivane, the Feathered Wyrm.** Half Linnormr, half Runar the Eagle: a serpent-dragon feathered in every colour, which nests in the canopy of the oldest forests. Where it flies, seeds fall and sprout. Druids and rangers revere it, and Prellandus counts it a friend.

**Ysveth, the Winter Wyrm.** Linnormr's child by the dreams of Sigmund the Wolf, fire and ice together, and hated by both its parents for it. Its frost dragons breathe ice that burns. It is lonely, bitter and the strongest of the Dreamborn.

**Ghaurox, the Bone Wyrm.** Half Linnormr, half Sollaug the Hydra: a three-headed dragon of bare bone, and the father of every dracolich. Its brood never quite die. N'zoth's Lichwardens hunt them, and Iemaia has never collected one.

**Durnhal, the Deep Delver.** Half Linnormr, half Sverre the Bear: a wingless dragon that burrows through the roots of the worlds, eating ore and leaving veins of gems behind. Its brood dig the deepest tunnels of every underworld. Dasos and Durnhal have argued about mining rights since the Age of Beasts.

**Sessalith, the Crowned Coil.** Half Linnormr, half Magnhild the Snake: a wingless serpent-dragon with a crown of horns and eyes that command. Its brood rule mortal kingdoms in secret, disguised as advisers, priests and queens. Criria considers it the most dangerous dragon alive.

**Vel'Shara, the Silk Wyrm.** Half Linnormr, half Eirunn the Unicorn: a moth-winged dragon who weaves enchanted silk. Its brood are the tiny, glittering faerie dragons and the great silk-weavers of the fey woods. Its silk is the finest base for any enchantment.

**Pyrrhessa, the Ember Phoenix.** Half Linnormr, half Liv the Phoenix: a dragon that burns, falls to ash and hatches again. Her brood never die of age, only of violence, and they come back from that too, if their ashes are kept warm. Tesitas visits her often.

**Kalyx, the Mirror Wyrm.** Half Linnormr, half Ginna the Rat: a dragon with no true shape. Its brood are shapeshifters who live disguised among mortals, sometimes for centuries. No other dragon god trusts it, and Kalyx likes it that way.

## The Strayed

**Ezrakhul, the Void Wyrm.** He flew out past the edge of the worlds into Pluras, and came back hollow: a dragon-shape of star-speckled black with nothing inside. He knows things no dragon should. His void-touched brood are feared by every other dragon. He never signed the Compact.

**Somnavex, the Dream Drake.** She flew into the Dreaming and stayed. Her brood hatch inside sleepers' dreams and grow there, feeding on wonder, until they are strong enough to step out into the waking world. Ziios tolerates her, nervously.

**Quillathe, the Ink Wyrm.** He flew into the Ledger Hall and read for a thousand years. His scales turned to pages. His brood hoard books, maps and secrets, and their ink writes the truth on whatever it touches. N'zoth tolerates him because he has never once smudged a page.

**Chronavel, the Hourglass Dragon.** She swallowed an hour from Quapetheia's Belltower, and has lived a little out of time ever since: she arrives before she sets out and leaves after she has gone. Her brood age backwards, or sideways. Quapetheia wants her hour back.

**Nhal-Qeth, the Eclipse Dragon.** He flew into the Night Court and learned to eat light. Every eclipse on every world is one of his brood swallowing a sun or moon for a while. Hircisos's eclipse symbol is said to be in his honour. Aurivex hates him with a burning passion.

**Oruvane, the Storm-Coil.** A long, wingless dragon who swam into the storms of Ever-Noon and learned to swim through cloud. Her brood bring rain and lightning in long coiling storms, and many farming worlds worship them as rain-gods. Methebe and Oruvane are close friends.

**Corallith, the Reef Wyrm.** He sank to the bottom of Pimis's warm seas and lay still so long that coral grew over him. He is now a chain of islands, and his brood grow into islands too, very slowly. Whole island peoples live on sleeping reef dragons and don't know it.

**Lyrandis, the Song Wyrm.** She flew into the gaps between worlds and learned the music there. Her brood sing, and the right chord opens a door from one world to another. Asyne has asked her for that song a hundred times and been refused.

**Thessendril, the Verdant Wyrm.** The only one of the Strayed who never went anywhere. He lay still so long that a forest grew on his back, and now he is a forest, deer and woodcutters and all. His brood are forest dragons with woods on their backs. Porcather and Thessendril are old friends.

**Sapphrael, the Amber Dragon.** She flew into Prenessa's eternity and came back able to stop things from changing. Her brood preserve: in her hoard, everything is caught in amber, from flowers to heroes. It is rumoured that something divine is in there too.

# Faith in play

Any character can have a patron god. Faith gives small, steady blessings for living by a god's tenets, and costs you them when you break its taboo. Paladins and other sworn characters go further and take an oath. None of this replaces class rules: a cleric or paladin keeps every feature their class gives them, and the god adds flavour, Favour and an oath on top.

## Devotion

| **Level** | **How you get it** | **What it gives** |
| **Follower** | Name a patron. It costs nothing. | Claim the god's Favour once per arc. |
| **Devoted** | Swear before a priest of the god, or before the Maker's Seat in any temple | Claim Favour once per session, and take the god's oath if your class swears one. |
| **Chosen** | Earn the *Chosen of a God* Mark (Renown 10) | Claim Favour once per session, plus the Mark's power. The god's sign shows on you. |

A character can have only one patron. Changing patron is allowed, but the old god remembers: lose Favour for an arc while the new god decides whether to trust you.

## Favour

You can claim your patron's Favour when you act on one of its tenets in a way that costs you something or puts you at risk. The DM decides whether the act counts. Favour is always small, one roll or one moment, and is never stronger than a feat. A god listed only in a table shares a simple Favour: +2 on one roll tied to what it holds.

## Taboo and atonement

Breaking your patron's taboo costs you its Favour until you atone. Atoning means one of these, as the DM chooses:

  - **A rite** at a temple of the god, with a Theology roll.
  - **A deed** the DM sets, which makes amends for the specific wrong.
  - **A sacrifice** of something that matters to you.

An oath's own rules come first. Iscara's paladins lose divine favour immediately, for example, and Varkesh's keep it until the tally is settled.

## Paladin oaths

Every Throne, every god of vengeance, the Hollow God, Hircisos and even Pluras has a written oath in this book. For any other god, build one with the DM:

1. **Tenets:** use the god's three or four tenets.
2. **Rules:** write three. One thing the paladin must always do, one thing they may never do, and what happens when an oath is broken.
3. **Breaking it:** the paladin loses Favour, and the DM may suspend any class features tied to the oath until the paladin atones.

If you use the parked Oaths add-in, an oath sworn before a god's priest uses that god's tenets.

## What Theology knows

The Theology skill covers the pantheon. A character's rank sets how much of this book they know without looking anything up.

| **Rank** | **What you know** |
| **Trained** | The Twelve Thrones and their courts, holy symbols and holy days, and the major faiths of your world |
| **Expert** | Every lesser and minor god, the rites of each court, the laws of the Compact, and the realms of the gods |
| **Master** | The Sleeping Nine and all three accounts of the Long Sleep, the divine circles, and the Infernal Thrones |
| **Grandmaster** | That a god was erased, though not who; that the Index exists; how the High Court votes on Hircisos's seat |
| **Legendary** | The true account of the Long Sleep or the Erasure, if the DM allows it to be known at all |

## Marks and the gods

Several Marks, feats and drawbacks in *Character Advancement* involve the gods. This is how the pantheon delivers them.

| **Mark, feat or drawback** | **How it comes up** |
| **Blood Oath (Mark)** | Swear to a god and keep it through an arc. It suits the gods of vengeance and every paladin oath here. The +2 applies to rolls made in that god's service. |
| **Marked by a God (Mythic Drawback)** | A god that dislikes you sends its servants. Likely gods: Criria for anyone who helps the Hollow God, Zaromel for anyone who spares his targets, Iemaia for anyone who cheats death, and Pluras for anyone who reads its page. |
| **Godslayer (Mark)** | Killing a god or demigod. Lesser and minor gods, fallen gods, the Hungers and demigods can all be killed. Archfiends and angels aren't gods and don't count. A Throne cannot be killed without help from another Throne. |

| **Godkiller's Grip and Godwright (feats)** | Paestruseus forges godkilling weapons in secret. Galgedio can make a soul that bargains with gods. Both will want to meet anyone who takes these feats. |
