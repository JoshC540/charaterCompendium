# Soldier
class: Soldier

- **To the Bitter End.** When in a battle line, you gain one auto-pass on death saves each turn, so long as one of those next to you still stands.
- **Martial Prowess.** +2 to attack rolls and +5 damage on all successful attacks.

## Commander

The commander is a soldier who has proven particularly adept at reading the ebbs and flows of the battlefield, understanding where to push to the most effect. They have also garnered greater skill at enabling others to reach their full potential on the battlefield.

- **Inspiring Presence.** To have a commander present does a lot to ease the nerves of soldiers. All nearby friendly combatants are immune to the charm, fear and terror effects.
- **Rally Me.** When the odds are unfavourable you know how to rally your forces. When you use this ability (up to 3 times a fight), all allies who withdraw to you gain +5 defence and +5 to attack rolls when near you for the next 1d5 turns.
- **Commanding Presence.** You emit a level of confidence that inspires those weaker than you to follow with little to no question, allowing you to quickly rally a fighting force. You can also speak louder than the average person shouts, if you wish.
- **Right Approach.** Upon passing an intelligence check, you gain a better understanding of the threat you face and your best approaches to it — what they are weak to, which troops are best suited to the task, etc.
- **Follow My Lead.** Every attack made after yours that hits the same target you struck that turn deals an additional 5 damage and grants the attacker 1d4 temporary health.

## Horseman

Horsemen make up the standard mounted forces of a kingdom, riding whatever mount is most easily accessible — generally horses. They are often deployed on the flanks to punish poorly positioned enemies, or at the front and back of convoys for efficient protection.

- **Charge.** Spend a turn rushing at the enemy atop your steed. The next turn you deal your damage per turn + your damage from the charging turn, multiplied by two. 3 turn cooldown.
- **Hit and Run.** You are capable of taking both the attack and disengage action each turn. Or you and your mount may both attack the foe without any risk of unseating you or giving disadvantage.
- **Mounted Attack.** If you or your mount succeeds in an attack, the other is granted advantage on their attack against the same foe and deals +3 damage.
- **Strike from Above.** Your crit range against non-mounted/smaller foes is 18–20.
- **Born to the Saddle.** You cannot be dismounted against your will by anything short of death or magical means.

## Field Medic

Field medics are responsible for ensuring that none of their fellow warriors go to an untimely grave. They take an oath to protect all life, which heavily discourages them from taking lives — even of foes. But the oath says nothing about maiming.

- **Sleep Now.** When you deal what would be lethal damage, you render the target unconscious instead, upholding your oath to protect all life.
- **Crippling Slice.** You target muscle groups to give a target disadvantage on attacks or dodges, depending on whether you strike the arms or the legs.
- **Hold On.** You can grant help actions as a bonus action to a target making death saves, or suspend the rolling of death saves for up to 10 turns.
- **Patch Up.** During a short rest, you heal yourself and all allies twice their hit die value. You also cure any natural status effect twice as effectively.
- **Home Remedy.** You may carry a home remedy for a status effect of your choice that also heals 1d20. It holds up to 5 uses and refills upon entering a town or village. Remedies can also be sold there if there is demand.

## Swordsman

Swordsmen train with their blades until they become an extension of themselves. They are suited to both group and single combat, making them desirable to adventuring parties and military groups alike.

- **Improved Dodge.** When you dodge you may attempt to trip (dex save) or disarm (strength save) the attacker.
- **Perfect Strikes.** Every successive hit on a target increases your crit range on that target until you land a crit or miss. The minimum crit value you can reach is 10.
- **Parry.** In place of a dodge you may attempt a parry: roll within one of the attacker's melee roll. On a success you deal your damage + the damage that attack would have done back to them; on a fail you take double damage.
- **Second Slice.** Every second turn you can make an additional attack that deals half damage.
- **Dismember.** When you critically strike a foe, you deal an additional 2d12 damage, and if the victim fails a strength check you chop off one of their limbs (or may deal an extra 10 damage).

## Bulwark

Bulwarks are walking walls — difficult to hit for any meaningful damage and skilled at enduring vast amounts of abuse until others can clear the attackers. They are the anvil for the hammer to strike against.

- **I Am the Shield.** When wearing heavy armour you gain temp hp equal to half your max hp; you can refresh this once a turn.
- **Anvil.** You gain +25 max hp and your min hit value is doubled (if applicable), letting you take greater damage and hold out longer.
- **Shields Are Weapons Too.** Your shields deal damage equal to a sword of the same tier, letting bulwarks fight solely with shields. Shields also have a 3 in 4 chance to stun or stagger.
- **Turtle Mode.** You lose the ability to dodge or Squash for 2 turns but triple your temp hp. Only usable in combat and when not already in turtle mode; you can exit at any time, losing the additional temp hp.
- **Squash.** Fall atop a foe, dealing damage equal to your temp hp + your min hit value. 1d4 recharge time.

## Warbreaker
sixth: yes

*The One Sent Ahead*

Every other soldier is trained to hold a line. The warbreaker is trained to find the one man whose death ends it. They are sent ahead of the army rather than in it — into the enemy camp, the enemy formation, the enemy command tent — with the understanding that they are not expected to come back, and the further understanding that they usually do. Where a commander wins battles, a warbreaker prevents them.

- **Break the Line.** Hold the Line works in reverse against enemies. Any foe adjacent to you loses all formation, leadership and adjacency bonuses, including their own line bonuses and any buffs granted by a commanding ally. This applies whether or not you attack them.
- **Kill the Head.** You automatically identify the ranking figure of any enemy force on sight, as the Hunter does. You gain +4 melee against them and they cannot be shielded, guarded or interposed for — bodyguards, taunts and protective abilities do not prevent you reaching them.
- **Rout.** When you fell an enemy in view of their allies, every enemy that witnessed it rolls a wisdom save or is Shaken until the end of their next turn: disadvantage on attacks, and they cannot advance toward you. Enemies who watch their leader fall save at disadvantage.
- **Through the Ranks.** Moving between enemies does not provoke attacks, and you may move through any space occupied by an enemy of your size or smaller, forcing a strength save or knocking them prone as you pass.
- **No Army Stands Forever (1/day).** Declare a formation broken. For 1d4+1 turns, no enemy within sight may benefit from any ally-granted bonus, issue or receive orders, or take reactions to protect one another. Every enemy fights as an individual, and you gain an additional attack action each turn for the duration.

# Berserker
class: Berserker

- Gain 1d20 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **Greater Bloodlust.** You can drop to half hp as a bonus action; this hp loss is imbued into your next normal attack.
- **Two-handed Combat Proficiency.** You can dual wield your weapons, making two attacks a turn, one with each weapon.

**Starting gear — choose one.**
Weapons: Darksteel Greataxe (two-handed, 1d20) · Darksteel Claymore (two-handed, 1d20 + 15) · Bloodsteel short axes (2d12, half damage dealt is lifestolen).
Or equipment: Helm of Discord (−5 to damage from all sources within striking distance) · Tattoo of Rage (below 10% hp, all damage out is doubled) · Boots of Stomping (on turn one of combat, all combatants must roll to stay standing; can be repeated as an action on later turns).

## Path of the Divine

The path of the divine berserker uses their rage to reach a higher plane of thought and power, reaching towards enlightenment with each rage — some seem almost tranquil while raging. Forces of the divine smile upon these warriors, and in exchange for slaying the undead, the foes of the divine, reward them with greater health and strength.

- **Divine Bloodlust (Passive).** The first time you hit 50% and/or 25% hp, you may enter a standard rage, or harness the divine to heal back to full hp and remove all current status effects.
- **Clarity Through Destruction.** Upon killing a foe, you gain advantage on your next non-combat roll, stacking up to 2 advantages on a roll.
- **Blessed Strike.** Three times a combat, your weapon attack is blessed, dealing magic damage with +10 damage.
- **Favoured by the Divine.** When you slay an undead foe, gain 5 stacking temp hp and +2 to your next melee roll.
- **Shoulder to Lean On.** Once per combat, channel divine power to swap current hp values (excluding temp hp) with a party member.

## Path of the Bloodlust

Bloodlust berserkers live for one thing and one thing only: glorious carnage. They seek to spill the blood of all who stand in their way and grow stronger with each blow. There are numerous records of these berserkers dying at the end of combat as their hearts simply stop.

- **Blood Spilt (Passive).** Gain 1 stack each time you successfully attack an enemy or are hit. Spend stacks for +1 damage each, 1 hp of healing per 2 stacks, or 10 stacks for a blood-chilling shout that forces all combatants to save or attempt to flee combat. At 15 stacks you go down at the end of combat; at 30 you die at the end of combat.
- **One with the Blade.** Advantage on all melee rolls and disadvantage on all ranged rolls.
- **There Is Always a Voice Yelling in My Head.** You are almost immune to effects that target the mind — there's not much there to target.
- **Down to My Level.** Once per combat, bring a targeted non-boss enemy down to your hp value in one crushing blow.
- **One More Time for Those Who Have Fallen in My Name.** If you go down, deal XdY damage to a targeted enemy — X = number of dead combatants, Y = your total weapon damage per turn.

## Path of the Slayer

A slayer lives and breathes for combat and for striking down anything in their path. They are warriors who know no fear and fight to their last breath, never retreating.

- **Going Down Swinging (Passive).** Your death saves become attack rolls with the same rule as death saves (11+ to pass). You fight even as your body fails you.
- **Edge of Rage.** You begin berserking at 75% hp. Attacks deal x3 damage below 10% hp and x4 damage while on death saves.
- **Fearless Beyond Sense.** Immune to terror and fear, at the cost of being unable to disengage the moment you berserk.
- **The Bigger They Are.** If a foe is taller than you, all your attacks once you berserk deal an additional 10 damage.
- **Battle Roar.** Once per combat, unleash a primal roar: all allies gain +1d3 to melee for 1d4 turns, and enemies either flee or gain the vulnerable status for 3 turns.

## Path of the Beast

Path of the beast berserkers revert to a more primal state, choosing the beast they most align with and gaining some of its talents in combat.

- **Beastly Soul (Passive).** Choose your beast once; it cannot be changed. It shapes all abilities in this subclass and grants +2 to its stat: **Boar** (Strength), **Snake** (Dex), **Dolphin** (Intelligence), **Owl** (Wisdom), **Hawk** (Perception), **Panda** (Charisma). Other creatures allowed with DM approval.
- **Empowered Strikes.** Your first attack each combat triggers your animal effect; after that, all hits have a chance to trigger it.
  - Boar: stun and stagger on a failed strength check
  - Snake: poison and slow (−1 initiative position)
  - Dolphin: +3 damage and a 1d6 chance to disarm
  - Owl: advantage on your next roll and +2 damage for the next person to attack this target
  - Hawk: +1 initiative position and a guaranteed pass on your next dodge roll
  - Panda: the foe cannot target you for 1d3 turns, and you heal 1d3 hp
- **Spirit Animal.** Manifest a spectral copy of your beast that accompanies you and can do whatever its natural version can, but you lose access to Empowered Strikes.
- **Primal Rage.** You may berserk whenever you wish, gaining advantage on dex, str and melee rolls but disadvantage on all other rolls.
- **Animalistic Charms.** Your beast grants advantage on certain charisma rolls — Boar: intimidation · Snake: deception · Dolphin: information gathering · Owl: defending against charisma checks · Hawk: charming · Panda: all charisma.

## Path of the Merciful

Merciful berserkers seek to reduce the suffering of all those around them, raging against the pain of others. They are the most compassionate of all berserkers, fiercely loyal and protective of those they call friends — treat them well and they will often do the same for you.

- **Protective Rage.** You may enter rage when any party member takes damage. You enter an advanced rage (+10 damage) when a party member goes down, and can provide the help action on their death saves while attacking nearby foes.
- **Quick Death.** Deal an additional 5 damage to a target for each unique status effect they suffer. A terminally ill creature can be killed instantly and painlessly.
- **Healing Blood.** Take 1d12 self damage; any creature that consumes this blood (2 charges, lasts 5 in-game minutes) heals half the self damage taken or removes a status effect.
- **Be at Peace Now.** When a combatant enters death saves, you may make a free attack against them; on a pass they get 2 auto-fails or disadvantage on all death saves.
- **Mercy (Passive).** You are under oath not to attack non-combatants, the unarmed (those proficient in hand-to-hand don't count as unarmed), the unconscious or the fleeing. Following this path grants an auto-pass on death saves — but break a single part of this oath and you instantly die on your next death saves.

## Path of Ruin
sixth: yes

*Nothing Left Standing*

The other paths are personal — a berserker's god, their grief, their hunger, their beast, their mercy. Ruin is not personal. A berserker who walks this path stops being a man in a battle and becomes something the battle happens inside of; walls come down, ground gives way, and the difference between an enemy and an ally becomes a question of proximity rather than allegiance.

Those who survive fighting alongside one tend to describe the experience in the same language they would use for a landslide. Most tribes will not train it. The ones that do only send them where nothing is meant to be left standing.

- **Everything In Reach.** While in Blood Rage, every melee attack you make strikes all creatures adjacent to you, not only your target. This does not distinguish between friend and foe.
- **Sunder.** Your attacks damage terrain, cover, structures and held equipment as readily as flesh. You ignore all armour, shields and non-magical barriers entirely — walls, doors, barricades and siege works included. Weapons that block your strike are destroyed on a failed roll by their wielder.
- **Deeper Still.** Blood Rage gains a second threshold. Below 25% HP, damage out is tripled rather than doubled and damage in is quartered. You cannot voluntarily leave Blood Rage while below this threshold, and you cannot be healed above 50% during combat by any means.
- **The Ground Gives (3/combat).** Strike the earth instead of a target. Everything within 10m is knocked prone on a failed strength save and the area becomes difficult, broken terrain permanently. Indoors, this may bring down floors, ceilings or supports at GM discretion.
- **Ruin (1/day).** On entering Blood Rage, sustain it for 1d4+2 turns regardless of your HP. For the duration you cannot be reduced below 1 HP, your reach extends to 5m, and every turn you act the surrounding area degrades further — cover destroyed, structures collapsing, ground opening. When it ends you drop to 1 HP and cannot act on your next turn.

# Gladiator
class: Gladiator

- Gain 3d6 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **Follow Up.** After an attack, you may attempt to punch a target (1d6 — hits on a 1 or 6), dealing 1d2 damage with a 1d2 chance to stun.
- **Master of Weapon Repair.** Having mastered all melee weapons, you have turned to their repair. You may attempt to repair damaged weapons if you have the necessary facilities and gear.

**Starting gear — choose one.**
Weapons: Glass sword (one-handed, 1d40 — breaks if hit, blocked or on a missed attack) · Fascina (3d8, advantage on pulling an enemy impaled by it) · Retes (weighted net, disadvantage to escape).
Or equipment: Bag of holding · Weapon sharpening kit · Snake tattoo (advantage on melee attacks while grappling, instead of disadvantage).

## Andabata
Dodge-heavy, death by a thousand cuts.

Andabata fight blindfolded and yet seem impossible to hit. Some attribute this to blind luck, others to a pact with a dark god, but most agree it stems from intensive training. They dodge a foe and make shallow cuts until their opponent loses control and charges — then it's a simple matter to slay them.

- **Heightened Sense.** Whilst blindfolded, you gain +2 to perception via all other senses, and can still dodge at normal value.
- **Put Down.** If your opponent is berserk or affected by your Battle Madness, all successful strikes against them are automatic crits and you gain advantage on dodging them.
- **Battle Madness.** Each time you strike a target, instead of dealing damage you may apply battle madness stacks (1 per 10 damage). At 10 stacks the target goes mad and will only target you. The counter resets if you are hit.
- **Greater Agility.** You may dodge up to 1d3 times per turn, one fewer if you make an attack.
- **Blinding Strike.** Four times per combat, strike an opponent on the nose with your fist, blinding them for 1d2 turns.

## Rudiarius
Switching weapons.

A gladiator who has earned or taken their freedom is often called a rudiarius. They remain highly skilled fighters after leaving the pits, and those who keep fighting are dangerous opponents and often fan favourites. They fight with whatever weapon is closest to hand, with deadly effectiveness and practised ease.

- **Final Blow (Active).** Strike with your current weapon for +5 damage; the weapon breaks.
- **Final Blow (Passive).** When unarmed and hit by a melee weapon, you may try to seize it (contested strength roll; you take 5 damage pass or fail).
- **First Strike (evolves at 10 and 25 unique weapons).** Your first attack with a new weapon deals +25% damage (→ 50% at evolution 1, → 100% at evolution 2). Applies only to the first attempt, even on a miss.
- **Disarming Strike.** Twice per combat, attack an opponent's weapon instead; on a failed strength check they drop it.
- **Knockout Blow (replaces Follow Up).** After striking a target, you may strike the head (melee check). On a hit they take 1d4 damage with a 1d2 chance to be knocked out for 1d6 turns. On a fail, the target gains advantage against you next turn.
- **Better Improvised Weapons.** Improvised weapons deal 1d4 instead of 1d2.

## Cestus
Hand-to-hand and grappling.

- **Brawler.** Advantage on all unarmed attacks. Unarmed attacks deal 1d4 with a 1d4 chance to stun. On a 19 or 20, break one of the target's bones (−1 strength).
- **Flurry of Blows.** Three times per combat, make 1d6 unarmed attacks against a target.
- **Skilled Grappler.** Contested strength roll; on success the target is grappled to the ground. All combatants have advantage against the grappling pair. The target makes strength rolls at disadvantage to escape, and you gain advantage on unarmed strikes against them.
- **Chokehold.** With a foe grappled, maintain a chokehold for 3 turns and they black out (if they need oxygen). They make strength saves to escape meanwhile.
- **Catch.** Toss your equipped weapon at a target and roll a d2: on a 1 it stuns for a turn, on a 2 it deals half its weapon damage. You may then attempt to grapple the target as a follow-up.

## Gallus
Heavy armour, sponging hits.

- **Iron Discipline (Passive).** Armour gains extra bonuses:
  - Light: +1 defence, +10 temp hp
  - Medium: +3 defence, +20 temp hp
  - Heavy: +5 defence, reduce all damage by 3 (stacks), +30 temp hp, and you cannot be knocked prone
- **Juggernaut Stride.** Move through enemy spaces (they must dodge or be shoved aside). In heavy armour: **Trample** deals 1d6 + (20 − STR) to anyone you pass through; **Sundering Step** destroys light weapons/shields in your path on a crit.
- **Gladiator's Bulwark (Reaction).** When hit, reduce damage by half your defence bonus (min 1) in heavy armour. If damage is reduced to 0, breaks your armour, or only hits temp hp, counterattack automatically for 1d8 + current temp hp.
- **Arena-Crushing Onslaught.** Once per combat, enter Titan Mode for 3 turns: strikes deal +1d12 but cost −2 to dex, dodge and disengage that turn; you cannot be slowed, grappled or moved against your will; at the end, smash the ground (all foes DEX save or prone).
- **Blood and Sand.** Each time you block/ignore damage, gain 1 Favour (max 10; 2 per instance in an arena). Spend 5 to heal 1d8 HP, or 10 to bellow a war cry giving all foes −2 attack next turn.

## Laquearii
Beast fighting, whips, disabling.

- **Beast's Call (Passive).** Advantage on all attacks against animals/monsters. You can attempt to tame wounded beasts, which fight with you until combat ends and then leave (d100 vs the beast's remaining HP × 5).
- **Lash of Pain.** Whip attacks gain +1d8 damage, +1d4 bleed and +10 ft range. On hit, choose to disarm (STR check) or knock prone (DEX check).
- **Arena Trap.** Prepare a trap as an action (3 uses per combat): **Caltrops** — 2d4 damage + slow (DEX save); **Snare** — restrain target (STR save); **Blinding Dust** — disadvantage on attacks (CON save).
- **Beast's Fury.** When damaged by a beast/monster, frenzy: your next 3 attacks gain +1d6 damage, but you take +2 damage from all sources. Ends if no beasts remain.
- **Crowd Pleaser.** Perform a spectacle move (recharges on a kill): **Whirling Lash** — attack all enemies within 10 ft; **Daring Escape** — disengage with +5 movement; **Final Stroke** — automatically hit a bleeding target for 2x damage.

## Primus Palus
sixth: yes

*The First Sword*

A gladiator who has stood on the sands long enough that the crowd knows their name before the gates open, and so do the men sent out to die against them. The other five are styles of fighting; this is what happens when a style becomes a legend. A primus palus fights half the battle before drawing steel, because the opponent has already heard the stories, already seen the odds posted, and already knows what happened to the last four men who stood where they are standing.

Free or bonded, retired or still bleeding for coin, they never stop being the champion — the name follows them out of the arena and into every tavern, court and battlefield they walk onto.

- **Name Known.** Any humanoid who can see you recognises you. Pit Fighter increases to +4 against humanoid foes, and on first sight each enemy rolls a wisdom save or loses their first action of combat to hesitation. Enemies who have witnessed you win before save at disadvantage.
- **Call Out.** Challenge a single enemy as a free action. If they accept, you gain advantage on all rolls against them and any of their allies who interfere are Shaken by the crowd's contempt. If they refuse, they and every enemy who saw the refusal take -2 to all rolls until the end of combat.
- **Play to the Crowd.** Any onlookers — audience, townsfolk, even the enemy's own rank and file — can be turned. Make a combined melee and charisma roll to sway a watching group: they may cheer you, refuse to intervene, or turn on their own side outright. Usable once per gathering of witnesses.
- **The Champion Does Not Fall (1/combat).** When reduced to 0 HP in view of witnesses, you remain standing at 1 HP and immediately take an additional turn. Any enemy who watched this happen must save or be Shaken for the remainder of combat.
- **Undefeated (1/day).** Declare a fight yours. For 1d4+2 turns you cannot be flanked, surrounded or attacked by more than two enemies at once — the rest hold back to watch — and every foe you fell in that time grants you an additional attack action for the remainder of the duration. Ends early if you flee or refuse a challenge.

# Wardancer
class: Wardancer

- Gain temp hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **No Missed Opportunities.** Failing an attack action grants advantage on your next combat roll. Failing a climbing, running or similar roll by 5 or less lets you reroll it.
- **Parry.** Wardancer dodges are replaced with parries, and each subclass adds an additional parry type. Parries don't need leg movement to perform.

## Hail of Blades

Hail of blades wardancers are a storm of steel on the battlefield, seeming to strike down foes before they can react. Even when a foe does strike at them, a blade seems to materialise in its path before it can draw blood.

- **Punishing Parry.** When you parry, make an attack roll dealing half damage to the attacker.
- **Cleaving Strikes.** Your attacks also hit the targets adjacent to your target, for half damage.
- **Swift Strikes.** Twice per combat, strike 1d4 targets per attack roll. Out of combat, you can quickly interact with several objects at rapid speed.
- **Live by the Blade, Die by the Blade.** Below 20 hp, gain an additional attack action per turn. As you enter death saves you may make one attack against a nearby foe before going prone.
- **Reflective Movement.** An unused bonus action at the end of your turn becomes an automatic basic parry against the next attack. Out of combat, you roll with advantage to resist traps you trigger.

## Maiming

*"Haha, no more arms for you."*

- **Cutting Parry.** When you parry, make an attack roll to cut the foe as they withdraw: 1d4 damage with a 3d2 chance to bleed.
- **Maim (Passive).** Successful attacks give you a stack of maim (max 25), consumed by other abilities. Stacks beyond 25 convert to +1 damage on your next attack.
- **Severing Strike.** Spend maim to attempt to sever a limb (d20, must beat 6) on your next successful attack. On a standard human: 3 stacks for an arm, 5 for a leg, 10 for the head.
- **Blood Drenched.** Against a bleeding foe, gain an additional maim stack per hit, roll Severing Strike with advantage, and apply an additional bleed stack each hit.
- **Cut Down.** Mark an enemy with less current health than you (one mark at a time). Your attacks against them have advantage and give double maim stacks. When they die, gain 1d12 temp hp and you may reapply the mark.

## Crippling

*"You don't need to kill them — just make them wish you had."*

- **Inhibiting Parry.** On a successful parry, strike back to inhibit them: arms (disadvantage on their next attack or strength roll) or legs (disadvantage on their next dodge/disengage or dex roll).
- **Tendon Slice.** On hit, reduce the target's speed by 5 ft (stacks). At 0 speed they collapse prone and can't stand until healed; while prone they attack at disadvantage.
- **Joint Lock (Bonus Action).** Grapple a foe with blade-assisted holds, dealing 1d6/turn. Escaping requires a STR check; failing deals 1d6 to them. If the grappled foe has arm inhibition, they drop their weapons.
- **Pressure Points.** After 3 hits on the same foe: they have disadvantage on all rolls for 1 turn, and your next hit ignores armour.
- **"Stay Down" (Passive).** If a prone foe tries to stand, make a free 1d8 attack; on hit they stay prone (STR to resist).

## Death by 100s Cuts

- **Tripping Parry.** On parry, sweep the foe's legs — DEX save or fall prone. If prone, your next attack on them adds +2 bleed stacks.
- **Bleeding Dance (Passive).** Every 3rd hit on the same foe inflicts bleed. Critical hits double existing bleed stacks.
- **Flurry of Nicks (Action).** Make 4 attacks at 1d4 each; each hit adds 1 bleed stack.
- **Final Stroke.** If a foe has 5+ bleed stacks, consume them all to deal +1d6 per stack. If this kills, nearby foes are splattered (1d4 bleed).
- **Measured Pace (Stance).** If you haven't moved this turn: +2 to dodge rolls, and your next attack deals +2 damage per bleed stack on the target.

## Party Protection

- **Deflecting Parry.** On parry, redirect the attack: choose another foe within 5 ft to take the original damage roll.
- **Guardian's Step (Reaction).** Swap places with an ally before an attack hits them; your reaction refreshes after being hit. The ally's next attack gains +2 damage.
- **Blade Barrier (Action).** Create a 10 ft protective zone: allies inside gain +2 reactions. Lasts until you move or are knocked prone.
- **Sacrificial Parry (Reaction).** Intercept an attack meant for an ally, taking the damage reduced by your weapon's damage. If this reduces it to 0, counterattack for x2 damage.
- **Rallying Flourish (Passive).** After killing a foe, allies gain 1d6 temp HP. If it downs the last enemy, heal 1d8 instead.

## Endless Dance
sixth: yes

*The Step That Does Not Stop*

Flow of the Battle grants a wardancer a second attack; the endless dance asks why it should ever stop at two. These wardancers treat a fight as a single unbroken movement rather than a sequence of turns — each blow feeding the next, each fallen enemy opening the step toward another. They do not tire, they do not disengage, and they do not pause to reassess. Watching one work is less like watching a duel and more like watching something wind up. The danger, always, is that the dance is the only thing keeping them alive, and it needs bodies to continue.

- **Momentum.** Every enemy you strike this turn adds a Beat, to a maximum of 6. At the start of each of your turns you gain one additional attack action per 2 Beats held. Beats are lost entirely if you end a turn without landing a hit, or if you take no attack action.
- **Never Set Down.** Felling an enemy immediately grants an extra attack action, which does not count against Momentum's cap and may itself chain if it fells another. Chained kills have no limit.
- **Cannot Be Caught.** Moving between attacks never provokes, and you may move up to 3m before or after each attack action you take. Enemies cannot make attacks of opportunity against you at all while you hold 4 or more Beats.
- **Answer With Steel.** While at maximum Beats, any enemy who attacks you and misses is struck immediately for a free half-damage hit. This does not consume an attack action or add a Beat.
- **The Dance Does Not End (1/day).** Lock your Beats at maximum for 1d4+2 turns. They cannot be lost, and for the duration every attack action you take that hits grants a further attack action on a 4+ on a d6, rolled each time, with no cap. When it ends you lose all Beats, cannot gain Beats on your next turn, and take a level of exhaustion.

# Duelist
class: Duelist

- **Challenge.** As a bonus action, challenge a creature you can see within 30 ft. For the next minute you have advantage on opportunity attacks against it, and it has disadvantage on attack rolls against anyone other than you. Only one creature can be challenged at a time.
- **Superior Technique.** Once per turn, when you hit a creature with a weapon attack, add your Dueling Bonus to the damage roll.
- **Dueling Bonus.** Starts at 0 and increases by 1 for each duel won.

## Brawler

This duelist doesn't believe in a fair fight; they believe in a finished fight. Brawlers use grapples, throws and overwhelming physical pressure to dominate an opponent up close, using their environment and sheer strength to break the enemy's stance and spirit.

- **Dirty Hands (Passive).** You are always considered armed. Your unarmed strikes deal 1d8; on a hit the target must pass a strength save or be pushed back 5 ft with speed reduced by 10 ft until the end of their next turn.
- **Savage Takedown.** As an action, make a melee attack against your challenged target. On a hit it is grappled (escape is contested strength; you add your Dueling Bonus) and you immediately make an unarmed strike against it as part of the same action. Doesn't need a free hand.
- **Improvised Weapon Master.** You're always proficient with improvised weapons, which deal 1d10 of an appropriate type. Once per short rest, a hit with one can force a dex save or stun the target until the end of their next turn.
- **Unbreakable Hold (Evolving — Evo 1 at 5 grapples, Evo 2 at 10 kills on grappled foes).** While you have a creature grappled:
  - it has disadvantage on dexterity saves (Evo 1: double disadvantage; Evo 2: it also loses all resistances)
  - at the start of each of its turns it takes damage equal to your Dueling Bonus (Evo 1: +5 damage; Evo 2: 1d4 chance to deal double)
  - you can use your reaction to make an opportunity attack if it tries to cast a spell
- **Final Beatdown.** Once per combat, when you drop your challenged target to 0 HP with an unarmed strike or improvised weapon, you may knock them out non-lethally for 1 hour instead — or make a terrifying example of them: all enemies who witness it make a wisdom save or are frightened of you for 1 minute.

## Dirty Fighting

A duelist of this school knows honour is a luxury for the living. They win by any means necessary — sand in the eyes, a kicked shin, a hidden dagger.

- **Pocket Sand! (Passive).** As a bonus action, force your challenged target to make a dex save or be blinded until the end of their next turn. Once per target per short rest.
- **Exploit Weakness.** Once per turn, your weapon attacks against your challenged target deal +1d8, or 2d6 + Dueling Bonus if the target is suffering any condition (blinded, poisoned, frightened…).
- **Feint and Strike.** As a bonus action, make a dex check against your target's perception. On a success you have advantage on your next weapon attack against them this turn, and it deals an extra weapon damage die on a hit.
- **Hidden Blade.** You can draw a light weapon (dagger, stiletto) as part of any attack. Attacks with a light weapon the target didn't see you draw have advantage, and are critical hits if they land.
- **No Honour Among Knaves.** When your challenged target crits you, use your reaction to make it a normal hit and immediately attack them with advantage. Uses per short rest equal to your Dueling Bonus.

## Mage Slayer

This duelist has dedicated their life to hunting those who wield arcane power, learning the subtle tells of a spellcaster and moving fast enough to break their concentration and punish every utterance.

- **Arcane Hunter (Passive).** Advantage on saves against your challenged target's spells, and +2 to saves against their spell attacks.
- **Disruptive Strike.** When a creature within reach casts a spell, use your reaction to make a melee attack; on a hit it has disadvantage on concentration saves to maintain the spell.
- **Counterspell Parry.** As a reaction when your challenged target targets you with a spell, make a melee attack roll; on a pass, the spell has no effect on you.
- **Antimagic Dueling Field.** As an action, create an unstable null-magic zone for one minute: your challenged target has disadvantage on spell attacks, and anyone casting within 15 ft of you must pass a wisdom save or the spell fails and is wasted.
- **Silence the Arcane.** Once per combat, when you hit your challenged target, they make a wisdom save or can't cast spells above tier 1 for 1d4 rounds.

## Crowd Pleaser

This duelist fights for the roar of the crowd and the flash of coin — a performer first and a warrior second, using flamboyance, acrobatics and charisma to control the fight's narrative and win over onlookers.

- **Play to the Crowd (Passive).** Gain an Applause point whenever you bring your challenged target below half HP, score a crit, or similar. Spend 3 to give yourself or an ally within 30 ft advantage on one attack, check or save.
- **Dazzling Display.** As a bonus action, perform a dazzling feat; your challenged target makes a wisdom save or has disadvantage on their next attack against you.
- **Taunting Jeer.** As an action, mock your challenged target; on a failed charisma save they must use their next turn's movement and action to come at you with a melee attack, if possible.
- **Award-Winning Performance (Evolving).** If you've challenged the same target for 3+ rounds, gain temp HP equal to your Dueling Bonus at the start of your turn. After 5 rounds, +10 ft speed. After 10, your damage doubles.
- **Grand Finale.** Once per combat, make one weapon attack at disadvantage against your challenged target; if it hits it's an automatic crit. Hit or miss, the challenge ends.

## Opportunist

Opportunists are patient, analytical duelists who treat combat like chess — masters of defence who study an opponent for the weakness they can punish with one perfectly timed counter.

- **Patient Analysis (Passive).** At the end of each round your challenge is active, gain an Analysis point (max 5). Spend them for +1 each to attack or damage rolls against your challenged target.
- **Predictable Pattern.** When your challenged target misses you with a melee attack, counterattack for weapon damage + Dueling Bonus + (20 − INT score), without spending your reaction for the turn.
- **Perfect Parry.** As a reaction when your challenged target hits you in melee, make an opposed attack roll. If yours is higher, negate all damage and immediately attack them. If you fail, you take the damage plus a chance of being wounded/maimed.
- **Exploit Hubris.** If your challenged target is affected by an outside effect such as healing magic, use your reaction to move up to your speed toward them without provoking and make a weapon attack.
- **Ultimate Counter.** Once per combat, when your challenged target attacks you, strike first as a reaction. If you hit, their attack has disadvantage and deals half damage; if you crit, their attack automatically misses.

## Nemesis
sixth: yes

*The Worst Possible Opponent*

One on One Combat guarantees a duelist their fight; a nemesis makes that fight the last one their opponent will ever have. These duelists study rather than swing. The first exchange is reconnaissance, the second is confirmation, and by the third they are no longer fighting a swordsman — they are fighting this swordsman, whose guard drops a half-inch on the recovery and who always steps left off a feint.

Nemeses take contracts against named individuals and vanish for months at a time preparing for a single meeting. They are rarely the strongest fighter in a room. They are simply the worst possible opponent for whoever they came for.

- **Read.** Each round you spend engaged with the same enemy grants a Tell, to a maximum of 5. Tells are lost if you attack a different target or leave the duel. Each Tell grants +2 to all rolls against that enemy and -1 to their rolls against you.
- **Their Own Weapon.** At 2 Tells, you may declare an enemy's fighting style learned. Their trait, subclass abilities and special attacks function at half effect against you, and you may make melee rolls with any weapon they use at your own proficiency for the remainder of the fight.
- **Counter.** At 3 Tells, any enemy attack against you that misses may be answered immediately with a free attack. At 5 Tells, this triggers on their failed rolls of any kind, not only attacks.
- **Study the Absent.** Given a day and material to work from — a witness, a body they killed, a weapon, written accounts — you may begin a duel already holding 2 Tells against that named individual. This carries between sessions and persists until they die.
- **The Last Exchange (1/day).** At 5 Tells, declare the duel finished. Make a single attack that cannot be dodged, blocked, parried or prevented by any ability, and which ignores armour entirely. It deals your weapon damage plus 5d10, plus a further 2d10 if you have studied this enemy in advance. If this fails to kill them, you lose all Tells and cannot regain them against that enemy for the rest of the combat.

# Paladin
class: Paladin

- Gain 1d20 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **Channel Divinity.** Channel some of the power of the divinity you worship; the effect depends on your divinity's powers.
- **Righteous Shield.** Shield yourself and 1d6 allies from all attacks for one turn. Three uses a day.
- **Ascension.** On death there's a chance to revive as an angel if you have gathered enough divine favour. Divine favour is a hidden value — the more you have, the better your chance.

**Starting weapon — choose one.**
- **Hammer of Warning** (1d12): makes the party immune to surprise attacks and wakes the party from natural sleep during night attacks (not magical sleep).
- **Shield of the Moon:** you cannot be hit by an attack unless it does 20 damage in a turn. Once a day, inflict the sleep effect on an enemy.
- **Sword of Kelna** (1d10): this sword does not appear to have any additional effects.

## Oath of Redemption

Paladins of this oath feel they have wronged their god at some stage and wish to redeem themselves in their god's eyes. This redemption can take many different forms.

- **Redeem Yourself.** Once a day, revive a target making death saves back to full hp. They go down again at the end of combat or when their hp falls to 0.
- **Steel Yourself.** Gain temporary hp equal to half damage taken, lasting one turn.
- **Seal of Judgement.** Mark a target. All attacks against it have a chance to heal the attacker (1d4: 1–2 no healing, 3 heal half the damage, 4 heal the full damage).
- **Aura of Elemental Resistance.** Choose an element; all allies around you gain resistance to it.
- **Sense Undead.** Sense any nearby undead creatures.

## Oath of Glory

Glory paladins wish to amass glory in the eyes of their god and long to ascend as their god's right hand when they pass from their mortal form. It is rumoured some have become angels after death so they could keep serving their god.

- **Fight Me or Run.** Compel an enemy to roll to determine whether they flee combat or solely target you.
- **Iron Will.** Once a day, gain temporary hp equal to your maximum hp, lasting until the end of combat.
- **Hallowed Ground.** You and all nearby allies are immune to all non-magic attacks for one turn.
- **Blade of Truth.** Upon striking an enemy, they make a wisdom save; on a fail they must answer a question truthfully. 5 times a day.
- **Timed Strike.** Once per combat, deal Xd6 additional damage when you strike an enemy this turn (X = number of turns in combat).

## Oath of Reaping

Reaping paladins believe the best way for a soul to be judged is by the divine, so they will not hesitate to cut down any they deem in need of judgement. In the past this has corrupted some paladins, who went on rampages cutting down innocents so they could be with their gods.

- **To the Gods with You.** When you land the final blow on an enemy, gain +1 max hp.
- **Godly Might.** 5 times a day, imbue your weapon with your god's power for an additional 2d6 damage.
- **Stunning Strike.** 3 times a day, imbue your weapon with a stun; stunned targets cannot act until the end of your next turn.
- **Cleanse the Unclean.** Advantage on all rolls against undead creatures.
- **God's Executioner.** Once a day, let your god guide your hand for a guaranteed nat 20 on a combat roll.

## Oath of Mercy

Mercy paladins try to keep those around them safe and healthy through small amounts of divine magic, and by using their bodies as shields when needed. They make excellent bodyguards, caring little for the damage they take while protecting another.

- **Divine Mass Healing Word.** Once a day, heal all nearby allies 6d4 hp.
- **Healing Presence.** As a bonus action, grant a downed party member a pass on their death saving throws.
- **Pain Is Temporary.** When protecting an ally, all damage you take for them is halved.
- **Respite.** Enter a deep meditative state in which you cannot be attacked or attack. Nearby allies can spend their actions to join you. While in this state your divinity may contact you.
- **Suffering Unleashed.** Deal additional damage on your next attack equal to your healing done this combat. 3 uses a day.

## Oath of Silence

Silence paladins are far less common now due to the rapid decline in mages. They use their powers to silence the abilities of mages, making them highly sought after in prisons for magic users, or for tasks where a magic user must be captured alive.

- **Divine Counterspell.** Twice a day, counterspell a spell; the counterspell's tier is 1d6 + 3.
- **Anti-magic Field.** You emanate a weak anti-magic field; your party rolls wisdom saves with advantage.
- **Absorb and Release.** Once per day, absorb a cast spell (you still take its damage/effects), then recast it at a chosen target.
- **Cure Wounds.** Remove all negative effects caused by magic from a target.
- **Silence.** Upon striking a magic user, they roll a 1d2; on a 1 they lose a spell slot of a random tier.

## Oath of Crucible
sixth: yes

- **Crucible Form.** Three times per day, enter Crucible Form for 2 turns. All damage you take is reduced to 1 per hit, and any damage reduced this way is stored. When the form ends, release the stored total as radiant damage to a chosen enemy.
- **Aegis of the Doomed.** Choose up to 1d4 allies. For 3 turns they can't drop below 1 HP. When it ends, you take half of the total damage they avoided; if this would kill you, you instead drop to 1 HP and fall unconscious at the end of combat. Once per day.
- **Martyr's Rebuke.** When any ally within 20 ft is hit, you may take the hit instead as a reaction, then make an immediate weapon attack against the attacker. Unlimited uses.
- **Soul-Chain.** Select a hostile creature. For 3 turns, all damage it deals is halved and the other half is dealt to you instead. If you die while Soul-Chain is active, that creature immediately takes damage equal to your maximum HP. Once per day.
- **Grave Denial.** As a reaction, choose a creature that has dropped to 0 HP; it drops to 1 HP instead and gains 2d6 temp HP, and you take 2d6 damage. Five uses per day.

# Monk
class: Monk

- Gain 1d20 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **Stances.** Subclassed monks gain a variety of stances, each with an active and a passive ability. Switching stance is a bonus action. Every monk has these two, plus their subclass's stance:
  - **Meditative Stance** — Active: heal 1d4 if your HP is above 0. Passive: −5 to damage from all magic sources.
  - **Focused Stance** — Active: for the next round, you can catch any arrows and bolts shot at you and throw them back at targets of your choice. Passive: advantage on perception rolls.

## Way of Water

Monks of the Way of Water know flow, not force, is the ultimate power. They move with fluid grace, redirecting their foes' energy and using ki to mend their allies — the adaptable heart of any party, a relentless tide in combat.

- **Fluid Motion (Passive).** Difficult terrain never impedes you. When an enemy's melee attack misses you, use your reaction to make an opportunity attack. Arrows that miss you can be thrown back at twice the damage while in this stance.
- **Stance of the Flowing River.** Active: as an action, surround an ally within 30 ft with a swirling vortex; the next attack that would hit them misses (ends after 1 attack or at the end of your next turn). Passive: +2 defence.
- **Crashing Wave Technique.** Once per combat, attack every creature in a 15 ft cone in front of you; those hit take your staff damage and are pushed back 10 ft.
- **Healing Spring.** Three times per day, as an action, touch a creature and roll your 1d20 hit die; they heal that amount + your level.
- **Tidal Surge (Evolving).** When Divine Protection lets you act at 0 HP, you instead enter pure fluidity for 1d4 rounds (+1 per previous use, max +5): immune to all damage and control effects, but healing only has a 1d4 chance to work on you. If you haven't been healed when it ends, begin death saves as normal.

## Way of Fire

These monks stoke their inner ki into a raging inferno — explosive, aggressive combatants who overwhelm foes with relentless searing attacks. Their philosophy is simple: the fastest way to end a conflict is to reduce your opponent to ash.

- **Burning Ki (Passive).** Unarmed strikes and staff attacks deal +1d4 fire. On a crit the target catches fire, taking 1d4 fire at the start of their turn for 3 rounds.
- **Stance of the Cinder Dance.** Active: wreathe yourself in harmless flames for 1 minute; creatures ending their turn within 5 ft of you take 1d6 fire. Passive: +10 ft movement.
- **Searing Palm Strike.** As an action, make a melee attack; on a hit the target makes a wisdom save or is Seared for 1 minute — vulnerable to fire (resistance becomes neutral; immunity unaffected) and at disadvantage on concentration checks.
- **Phoenix Flash.** Once per short rest, move up to your speed in a straight line without provoking, passing through enemy spaces; every enemy you pass through or beside takes 1d8 fire.
- **Supernova.** Once per combat, when reduced to 0 HP, consume your Divine Protection turn to explode: all creatures within 30 ft make a dex save, taking 10d6 fire (half on a success). You automatically take 2 death save failures.

## Way of Earth

Unshakable and immovable, earth monks are the foundation victory is built on — fortifying their bodies to stone and shaping the battlefield into cover for allies and obstacles for enemies.

- **Rooted Like a Mountain (Passive).** Advantage on checks to avoid being moved, knocked prone, grappled, etc. You can use your reaction to grant this to an ally within 10 ft.
- **Stance of the Unmoving Mountain.** Active: become immovable until the start of your next turn; the next single attack against you deals 0. Passive: gain temp HP equal to your level at the start of each combat.
- **Earthen Grasp.** As an action, a stony hand erupts within 60 ft; a medium or smaller creature makes a strength save or is grappled and restrained. Maintain it as a bonus action on later turns.
- **Fortify the Weakest Point.** Three times per day, touch a non-magical object no larger than a door (a lock, a weakened wall, an ally's shield). For an hour its min hit and HP are massively boosted, or a character behind it gains an extra level of cover.
- **Final Bulwark.** When an ally within 30 ft would drop to 0 HP from an attack, use your reaction to swap places and take the attack. If it drops you to 0, you immediately benefit from Divine Protection, even if already used this combat.

## Way of Air

Masters of wind and thought, these monks believe in freedom of movement and perception — elusive scouts and strategists who use the air to avoid danger and gather information, often ending conflicts before a blow is struck.

- **Gossamer Step (Passive).** You are always under a slow fall effect and can jump three times your normal distance.
- **Stance of the Zephyr.** Active: become invisible for 1 round in a swirling, distorting breeze. Passive: ranged attacks against you have disadvantage, and marksmen can miss you as normal archers can.
- **Wind Tunnel.** As an action, blast wind in a 60 ft × 10 ft line. Creatures in it make a strength check: on a fail they take 2d10, are pushed back 20 ft and knocked prone; on a success, half damage only.
- **Eyes of the Hurricane.** As an action, create an invisible sensor within 120 ft for 10 minutes that you see and hear through; move it up to 30 ft per round as a bonus action.
- **One with the Sky.** Once per long rest, as a bonus action, become gaseous for up to 1 hour: fly at walking speed, share spaces with creatures and slip through small gaps. You can't attack, and can't be attacked by non-magical means.

## Way of Tranquility

These monks are the ultimate pacifists and healers, believing true strength lies in preserving life. They channel ki into restorative and protective energy, shielding allies and mending even grievous wounds.

- **Aura of Peace (Passive).** You and allies within 10 ft resist psychic damage and have advantage on saves against being charmed or frightened.
- **Stance of the Healing Heart.** Active: as a bonus action, touch a creature to restore 1d8 HP. Passive: healing you receive or provide is increased by your level.
- **Ki Barrier.** As a reaction when you or an ally within 30 ft is hit, grant temp HP equal to your monk level + 20 before the damage is applied.
- **Sanctuary of the Soul.** Once per short rest, sanctify a 15 ft area for 1 minute. Allies starting their turn there remove one level of exhaustion or one non-magical disease; hostile creatures can't willingly enter.
- **Ultimate Sacrifice.** When Divine Protection lets you act at 0 HP, you may forgo your turn: all allies within 60 ft heal your 1d20 hit die + your max HP and lose all negative status effects. You then die immediately, ignoring death saves — reversible only by a Wish or divine intervention.

## Way of the Storm
sixth: yes

*The Turning Discipline*

The four elemental Ways each teach a discipline; Tranquility teaches the stillness between them. The Storm teaches that stillness is a lie — that water becomes cloud, cloud becomes wind, wind strikes stone, and stone is worn away, and the monk who learns to move between them without pausing is not practising four arts but one.

It is taught in very few monasteries and completed in fewer. A monk of the Storm cannot repeat themselves; the moment they do, the movement stops and they are simply a man swinging a staff again. Everything about the discipline is built to keep them turning.

- **Cycle.** At the start of each of your turns, name an element: Water, Fire, Earth or Air. You gain that Way's core benefit for the turn and may use one ability from its list. You cannot name the same element twice in a row, and repeating an element you used the previous turn ends the Cycle entirely for 1d3 turns.
- **Confluence.** Each consecutive turn spent in Cycle without repeating grants a stacking +2 to all rolls and +1d6 damage, to a maximum of +8 and +4d6. Lost entirely the moment the Cycle breaks.
- **Elemental Body.** While in Cycle you have resistance to the element you named this turn and to the one you named last turn. At 4 stacks of Confluence or higher, this becomes immunity.
- **Between Breaths.** Changing element is a free action taken at any point in your turn rather than only at the start, allowing you to open with one element and finish with another. The second element's benefit applies at half effect and still counts as used for repetition purposes.
- **The Storm Entire (1/day).** At maximum Confluence, hold all four at once for 1d3+1 turns. You gain every Way's core benefit simultaneously, may use one ability from each list per turn, and are immune to all four elements. Repetition rules are suspended. When it ends you lose all Confluence, cannot enter Cycle for 1d4 turns, and Divine Protection is unavailable for the remainder of combat.

# Rogue
class: Rogue

- Gain 1d10 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **Glancing Blow.** Once per combat, take half damage from any attack made against you, including attacks you failed to dodge.
- **Two-handed Combat Proficiency.** You can dual wield your one-handed weapons, making two attacks a turn, one with each weapon.

**Starting gear — choose one.**
Weapons: Duelling hand axes x2 (1d10 each — if both hit, apply bleed) · Duelling knives x2 (1d12 each) · Darksteel short axe (1d20).
Or equipment: Lockpicks · Tattoo granting dark vision (see in darkness as though it were bright) · Boots of light fall.

## Natural Assassin

A natural assassin's blade is an extension of them — it's not uncommon for them to name their weapons. They have great skill at duelling and picking off targets, and are hired to take out high-value targets such as rogue dukes, enemy commanders and rulers of other nations. An uncommon subclass, due to how hard it is to obtain and the risk of the jobs that follow.

- **Dodging Strike.** When you pass a dodge roll, you may make a retaliation attack.
- **Strike Fast.** On the first turn of combat, you may attack all enemies, gaining 1 additional disadvantage per extra target (target 3 is 2 disadvantage, target 4 is 3, etc).
- **Disarming Strike.** Make an attack for half damage; if the opponent fails a strength roll they drop what they are holding.
- **Backstab.** When a foe is fighting someone else (and focused on them or unable to see you), all your strikes deal +5 and crit on 15 or higher.
- **Cloak and Dagger.** Conceal a blade with a dex roll, letting you smuggle one into places you shouldn't have one, such as a throne room or place of worship. You always have access to a 1d12 blade somewhere on your person.

## Poisoner

Poisoners eliminate targets through less direct methods than the natural assassin, using elemental imbuing potions to strike with elemental effects and poisons.

- **Poison Expertise.** Craft poisons to cause specific effects; more complex effects need rarer ingredients. Gather the ingredients and pass an intelligence roll. Each batch can poison 1d4 targets.
- **Always Prepared.** You always carry an antidote to any poisons or elemental effects you carry, and cures for all common status effects.
- **Elemental Tonic.** You always have an elemental tonic to imbue your weapon with; choose the element when you take this subclass (tier 1 and 2 elements only).
- **Contract Killer.** You can find high-risk, high-reward work from wealthy patrons, and have contacts in most major cities — where there is money there is a need for a poisoner.
- **Enhancing Brew.** You carry a small dose of a complex brew: advantage on all rolls for 3 turns (or 30 in-game minutes), heal 1d10, a second dodge action, and an automatic pass on going into the shadows. When it wears off you fall asleep for an hour — or, if your hp is below 15, enter death saves.

## Wraith

Wraiths focus on moving unseen. They are skilled spies and great scouts, eavesdropping and gathering secrets to blackmail people or trade for items, gold or other secrets.

- **Move Unseen.** You move in the shadows; to spot you a person must pass a disadvantage perception roll. You may place yourself anywhere in the initiative order (except in a surprise round). You start all combats in the shadows, and returning to them passes on a 3 or 4 instead of just a 4.
- **Fear the Dark.** Once you've attacked from the shadows in a combat, the victim saves against INT; on a fail they have disadvantage on all rolls while they can't see you. Out of combat, you may inflict terror (they try to flee), saved against INT or WIS — whichever is worse.
- **We Move Silently.** Mark a target, who gains your Move Unseen passive until they are seen or make an attack roll.
- **Surprise Strike.** Attacks from the shadows always cause their elemental effect and deal double damage.
- **Vanish.** Upon passing a dodge roll, you may slip back into the shadows.

## Master Thief

These rogues enjoy stealing — to them it's a game, not a crime, and they'll try to steal anything that isn't nailed down. Sometimes they steal an item and return it to its owner just to prove they could.

- **Thief.** Attempt to steal an item; the roll must beat your dex and the target's passive perception. Out of combat and in the shadows, you have advantage.
- **Don't Mind If I Do.** When you successfully strike a target in combat, you may attempt to steal any object on their person that isn't in their hands.
- **No Hard Feelings.** After stealing an item and returning it, you gain advantage on charisma with the person you robbed.
- **Street Smarts.** You can find obscure entrances and exits to structures with a perception roll. You and nearby party members cannot be pickpocketed.
- **Planned Exit.** Years of theft taught you to always have an escape plan. In combat, the entire party can escape using your disengage stat, and all can disengage as a bonus action.

## Showman

The showman hides in plain sight rather than in the shadows, taking the guise of bards, acrobats, jesters and the like to gather information, find lodging and enter places they have no right to be.

- **Disguise.** Disguise yourself as a different class/occupation (bard, acrobat, jester, etc). While in disguise and not attacked, you can travel under that guise, moving sneakily and accessing restricted areas.
- **Expert Acrobat.** If targeted by a spell and you pass the wisdom save, you take no damage (mind-targeting spells excepted); on a fail you take half.
- **Musician.** You can play an instrument of your choice, received on taking this subclass. Your music can charm a single target for as long as you play.
- **Flamboyant Flourish.** You push the boundaries of what combat is: enemies have disadvantage on dodge rolls against your attacks, and the spectacle forces any new combatants to make a charisma save before entering combat.
- **Nimble.** Dodge up to four attacks a turn. After a successful dodge you may move up or down the initiative order one place.

## Nameless
sixth: yes

*Unaccounted For*

The other rogues take a life and leave. A nameless takes the life and then takes the fact of it — the memory of the face at the window, the name on the contract, the guard who could have described them, the reason anyone would think to look. They are not hidden; they are unaccounted for.

The oldest of them have given up so much of themselves in the process that they no longer remember what they were called before they started, and a few no longer cast a reflection worth recognising. The Academy holds no records on them. It is not certain the records were ever written.

- **Unaccounted For.** From the Shadows triggers on a 3 or 4 rather than only a 4, and you may enter the shadows freely as a bonus action once per combat. Any enemy who loses sight of you must pass a wisdom save to remember they were pursuing anyone at all.
- **No Witnesses.** Any non-boss enemy who sees you kill and survives the encounter recalls the killing but not the killer. Descriptions, sketches, magical divination and truth-compelled testimony all come back blank. Bosses and named NPCs get a wisdom save to retain the memory.
- **Nobody In Particular.** You may walk into any place a plain, forgettable person could plausibly be — a servant, a guard on shift, a mourner, a courier — with no disguise, roll or preparation. The role holds until you do something the role would not do. Magical detection of identity, alignment or intent returns nothing.
- **Spend the Name.** You carry 3 Names, restored on a long rest. Spend one to erase a single fact about yourself from the world: a debt, a bounty, a sworn oath, a witness's certainty, a lock keyed to your blood, a mark laid on you by an enemy. The fact simply was never true. You also lose something you had because of it — GM's choice, and it is permanent.
- **Never Was (1/day).** Remove yourself from a scene for 1d4+2 turns. You cannot be perceived, targeted, tracked, divined or remembered while it lasts, you move at double speed, and every attack you make from this state is an automatic critical hit. Enemies killed during it are found later with no cause of death anyone can establish. When it ends, you are somewhere you were not, and the people you left behind cannot say when you were last there.

# Reaper
class: Reaper

- Gain 1d10 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **Pure Ethereal Form.** Once per combat, you are immune to all non-magic attacks while in ethereal form, and it now lasts 5 + 1d3 turns.

## Shadow Reaper

Shadow reapers exist in a near-constant ethereal state, letting them shake off damage and move swiftly. Sending a soul to its grave sustains them a while longer, and they debuff foes for easier kills.

- **You Don't Need This Anymore (Passive).** Heal 1d6 every time you slay a foe.
- **Ethereal Scabbards (Passive).** Your weapons are stored ethereally — invisible until drawn, appearing instantly in your hands, and vanishable whenever you like.
- **Truly Ethereal.** Once per day, take only one damage from each source of damage for 1d2 turns.
- **Hexing Strike.** Three times a day, make all your damage apply a damage-reducing hex for 1d6 turns.

## Soul Ripper

A soul ripper targets the soul rather than the body, able to kill without leaving a single mark. They are often employed to hunt heavily armoured threats, and hold on to fragments of their victims' souls for greater strength.

- **Lend Me Your Strength (Passive).** Take an ability score from a target you slay. Only one stolen stat at a time; it lasts until you release the soul or take a new fragment.
- **Non-material Blades (Passive).** Switch your blades to a non-physical form that strikes the soul, ignoring armour and resistances completely.
- **Knowledge from the Grave.** While a victim is in death saves, execute them and ask one question they must answer honestly.
- **Separating Strike.** Once per day, strike to temporarily (1d4) separate a target's soul from their body. The soul can be targeted by other reapers, magical weapons or spells.
- **Severing Strikes.** Attacks on non-boss enemies have a 1 in 1d100 chance to separate body and soul completely. Once a day you may raise the odds to 1d20.

## Spectre

Spectres move quickly and silently, gathering intel and assassinating targets without leaving a trace. They often work with rogues, reaching areas a rogue cannot and opening new entrances for them. They are rarely spotted — and few who do spot one live to tell of it.

- **Ghostly Form (Passive).** Ethereal form lasts 2 extra turns, lets you pass through any non-magic barrier, and leaves zero trace of your passing.
- **Silent Movement.** Three times a day, mark a target who can travel silently and traceless for one hour.
- **You Never Saw Me.** With a charisma check, convince a target they never saw you; on a pass, you are removed from their memory.
- **Possessing Marks.** Once a day, mark a target by touch and perceive through their senses.
- **Life Draining Strikes.** Three times a day, gain 100% lifesteal on your strikes.

## God's Hand

A god's hand reaper takes a spiritual approach to releasing souls back to the god or entity they belong to, usually aligning with a god that suits their approach.

- **Divine Guidance (Passive).** Ask the entity you worship for guidance or advice. It may help, or be unable to, depending on the entity and the request.
- **Empowered Strikes.** Once per combat, your god imbues your weapons with extra damage, status or other effects for 5 turns.
- **Wanted by the Gods.** Receive contracts to kill on behalf of your god or others. Your damage to the target gains +5 while hunting them.
- **Channel Divinity.** Twice per day, spend a turn channelling your divinity to buff, heal or affect your party, or debuff your foes.
- **I Choose Your Fate.** When you land the final blow, choose whether the target goes to their divinity, yours or a random one.

## Way of Gentle Passing

Reapers of gentle passing prefer their targets to slip painlessly into the afterlife, dispatching them quickly and quietly. They learn about their targets and try to make death less frightening.

- **Blissful Ignorance (Passive).** Targets unaware of your presence are automatically crit if you pass a melee roll.
- **Rest Now.** Twice a day, touch a target to make them drowsy; they soon fall asleep unless attacked or otherwise jolted by adrenaline.
- **Poison Understanding.** Poisons you use have double effect; poisons used on you have half effect.
- **It's Your Time to Go Now.** Once per day, with an advantage charisma roll, convince a non-boss, non-PC target to allow themselves to be reaped by your hand.

## Deathless
sixth: yes

*The Overdue*

Ethereal Form is a loan. Every reaper knows it: five turns of walking half in the next world, and then back to a body with 10 hp and a 1d6 hit die and every reason to be careful. The deathless stopped paying it back. Somewhere in the count of souls they have carried across, the thing that comes for reapers lost track of which one they were — and a reaper who cannot be collected is a reaper who can afford to be reckless. They are not immortal. They are overdue, and they know exactly how much of the debt they have left before it is called in.

- **Overdue.** You carry a Ledger, starting at 0. When you would be reduced to 0 HP, you may instead add 1d4 to your Ledger and remain standing at 1 HP, taking an immediate additional turn. Death saves do not apply to you while your Ledger is below 10.

- **Borrowed Time.** Ethereal Form may be entered at will as a bonus action, any number of times per combat. Each activation after the first adds 1 to your Ledger. While Ethereal, you may also pass through creatures as well as walls, striking as you go for half damage.

At a Ledger of 10 or higher, the debt is called in. GM's discretion on what arrives, but it should be a scene rather than a death — something walks in mid-encounter and wants a word. Clearing the Ledger back below 10 should require paying something real.

## Final Verse
sixth: yes

*The Song That Takes the Lead*

Every blade singer carries steel that listens. The final verse is what happens when the singer stops leading and the song takes the lead — when every blade within reach, carried or not, wakes to a piece the singer never wrote and cannot end.

Weapons lift off the dead. Arms rise from racks and scabbards and the hands of enemies still holding them. By the last movement there is no singer at the centre of it, only a shape that steel has agreed to move around. Those who have performed one say the same thing afterward: they remember the opening note and nothing else, and they do not know how the fight was won.

- **Movements.** The verse has four Movements, advancing one per turn. Begin it as a free action. It ends only when you choose, or when you die, and death does not necessarily stop it.
- **First — Prelude.** Every weapon you carry acts on your turn at full damage, and you take your own attack action as normal.
- **Second — Rising.** Your weapons act twice each and may strike anything within 30m of you, moving freely through the air. Any blade lying loose on the field within that range joins the verse.
- **Third — Crescendo.** Every weapon in the verse acts three times. Enemies within 30m have disadvantage on all rolls, and any armed enemy who ends their turn within range must pass a wisdom save or their own weapon turns and strikes them once.
- **Fourth — Final Verse.** There is no longer a limit on the steel answering you. Every weapon in the verse acts three times, you act three times, and any blade that fells a foe immediately acts again — with no cap on how far that chains.
- **Sung Steel.** Nothing takes a weapon from you. Disarming, sundering, dispelling, binding, magical seizure and antimagic all fail outright while a verse plays. Any attempt to grapple, restrain or hold you is answered by every blade in range at once, automatically, before the attempt resolves.
- **Chorus.** Any weapon in the verse may Defend instead of attacking — granting an ally within 30m advantage on dodge, intercepting an incoming attack entirely, or destroying an arrow. Weapons defending an ally who would drop to 0 HP take the blow in their place.
- **Answer the Song.** Enemy weapons are not exempt. On reaching the Third Movement, any armed non-boss enemy within 30m rolls a wisdom save each turn; on a failure their weapon leaves them and joins your verse for the remainder of the performance. Bosses save at advantage.
- **The Last Note.** End the verse from the Fourth Movement. Every blade answering you converges on a single point at once. The target and everything adjacent to it takes maximum damage from every weapon in the verse simultaneously, no roll, no save, ignoring armour, cover and immunity to physical damage. If you are dead when this resolves, it resolves anyway, and the steel falls still afterwards.

# Cavalier
class: Cavalier

- Gain 1d12 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **Lance & Charge.** Moving at least 15 ft in a straight line before a melee attack adds +2d10 to it; on a hit, you and your mount do not provoke opportunity attacks for the rest of the turn.
- **As One.** You and your mount each take a full turn (action, move and bonus action) on your initiative. You cannot be unseated against your will except by death or magic, and you may take any saving throw your mount makes (or it takes yours), choosing the better.

*Charging is a mounted rule, not exclusive to cavaliers: spending all your mount's movement charging an enemy grants 1d20 bonus damage.*

## Lancer

The lancer is the hammer of the cavalry charge — a single thunderous impact that shatters lines and unhorses champions. Everything they do builds toward the next devastating run.

- **Momentum (Passive).** Each consecutive turn you charge adds +10 damage (stacking up to +50), and you cannot be slowed or stopped while a charge is building.
- **Devastating Charge.** Your charge bonus dice become 2d20, and a charged hit knocks the target back 15 ft and prone (STR save at disadvantage).
- **Couched Lance.** A straight-line charge hits every enemy you pass at full damage; each one struck adds +1d10 to the next.
- **Unhorse.** A charged hit on a mounted, large or huge foe topples them automatically (no save) and deals +3d8; they spend their next turn rising.
- **Tilt (3/combat).** A called charge that, on a hit, runs the lance clean through — 5d12, the target is impaled (held, 2d8/turn) until they pull free, and anyone behind them takes 3d8.

## Dragoon

Dragoons ride mounts that take to the sky — wyverns, great rocs, stranger things. They strike from above and are gone before the dust settles.

- **Winged Mount.** Your mount flies at full speed. You gain altitude freely, ignore all ground terrain and hazards, and enemies without flight or reach cannot make opportunity attacks against you.
- **Dive Bomb.** Descending at least 15 ft onto a target adds 3d10 to the attack, knocks it prone, and may stun adjacent foes for a turn.
- **Strafing Run.** Fly over a line of enemies and attack each one beneath you at full damage, up to 3 foes.
- **Hard to Pin.** While airborne you have advantage on dodge, take half damage from ranged attacks, and the first attack against you each turn automatically misses.
- **Skyfall (3/combat).** Drop in a full dive — 5d12 to the target and 3d6 to everything within 15 ft, all knocked prone. You land safely and may immediately attack again.

## Outrider

Outriders never sit still. They harry the enemy's flanks, strike and wheel away before a blow can land, and run down anything that tries to flee.

- **Harrying Pass (Passive).** Enemies you attacked this turn have disadvantage on all rolls targeting you or your mount until your next turn.
- **Hit and Run.** You and your mount may move both before and after attacking without provoking attacks of opportunity.
- **Wheeling Strike.** After any melee hit, immediately move up to your full speed for free and gain +1d8 on your next attack this turn.
- **Run Them Down.** Advantage and +2d8 against fleeing, slower or isolated targets; a kill lets you keep moving and attack again.
- **Ride Circles (3/combat).** Circle a target; for 1d3 turns they cannot track you — your attacks against them auto-hit and crit on 17+, and they attack you at disadvantage.

## Banneret

The banneret rides at the head of the charge with the colours flying. Where they ride, allies fight harder, stand firmer and rally to the banner.

- **Rally to the Banner.** Allies within 30 ft gain +4 to attacks and saves and 1d8 temp HP each turn; the radius moves with you.
- **Inspiring Charge.** When you charge, every ally who charges the same turn gains your full charge bonus dice and cannot be knocked prone.
- **Hold the Line.** Allies adjacent to you or your mount gain +5 defence, cannot be made to flee, and reflect 1d6 to anyone who strikes them.
- **Reposition.** As a bonus action, ride past any number of allies to grant each an immediate free move and a free dodge.
- **Heroic Surge (1/combat).** Raise the banner — for 1d4 turns all allies who can see it gain an extra action each turn, immunity to fear, and advantage on everything.

## Beast Rider

Beast riders bond with something never meant to be ridden — a great boar, a moon bear, a dire cat, a young drake. Mount and rider are equal partners in the carnage.

- **Monstrous Mount.** Your mount is a huge beast with its own attacks (3d8) and two natural abilities (gore, pounce, breath, etc) chosen when you take this subclass.
- **Tandem Maul.** You and your mount may both attack the same target; if both hit, it is grappled or pinned and takes +2d8.
- **Feral Bond.** Your mount acts on its own initiative in addition to yours, effectively doubling your turns, and shares your buffs.
- **Thick Hide.** Your mount gains three random monstrous traits — for example +40 HP, resistance to all damage, and 2d6 regeneration each turn. You share these while riding.
- **Unleash (3/combat).** Loose your mount's signature ability, empowered: the breath, pounce or gore deals 4d10 in its area and applies its status to all caught, and the mount may then act again.

## Warhost
sixth: yes

*The One the Herd Follows*

A cavalier bonds one mount. The warhost bonded one, buried it, bonded another, buried that one too, and somewhere in the years of it stopped being a rider with a horse and became something the herd itself follows. They do not arrive; they are heard arriving.

A warhost fights at the head of a line of beasts that answer them without being trained to, and the ones that fall are replaced by morning — not because the warhost seeks them out, but because the herd knows where to go. Cavalry commanders have been known to lose an entire wing of their own horse to a single warhost riding past.

- **The Herd Answers.** You have three bonded mounts rather than one, all sharing your initiative and levelling with you. Two act as riderless cavalry under your command while you ride the third; you may switch which you ride as a free action, moving between them at any distance you could ride. Mounts that fall are replaced over a long rest, always.
- **Wall of Horse.** Lance & Charge applies to every mount in your host, whether you are riding it or not. A charge made by two or more of your mounts in the same turn against the same line knocks every enemy in that line prone on a failed strength save and breaks their formation, ending any adjacency or leadership bonuses they hold.
- **Ride Them Down.** Any enemy that flees, disengages, retreats or is knocked prone within reach of your host is trampled automatically for full mount damage. No roll, no action spent, no limit per turn.
- **Call the Wild Ones (1/combat).** Call to any beasts within earshot — enemy mounts, pack animals, wild herds, a Beastmaster's tames. Each rolls a wisdom save at disadvantage if it is being ridden; those that fail throw their riders and join your host for the remainder of the encounter, acting as riderless cavalry under your command.
- **Warhost (1/day).** The herd arrives. For 1d4+2 turns you are joined by 2d6 additional bonded mounts, each acting on your initiative with full charge bonuses. Enemies caught between them cannot take reactions, cannot hold ground, and must save each turn or be scattered from their positions. When it ends the extra mounts are gone — not dead, simply gone — and you keep your original three.

# Ranger
class: Ranger

- **Clean Shot.** All arrows have a 50% chance to be recovered after combat, except those with a consumable part (you can recover armour-piercing arrows, not toxic gas arrows).
- **Greater Hail of Arrows.** Shoot 2 + 1d4 arrows in one turn.

## Vanguard Hunter

In times of war, vanguard hunters are tasked with eliminating enemy scouts and the enemy vanguard. They can rapidly slay single targets with a hail of arrows or attack many targets at once — deadly in groups, but more than capable alone.

## Stalker

Often confused with rogues, as both prefer to see without being seen — but stalkers use it to surprise a foe and dispatch them in a hail of arrows before they can respond. If the fight lasts longer, they have tools to escape or clean up stragglers.

- **Ambusher.** With surprise, or when attacking from somewhere you can see your foe but they can't see you, you automatically shoot your max Hail of Arrows.
- **Escape Plan.** Before combat, locate the optimal escape path from the fight or ambush you're staging, giving you and your party advantage on disengage.
- **Still as a Rock.** After 2+ turns without moving, you blend into the landscape and are harder to spot, until you take an action.
- **Mental Conditioning.** Immune to all stunning, blinding and confusing effects.
- **Execution's Shot.** Spend half your Greater Hail of Arrows value (declared before rolling) for more powerful arrows, then roll 1d4: 1 = the effect of a nat 1, 2–3 = a normal shot, 4 = the effect of a nat 20.

## Archer of the Wind

Archers of the wind can fight foes up close or in confined spaces where other archers struggle — so adept that they can be found on the front lines, dodging blade strikes while loosing arrows.

- **Quick Release.** When you dodge an attack, make an attack at 50% of your normal single-shot damage.
- **Acrobat.** Your dodge training gives you a greater understanding of acrobatics: you can safely fall from greater heights and traverse difficult terrain with ease.
- **Surprise Volley.** Five times per day, loose 1d3 arrows at the end of an acrobatic sequence that disguises the action, bypassing certain anti-archer effects such as a monk's stance.
- **Crippling Shots.** Target vital points to non-lethally render foes unable to fight, dispatching them quickly with fewer shots — and they won't be dead.
- **Like the Wind.** Convert any number of Hail of Arrows shots into dodge actions.

## Wanderer

The wanderer is an explorer at heart, often found travelling the world atop a steed. In conflict they make formidable mounted archers, firing at breakneck speed, and grow a deep, wordless connection with their steed.

- **Steed.** All wanderers are equipped with a steed — a horse as standard, other options on request.
- **Sure Footed.** You and your mount have advantage on all dex rolls while you are mounted.
- **Kite.** While mounted, you and your mount can stay at the edge of an opponent's attack range unless they match your mount's speed or you are surrounded, engaging foes safely.
- **Master Forager.** In the wilds you can forage useful plant and animal materials to create basic salves, clothing and tools.
- **Master of Tongues.** You gain a language of your choice and can learn new languages within weeks, given a teacher.

## Beast Slayer

Bear, troll or dragon — beast slayers are always eager for the next great hunt. They'll work in groups, but the greatest honour goes to whoever lands the final blow. They are the ranged counterpart to slayers, which leads to intense competition between the two. Some see beast slayers as uncivilised — nearly as wild as the beasts they hunt.

- **Slayer's Mark.** Twice a day, select a target: reroll any 1s or 2s for damage against it, and it takes an additional 5 damage at the end of your turn if you attacked it.
- **Trained Hunter.** Quickly and efficiently skin and harvest resources from dead creatures.
- **Wild Gaze.** Intimidate a target with merely your gaze, potentially causing them to flee.
- **Chain Shot.** Three times per day, fire your normal Hail of Arrows for 0 damage; when the last arrow lands, magical chains join them all — to ensnare beasts, or form a chain to climb unclimbable surfaces.
- **Hunt Trophy.** Land the final blow on a creature to create a trophy — a new quiver, bow, cloak, etc — that aids you on future hunts.

## Deluge
sixth: yes

*The Sky Falls*

Deluge rangers abandon the idea of the aimed shot entirely. Where a marksman spends a minute on one arrow, a deluge ranger puts twelve in the air in the same span and cares only where they land collectively. Individually the shots are barely worth the shaft; together they decide where an enemy is permitted to stand. Veterans of siege lines and border skirmishes speak of the sky going dark and of men lying flat in the mud for an hour rather than risk crossing thirty feet of open ground.

- **Suppression.** Hail of Arrows fires 1d3+1 times per turn instead of 1d3, with all shots after the first dealing 25% damage rather than 50%. Every arrow that hits applies a Pin stack, regardless of damage dealt. Pin stacks last until the end of the target's next turn and refresh on each new hit.
- **Pinned.** A target's Pin stacks apply cumulatively — 2 stacks: disadvantage on ranged rolls and any roll requiring a steady hand. 4 stacks: movement halved, cannot dash or charge. 6 stacks: must pass a wisdom save to take any action other than seeking cover or retreating.
- **Broken Cover.** Any pinned enemy that leaves cover, stands from prone, or moves in a straight line toward you takes an immediate free shot at full damage. This shot applies a Pin stack as normal.
- **Ranging Shots.** You may split a single Hail of Arrows between two targets within 10m of one another. Arrows that miss still apply a Pin stack, as they land close enough to matter.
- **Spend the Volley.** Once per combat per target, spend a pinned enemy's accumulated stacks for an effect rather than the passive penalties: 4 stacks to disarm or force prone, 6 stacks to strip an active buff or deny their next reaction.
- **Blot Out The Sun (1/day).** Sustain a barrage over an area for 1d4 turns. Every enemy within it gains a Pin stack at the start of their turn whether you shoot them or not, and you may still take your own turn normally. Requires open sky; unusable indoors, in caves or under heavy canopy.

### Strengths

Answers armoured and elite foes without needing raw damage; shuts down mobile and charging enemies, traditionally the ranger's worst matchup; forces enemies to fight on the party's terms.

### Weaknesses

Damage per shot drops noticeably, contributing little to burst kills; enclosed spaces and dense cover blunt it badly; stacks decay in a single turn, so any turn spent doing something else resets the work; ammunition becomes a genuine cost if your table tracks it.

# Hunter

## Big Game Hunter

These hunters seek the most dangerous prey imaginable — not for treasure, but for the glory of the kill and the power within the trophies they harvest. They are patient and strong, and know every beast, however large, has a weakness.

## Runic Hunter

A specialist against magical threats, the runic hunter etches arcane symbols onto gear and arrows to disrupt, control and overpower spellcasters and enchanted beasts.

## Treasure Hunter

## Apex
sixth: yes

*The Recalibrated*

Every hunter learns to read a group and find its leader. The apex learned that the leader is rarely the most dangerous thing present, and went looking for what was. These hunters take contracts nobody else will read twice — the thing that has been eating the hunters sent before them, the beast that has learned what a crossbow is, the predator with a name in three languages.

They are not braver than other hunters. They have simply recalibrated what counts as prey, and everything below that line has stopped registering.

- **Apex Instinct.** Hunter's Instinct identifies the most dangerous creature present rather than the ranking one, and tells you its rough power tier, what it has killed recently, and whether it has hunted your kind before. Against that target, your instinct bonuses increase to +4 on dex and ranged rolls.

- **Apex (1/day).** Against a marked target above your tier, declare the hunt closed. For 1d4+2 turns you cannot miss it, it cannot flee or teleport from you, and every hit forces a save against a stacking wound that halves one of its capabilities — movement, attacks, casting, regeneration, in whatever order you choose. # Wildsman
class: Wildsman

Wardens who stand between the world and those who would harm it.

## Deep Roots

Draws strength from the land; settle them and they are near impossible to dislodge.

## Untamed
sixth: yes

*One of Them*

Wildsmen keep a foot in both worlds — a companion at their side, a village to return to, a balance to maintain.

The untamed took the foot out. They keep no camp, carry nothing worked by hand they did not make themselves, and answer to nothing that speaks.

What they lost in doing it is real and visible: they are poor company, worse diplomats, and physically uncomfortable inside walls. What they gained is that the wild stopped treating them as a guest. Guardian of Nature protects most wildsmen from wild animals. The untamed are not protected from them. They are simply one of them.

- **One of Them.** Wild creatures treat you as a member of their own kind, regardless of species. You may move through any wild territory unchallenged, feed and rest among predators, and give ground-level instructions to any wild animal without taming, rolling or a companion bond.

- **The Land Provides.** You never need to forage, rest indoors, ration food or water, or roll for survival in any wilderness. You gain the effects of a full long rest anywhere wild, and take no penalty from weather, terrain, altitude or exposure of any kind.
- **Answering Call (1/combat).** Call. Every wild creature within a mile that can reach you arrives within 1d4 turns and fights alongside you — numbers, size and type at GM discretion based on where you are standing. They are not tamed and will not follow you afterward.
- **Untamed (1/day).** Shed the last of it. For 1d4+2 turns your movement, senses and reflexes match the wildest thing in the region — you cannot be caught, cornered, restrained, tracked or outrun, your unarmed and self-made weapon damage doubles, and any creature that has ever feared a predator saves against fear on sight of you. For an hour afterwards you cannot speak, read or use anything requiring hands.

## The Long Watch
sixth: yes

*The Ground That Watches Back*

Waywatchers are set to observe a place. The long watch kept theirs past the point where anyone was left to relieve them — a pass with no army behind it any more, a road nobody uses, a border between two nations that no longer exist. They stayed anyway.

Somewhere in the decades of it, the thing they were watching started watching back, and the distinction between the watcher and the ground they hold quietly stopped mattering. They are still there. Whatever walks into their ground learns this at the same time it learns everything else.

- **Claimed Ground.** Spend an hour in a location to claim it, up to roughly a square mile. Within claimed ground you know the position, number and direction of every living thing, at all times, through walls, cover, darkness, invisibility and magical concealment. You may hold two claims at once.

- **The Watch Does Not Sleep.** Watcher's camouflage becomes permanent within claimed ground, breaking only when you attack and reinstating at the end of your next turn. You require no rest at all while holding a claim, and your party gains the benefits of a long rest each time they enter one.
- **What the Ground Remembers.** You may ask your claimed ground about the past. Learn who passed through, when, in what numbers, and whether they came back — reaching back roughly as long as you have held the claim.
- **The Long Watch (1/day).** Within claimed ground, hold it absolutely. For 1d4+2 turns nothing may leave the claim — enemies attempting to flee, teleport, tunnel or fly out are stopped at its edge and shot — you take no attacks of opportunity and cannot be located by any means, and every shot you fire ignores cover, concealment and range entirely.

# Trapper
class: Trapper

- Gain 1d12 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **Field Reset.** Reset or rebuild a used trap in half the normal time, if you have materials. Scavenging a battlefield or environment recovers 50% of used components, rounded up.
- **Trap Intuition.** You automatically sense when a creature enters a trapping ground you prepared within 1 mile. You don't know details, but you know the number of intruders, their general size, and whether the trap has triggered.

## Temptations

Trapper of bait, lure, whisper and bribe. Makes foes want exactly what kills them.

- **Irresistible Bait (Passive).** Attach bait to a trap — food, glitter, gold, scent or a promise. A creature that sees or smells it must roll or approach the trap at its next opportunity, unless actively aware it's dangerous.

- **False Escape.** When an enemy escapes a trap, immediately trigger a secondary layer that always applies a status (bleed, slow, blind or fear).
- **Taste of Fortune.** Rig treasure bait so whoever touches it loses 1 random item instead. Works even mid-combat.
- **The Perfect Lure.** Once per long rest, tailor bait so specific the target cannot resist unless immune to temptation — they walk into the trap even knowing it's a trap.

## Social Trapping

Trapmaker of people, secrets and favours. A battlefield of conversations, blackmail and leverage.

- **Sweet Words, Sharpened Hooks (Passive).** Any secret willingly shared with you becomes a string to pull later: call on it to make the target hesitate one turn, unable to attack you.
- **Loose Tongue Setup.** Once per encounter, ask a target a loaded question. On a poor reply they reveal more than intended — location, motive, hidden item, a lie.
- **Web of Favours.** Once per encounter, call in a favour owed to clear legal issues, open gates or secure resources.
- **Blackmail Ledger.** Catch someone in a snare — physical or social — to extract a damaging truth you can later use to force compliance or protection.
- **Conversation Is the Cage.** Once per long rest, verbally corner a target without rope or steel: they cannot act aggressively for 1d4 turns, or must flee the situation.

## Urban Trapper

Streets, alleys, rooftops. A manhole or window can kill.

The titan-hunter. Builds traps that break giants.

- **The Boulder.** Once per combat, release a rolling obstacle that batters, pushes or forces large foes to dodge.

Traps that aren't built for mercy — they're built for corpses. While other trappers restrain, bargain or capture, you count kills.

## The Long Game
sixth: yes

*It Was Already Set*

A trapper builds the right trap for the job. The long game builds it first, and finds the job for it later. These trappers think in seasons — a snare set in spring for something that will not walk that road until autumn, a debt arranged with a merchant three towns over against a need not yet identified.

They rarely appear to be doing anything. By the time it becomes clear what they were doing, it has already closed. Other trappers consider them insufferable, largely because they are correct.

- **Already Set (3/day).** Declare, at any time, that you set a trap here previously — before the scene, before the session, before the party arrived. It exists, it has always existed, and it triggers now. Requires only that you could plausibly have been in the area at some point.
- **Layered.** Right Trap for the Job may catch a group of any size rather than up to three, and you may chain two traps so that escaping the first delivers the target into the second. The second trap need not have been declared until the first is escaped.
- **Set for Anything.** Your traps may be built for concepts as well as creatures — a trap for whoever opens this door, whoever speaks a certain name, whoever is lying, whoever draws a weapon first. Bosses are no longer exempt, though they gain a save.
- **Never Just One.** Any trap of yours that is disarmed, spotted or destroyed reveals a second beneath it, once per trap. The second was always there and requires no additional resource.
- **The Long Game (1/day).** Declare a plan complete. Everything that has happened in this encounter was the trap: name an outcome you have been working toward, and the pieces are in place for it — the door was already unlocked, the guard was already bought, the ground was already undermined, the leader was already carrying your poison. The GM adjudicates scale, but it should reshape the encounter rather than adjust it.

# Tinkerer
class: Tinkerer

- Gain 1d8 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.

Steam knights charge into battle in homemade suits of steam-powered armour, with weapons running on the same power. A tank on the suit holds the steam build-up.

- **Steam Powered Strike.** With a steam weapon, consume steam for +1d6 damage per stack.
- **Cauterise Wounds.** As a bonus action, spend steam to heal 1d6 per stack.

Alchemists blur the line between magic and science, creating potions and items with magic-like effects for themselves and their party. They can also store spells to unleash at a more important moment.

- **Create Elixir (roll 1d6).** 1 Healing (1d8 healing) · 2 Speed (+1 action) · 3 Iron-skin (−3 to attack rolls against you) · 4 Strength (+1d6 damage for 1 turn) · 5 Shrink (advantage on dodge for 1 turn) · 6 Invisibility (vanish for one turn). Elixirs also give 10 temp hp.

- **Potion Master.** Any potion you consume can be added to a list you can create on demand (max 3 at a time).

Battle tinkerers master magical weapons, harnessing their full power for devastating strikes, and exploit opponents' weapons and armour to reduce their combat effectiveness.

A master of constructs builds mechanical helpers to simplify daily life and protect them in combat. How many they can command depends on the constructs' complexity.

- **Create Construct.** Build a construct; time and resources depend on its complexity. Max total complexity 5 (can be enhanced).

*The One Who Built It Once By Accident*

Every tinkerer builds. The arch-artificer builds the thing that should not have worked — once, years ago, by accident, and has spent every day since trying to do it deliberately. They are not better engineers than the master of constructs or the armourer; they are worse ones, working past the edge of what the discipline can explain, and their workshops are full of devices nobody can repair because nobody understands them. Including, on bad days, the artificer.

# Beastmaster
class: Beastmaster

- Gain 1d16 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- Attempt to reduce a tame's wildness with a wisdom check: a pass reduces it by one; a fail can mean +1 wildness, +1 wildness to all tames, the tame duelling you, or the tame leaving.
- Your beasts can now make saving throws when they are down.

**Starting weapon — choose one.** Steel Javelin (4d4) · Darksteel spearhead spear (1d20) · Darksteel short axe (1d12). All new beastmasters are also offered an item bound with the Find Familiar spell on entering the academy.

## Rare Beast Specialist

These beastmasters gather rare, mythic and legendary beasts, travelling far and wide to track them down.

- **Mythic Rapport.** When taming a rare, mythic or legendary beast, roll 2d100 and take the higher. If successful, the beast starts at +2 wildness, but you may immediately reduce it by 1 (min 1) with an intelligence check (disadvantage based on rarity).
- **Dangerous Allure.** Beasts above rare (celestial, fiendish, ancient) start with +5 wildness, but you have advantage on checks to manage their wilder tendencies.
- **Better Beast Knowledge.** Assess any common or uncommon beast's capabilities with an intelligence check, or a rare beast with a disadvantage intelligence check.
- **Fame and Fortune.** As you gather exotic beasts you gain fame — with its ups and downs.
- **Lesser Command rune.** You receive a rune of *Lesser Command* on a weapon or item: on a failed wisdom save (the target may choose to fail), a tame of wildness 3 or less must follow a one-word command.

## Greater Connection

These beastmasters form deep bonds with every creature they meet, communicating with tames without magic. They make exceptional forward scouts and are skilled at caring for a party's mounts and pets.

- **Clear Communication.** Communicate effectively with any tamed beast or animal working with the party (e.g. mounts), letting you push them safely to their limits and cut travel times.
- **Many Eyes and Ears.** Close your eyes and sense through a tame within 2 km, rolling perception with your stat through its senses. You can't move while doing so and take a stun stack on exiting. Using this on a wildness 4+ beast risks it acting independently (wisdom save to resist).
- **Complex Commands.** Tames can act independently — protecting a target, scouting ahead — and take beneficial actions without your command.
- **Help Actions.** Your tames can give you or your party help actions.
- **Tactical Retreat.** As an action, a larger tame can disengage a willing party member from combat (contested strength roll if unwilling). A wildness 3+ beast may refuse (1d6: 1–2 it disengages without the ally).

## Borrower

Borrowers temporarily use their beasts' abilities and skills, offering their party a wide variety of abilities depending on their tames.

- **Borrow.** Borrow one of a tame's abilities for a single turn, for you or an ally — but without its resistances (borrow a dragon's fire breath and you'll burn yourself). Each use gives that beast +1 wildness.
- **Beast Healing.** On your turn, painlessly transfer hp between your beasts, yourself or party members (never below 1 for a tame). The recipient's wildness drops by 1 and the donor's rises by 1.
- **Primal Sense.** Sense when you're being watched and from which direction, without turning or giving any outward sign.
- **Action Transfer.** Take an action in place of your tames on their turns.
- **Stronger Together.** Gain resistance to anything your beasts are immune to (e.g. a fire-immune beast gives you fire resistance) — only while that beast is at wildness 2 or lower.

## Stalker

Stalker beastmasters hunt and slay targets, following them until the ideal moment to strike. They have been paid to assassinate hard-to-locate or highly dangerous targets.

- **Pack Attack.** For each tame that hits a target in a turn, gain +5 damage on your hit (max +25). Each +5 gives you +1 wildness; at wildness 5+ you must pass a wisdom save or attack the nearest target, ally or foe. Your wildness resets each turn.
- **Silent as a Ghost.** When you and your tames follow something, if half of you pass the check the rest pass too. Fails automatically if any beast has wildness 4+.
- **Follow the Lead.** When you or a tame strikes a target, the rest of the pack gains +1 (stacks to +5) to attack rolls this turn.
- **Pinned Down.** Medium or larger tames can pin on a passed attack roll (strength check, then 1d2 — pinned on a 2). Pinned targets can't attack or dodge, and your party has advantage against them. Only for beasts at wildness 3+ or 0.
- **Ambush Statuses.** Status effects apply with advantage during a surprise round and the first round of combat.

## Caller

The caller doesn't need to tame beasts to benefit from them, and their tames needn't always be material — letting them travel stealthily and summon beasts when needed.

- **Personal Beast Realm.** Dismiss tames to a private realm with everything they need, and summon them back when needed. Tames fully heal there (unless at 0 hp) but can't be resummoned for X days (X = hp healed ÷ 10, rounded up). Wildness drops by 1 per day spent there. *Risk — Realm Corruption:* if more than half your tames are wildness 3+, the realm turns hostile and random encounters can occur when calling beasts back.
- **Summon Wild Beasts.** Call random nearby beasts to aid in combat or otherwise. They have wildness 5 and leave after combat unless tamed.
- **Lingering Spirits.** Beasts that have been to your realm remain with you as spirits when slain. They give no physical help, though some can use abilities in spirit form.
- **Beastly Spirits.** Tame the spirits of slain beasts (not their bodies). Spirit tames have fixed wildness 0 and can't interact physically, though some can use abilities in spirit form.
- **Beastly Blade.** Imbue your weapon with the ability of a beast or spirit in your realm, adding half that beast's standard attack damage.

## Titanbound

*The Arrangement*

Beastmasters keep a stable. The titanbound keeps one thing, and it is not a pet. Somewhere in the wild there was a creature too large and too old to tame, and the beastmaster who found it did not tame it — they made an arrangement.

## Inevitable
sixth: yes

*A Matter of Scheduling*

Trackers follow. The inevitable does not follow, exactly — following implies the possibility of losing the trail. Once they have a name, a scent or a piece of a person, the outcome stops being in question and becomes a matter of scheduling.

They are slow. They are not stealthy, not fast, and rarely first to arrive. They simply do not stop, and everyone who has ever been the subject of one describes the same thing: the growing understanding that distance was never the answer.

- **Named.** Given a name, a possession, a drop of blood or a clear description, you may mark a target permanently. You always know the direction to them and roughly how far, across any distance, plane, ward or concealment. The mark ends only when they die or you release it.
- **No Such Thing as Cold.** Trails do not age for you. You may follow a track laid years ago as readily as one laid this morning, through water, fire, crowds, teleportation and deliberate misdirection.
- **They Have to Rest.** You do not. You require no sleep or food while pursuing a marked target, move at full speed indefinitely, and gain +5 to all rolls resisting anything that would delay, detain, imprison or divert you.
- **Word Travels.** Every settlement you pass through while hunting yields something — a sighting, a name, a debt owed to your quarry. Roll perception; even a failure yields a fragment.
- **Inevitable (1/day).** Close the distance. You arrive where your marked target is, however far away, arriving within 1d4 turns and knowing exactly what you will find. You cannot bring the party. The GM may narrate the journey afterwards; mechanically, you are simply there.

- **Mortar.** Make cluster mortars (1 black powder charge per shot) dealing 20d5 in an area; the shot lands at the start of your next turn. Friendly fire applies.

## Logistics Master

Logistics masters are a core part of any major mobilising force, ensuring it always has the equipment it needs. Their contacts and knowledge get new equipment — or repairs — at a fraction of the cost.

- **Resupply.** Passing through a town, acquire up to 5 gp of consumables or repairs free at a logistics hub — arrows, bolts, potions, cloth, steel and so forth.
- **Haggler.** Determine an item's material value and convince the seller to sell closer to the cost of materials and labour.
- **Deep Connections.** In a town with a blacksmith, logistics hub or similar, easily get information on anything they know or have seen.
- **Routine Maintenance.** With a logistics master in the party, upkeep rolls for items or equipment have double advantage.
- **Weapon Maintenance Station.** Build a station that temporarily enhances any weapons serviced there for the next combat.

## Mason

Masons use their knowledge of destruction to create and fortify. They reinforce structures to withstand greater impacts and are often recruited to build castles and the like for nobles.

- **Reinforce.** Inside a structure, spend an action to fortify one aspect — block doors/windows, support walls, patch holes. Repairs have a min hit value of 20 and 1d50 hp.
- **Temporary Structures.** Build while travelling: Watchtower (50 ft, 2 hours) · Lean-to (up to 8 people, 4 hours) · Wall (10 ft tall, 10 ft long, 2 ft wide, 2 hours) · Foxhole (cover for a ranged fighter, 10 minutes).
- **Structural Integrity.** Quickly tell how much hp a structure has left, what it was built from, and whether anything harmful to it is present.
- **Master Builder.** Structures you build have +100 hp, equipment made near you gets +5 to its creation roll, and your siege engines have twice the hp.
- **The Leveller.** Build a machine that quickly levels land, removing vegetation and water (not structures) from the area it works over.

## Jack of All Trades

A versatile siegemaster who can do lesser versions of what other siegemasters do, plus a few tricks of their own. Sought by forces wanting a wider range of skills.

- **Stolen Siege Skills.** Lesser Reinforce (min hit 10, 1d20 hp) · Lesser Resupply (1 gp of equipment) · Lesser Crumble (small walls, wagons and the like only).
- **More Mini Siege Engines.** Create two additional mini siege engines.
- **Aligned Sights.** Your first shot with any siege weapon automatically hits and deals +25 damage.
- **Variety Is the Spice of Life.** You can't use the same siege weapon twice in a row, but each time you cycle through all your options, your siege weapons grow stronger.
- **Scorpio.** Build a scorpio as a bonus action: 1d20 damage, crits on 17+.

## Specialist

Specialists love to focus on a single siege machine.

- **Specialised Siege Weapon.** Pick a siege machine you can build instantly, with +5 to rolls with it and −5 with others.
- **Siege Weapon Enhancement.** Upgrade your siege weapons (money/time/materials); future builds keep the upgrades.
- **Specialised Ammo.** Procure special ammo with various effects; cost varies by effect.
- **My Siege Machine.** When your machine is destroyed, for 1d3 turns you crit every enemy who took part in destroying it.
- **Hunker Down.** Gain 50 temp hp and stay rooted at your siege machine for 1d4 turns, able to fire it an additional time (with disadvantage). Leaving loses any remaining temp hp.

## Citybreaker
sixth: yes

*The One Who Reads Walls*

Siege masters break structures. The citybreaker stopped thinking of a city as a collection of structures. They read a settlement the way a swordsman reads a stance — where the weight sits, what is holding what up, which one wall carries three districts behind it.

Given a week and a hill to stand on, they will tell you the order in which a fortress falls and be right. They are not welcome in most cities they have visited, on principle.

- **Read the Bones.** On seeing any structure, settlement or fortification, you know its load-bearing points, water supply, weak walls, tunnels, and how long it holds under siege. You may name one point whose destruction collapses the largest possible portion.
- **More Than One.** Siege Time may build two engines at once and they take 2 turns each rather than 3. You may crew them with allies, tames, constructs or willing civilians, all acting on your initiative.
- **Bigger Than the Book.** You may build engines that do not exist — a bore, a flooding device, a mobile tower, a mounted battery — designed with your GM. One such design at a time, replaceable over downtime.
- **Nothing Is Load-Bearing to You.** Your engines and demolitions ignore magical reinforcement, wards, hardened stone and antimagic. A wall built by magic falls like a wall.
- **Citybreaker (1/day).** Bring down a district. Name a section of a settlement or fortress — a wall run, a gate complex, a keep, a bridge, a quarter. Over 1d4 turns it comes down entirely. Everything inside it is a GM problem, and so are you, afterwards.

# Bard
class: Bard

- Gain 1d8 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- You gain 5 extra uses of bardic inspiration per day, and your inspiration die is now 1d12.
- **Wink.** Wink after any comment to confuse a target about its meaning or intention.

**Starting gear — choose one.**
- **Tattoo Instrument:** pull your instrument from a tattoo at any time; put it down and it returns to the tattoo.
- **Back-up Cap:** your hat backs up your shows, giving advantage on performance checks.
- **Deck of Illusions:** draw a random card and throw it down to form a major image of a creature, lasting until dispelled.

## Puppet Master

Puppet masters manipulate allies and enemies openly and visibly, where other bards are covert. They can be mistaken for necromancers when they use the husks of slain enemies, though they can't keep control of a husk for long.

- **Strings of Manipulation.** A target must pass a contested charisma roll or perform an action of your choice. On a pass, they know what you tried.
- **Ventriloquism.** Perform through a puppet, using your abilities through it.
- **Tangled.** Throw rope, twine and similar at a target; on a failed dodge they're tangled. The material vanishes at the end of the combat/encounter.
- **Allied Puppet.** Spend an inspiration point to bind a willing ally in magical ropes, letting you spend your actions and reactions through them.
- **Enemy Puppeteering.** Spend an inspiration point to mark a non-boss enemy (strength check to resist). On a fail, for the rest of combat you choose their action each turn; they contest it with a disadvantage strength roll.

**Performances:** *The Hook* — magic strings from your instrument pull a target towards you · *Curse of the Fallen Puppet* — for the next turn, the first 1d3 creatures that drop to 0 hp near you each take one action you command before death saves · *Lesser Message* — send a single-word message into the mind of a target you can see.

## Medicine Man

Medicine men offer less powerful healing than clerics but are far easier to find, travelling between villages to heal them. Taverns are booked to capacity on the nights they perform.

- **Serenade of Courage.** Boost your and your allies' damage for 1 turn by the number of successful hits the party has made.
- **Hold On.** Your help on death saves counts as double help.
- **Healing Inspiration.** All your inspiration also heals for the value of the inspiration die rolled.
- **Healing Manipulation.** After healing a target, you have advantage on charisma rolls against them — friend or foe feels indebted for your kindness.
- **Ballad of the Fallen.** Once per combat, recall an ally or enemy you saw die to gain a random one of their abilities for 1d3 turns.

**Performances:** *Mass Healing Song* — everything that hears it heals 1d4 · *Soothing Song* — everything that hears it has all active debuffs removed · *Lesser Message*.

## Heroic Tales

Heroic tales bards are walking tomes of past battles — often exaggerated, but so captivating that nobody questions them. They travel the world paying for nothing, convincing people they are, or work with, a hero.

- **Battle Recorder.** Enemies who know you're a heroic tales bard are less likely to target you — they want you to write down their victory.
- **Writer's Flourish.** Three times per combat, an ally makes a flourishing blow that automatically hits for at least 50% of their potential damage that turn.
- **From the Books.** Advantage on combat-related intelligence rolls, and on intelligence rolls while in combat.
- **Are You Sure?** Try to convince a target to take a different action on their turn — it'll make a better tale.
- **It Went Like This.** With a passed charisma roll, a target believes your account of a combat, however improbable.

**Performances:** *That's the Wrong Order* — once per combat, all participants reroll initiative · *Join the Winning Side* — twice per day, try to convince a target to fight on your side · *Lesser Message*.

## Secret Holder

Some think secret holders run every kingdom in the world, though there's little proof. They make excellent spies, often knowing more about their targets than the targets themselves. The best seem to be simple bards, easily gaining trust and manipulating those around them.

- **Trustable Face.** People are more likely to say things they shouldn't to you, not realising until it's too late.
- **Raise Eyebrow.** A raised eyebrow makes someone doubt what they said, letting you try to convince them their facts are wrong.
- **Blackmail.** With basic knowledge of a person, convince them you know their secret; on success they're prone to obey you and/or spill the secret they think you know.
- **Impersonate.** With a simple disguise and a brief conversation with a person, convince others you are them — opening greater secrets and secure locations.
- **Shadow Control.** Once per day, convince a person to do whatever you say for 1 hour, as long as it doesn't directly harm them.

**Performances:** *Cutting Words* — 1d4 damage and disadvantage on the target's next roll · *Mass Suggestion* — creatures that hear it make a charisma save or obey a single-word command (can't directly harm them) · *Lesser Message*.

## Seducer

Seducer bards manipulate through love, lust and desire, making it hard to act against them. In combat, their looks and smiles dissuade foes from attacking.

- **Subtle Seduction.** A target makes an intelligence or wisdom save (your choice); on a fail you give them an inverted bardic inspiration, reducing a chosen roll.
- **Charm.** Charm a target, passing a charisma save each turn to maintain each charm. Charmed enemies can't attack you.
- **Helpless.** While unarmed, advantage on charisma, and enemies are less likely to target you.
- **Anything You Want Me to Be.** Used with Alter Self, a target perceives you as their deepest desire — more willing to do as you say and unable to attack you until you deal 50 damage to them or 24 hours pass.
- **Humana, Humana, Humana.** Apply one effect to a creature you've charmed: it can't cast verbal spells or relay information as it stutters in amazement; or it can't willingly move away from you; or it makes a wisdom save and, failing by more than 5, is paralysed for the charm's duration.

**Performances:** *Alter Self* (out of combat only) — alter your appearance for 24 hours or until performed again · *Mass Desire* (out of combat only) — all who hear it make a charisma save or attempt a task you request · *Lesser Message*.

## The Voice
sixth: yes

*Said and Done*

Bards inspire. The voice is what happens when a bard's words stop being a performance and start being a fact. They are not louder or more eloquent than a seducer or a teller of heroic tales; what they say simply becomes true in the ears of everyone present, and the world adjusts to accommodate it.

It costs them. Every use spends standing somewhere — a favour, a reputation, a truth they will not be able to tell later — and the oldest of them are owed nothing by anyone.

- **Said and Done.** Speak a single command as an action. Every creature that hears and understands you rolls a wisdom save or obeys for one turn. Non-boss enemies save at disadvantage. Commands that would obviously kill the listener are refused.
- **Weight.** Inspiration points increase to 6 per day, and a spent point may instead be given as a d6 penalty to an enemy's roll. Allies you inspire also become immune to fear, charm and morale effects for the turn.
- **Take It Back (1/scene).** Unsay something. A statement you made — a promise, an insult, an order, a confession — was never said, and nobody present remembers it, though written records and absent parties remain.
- **Whose Name Is Known.** In any settlement, you may claim standing you have not earned. You are recognised, credited and extended courtesy appropriate to a name you invoke. Sustaining the claim under scrutiny requires a charisma roll; failing it costs the real name too.
- **The Voice (1/day).** Say something the world has to accept. Declare a single fact about the current scene — that the guards were expecting you, that this man is a traitor, that the crowd is already on your side, that the duel is over. It becomes true. You permanently lose a point of standing somewhere in the world for it, and the GM decides where.

# Trickster
class: Trickster

- Gain 1d6 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **Three Card Pull.** When you draw a card, you may draw three instead. If they share a colour, double the damage; if they share a suit, double the effect's duration. If they share a suit and are consecutive, triple the effect and multiply their values instead of adding them (2, 3, 4 = 24 damage).
- **Deck Mastery.** You always carry a full standard deck. Lost cards return after a short rest.

## Con Man

Smooth talker, liar, manipulator. Wins fights before they start.

- **Crooked Smile (Passive).** Whenever a creature believes a lie or falls for a trick of yours, store 1 Scheme (max 3). Spend a Scheme to force a target to roll with disadvantage against your next action involving them.
- **Sleight of Words.** Once per round, as a reaction, convince a hostile creature to hesitate. They skip their next attack unless directly harmed.
- **Loaded Deck.** At the start of combat, secretly set one card from the deck aside face-down. Reveal it at any time to replace a drawn card. Repeat the trick as a bonus action.
- **Switcheroo.** Swap an item in someone's possession for one of similar size without notice, unless they actively check for it.
- **Collapse the Deal.** Once per combat, declare a bargain broken, even if there never was one. Choose a target — their next save or dodge automatically fails.

## Street Artist

Performer, distraction expert, deadly flash of colour.

- **Flashy Technique (Passive).** The first time you hit each round, the target must roll. On a fail they're distracted, and the next attack on them has advantage.
- **Card Cascade.** Once per combat, throw 5 cards in one action. All their damage is dealt, but only one effect triggers — the highest card drawn.
- **Flourish Volley.** Draw any number of cards and toss them at any number of targets. The volley fails if the cards' combined value is greater than a 1d50 roll.
- **Encore.** When you land a face card, draw a bonus card and you may throw it immediately.
- **Grand Finale.** Once per long rest, throw a full fan of all 52 cards. Every enemy who sees it rolls wisdom or is stunned for 1 turn; those who pass are distracted for 2 turns instead.

## Fence

Fence tricksters turn a profit on everything that passes through their hands — stolen, smuggled or simply "found". They know the worth of any object and the buyer for any item, and can make almost anything disappear.

- **Appraiser's Eye.** Instantly know the true value, origin and any hidden properties of an item you handle.
- **Black Market.** In any settlement, find a buyer or seller for almost anything within a day at fair underground prices, with advantage on charisma rolls against criminal contacts. Once per settlement, sell loot for 25% more or buy for 25% less.
- **Sticky Deck.** Cards you throw return to your deck at the end of the turn or scene, and any small object you palm vanishes into the deck to be drawn back later.
- **Loaded Hand.** Once per combat, deal from the bottom of the deck — your next card counts as a face card of your choice (Jack, Queen or King) for its effect.
- **Launder.** Disguise the origin of an item or person — forging provenance, filing off marks, altering a face's notoriety — so hot goods or wanted allies pass inspection.

## Open Pockets

Open Pockets tricksters never met a pocket they couldn't empty. Coins, keys, even the weapon from an enemy's hand — it all finds its way to them, and they make it look like luck.

- **Light Fingers.** Attempt to steal an item; the roll must beat the target's dex and passive perception. Advantage if you're unnoticed.
- **Now You See It.** On a successful card strike in combat, lift one item the target isn't currently holding.
- **Disarming Draw.** Throw a card to flick a weapon or item from a foe's grip (DEX save); it lands where you choose within 10 ft.
- **Deep Pockets.** You conceal an improbable amount on your person; searches for hidden items on you are at disadvantage, and you always "happen to have" a mundane small item.
- **Sleight of Hand.** Once per scene, on a passed dex roll, plant or retrieve an object on or from a creature without them noticing — a card, a key, a piece of evidence.

## Luck of the Draw

Some tricksters master the cards; this one surrenders to them. Luck of the Draw tricksters live and die by the next card — and somehow it always seems to come up in their favour.

- **Wild Card.** At the start of combat, draw a card; its suit's greater effect is primed and triggers on your first hit, whatever card you actually play.
- **Double or Nothing.** When you play a card you may draw a second — if it's higher, both effects apply; if lower, neither does and you take the spades bonus damage yourself.
- **House Edge (Passive).** Your luck tokens increase to 5 (from 3), and drawing a natural matching pair in a turn refunds a token.
- **Stacked Odds.** Once per combat, look at the top 3 cards of your deck and arrange them in any order.
- **All In.** Once per day, play your whole hand at once, resolving up to 4 cards' effects on one target. You can't use cards again until you reshuffle (one full turn).

## Kingmaker
sixth: yes

*The Long Hand*

Tricksters win at tables. The kingmaker got bored of tables. The same instinct that reads a room of gamblers reads a room of councillors, and the same patience that waits three hands for a run of cards waits three years for a succession.

They are still gambling — they are simply gambling with cities now, and the deck they carry has stopped being a weapon and started being a habit they cannot break.

- **Stacked (3/combat).** Before drawing, name a suit. Your draw is of that suit. Resets on a long rest.
- **Face Cards.** Any card you draw of value 10 or higher may be treated as a jack; jacks may be treated as queens; queens as kings. Each upgrade costs one card discarded from your hand, and kings drawn naturally trigger the greater effect twice.
- **The Long Hand.** During downtime, place a marker on a faction, a household or an institution — a debt, a rumour, a planted man, a forged letter. You may cash it at any later point for an outcome proportional to how long it has sat. Three markers active at once.
- **Nobody Watches the Dealer.** In any situation involving negotiation, gambling, succession, inheritance or a contested claim, you may make a charisma roll to have been involved in setting it up. On a success, one term is already in your favour.
- **Kingmaker (1/day).** Deal a hand for something that is not a fight. Name a person and a position — a throne, a guild seat, a command, a bounty, a noose. Draw five cards; the suits determine how it lands and the values determine how fast. The outcome arrives within days and the GM writes the consequences. It is never clean.

# Runesmith
class: Runesmith

- Gain 1d8 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **Inscribe.** You don't cast in the moment. During a rest, inscribe runes onto gear, allies, the ground or objects; each lies dormant until its trigger (a word, a touch, an enemy crossing it) fires it. Carry prepared runes equal to twice your level, re-inscribed on a rest.
- **Quick Etch.** Once per combat, inscribe a single rune on the spot as an action.
- **Runic Sight.** Read any runes, glyphs or magical script, and detect inscribed magic at a glance.

**Starting gear — choose one.** Engraving tools (inscribe runes in half the time) · Rune-steel chisel (make one rune permanent on a single item) · Bag of blank rune-stones (5 throwable rune carriers, refilled in town).

## Wardsmith

Wardsmiths inscribe protection — shields that spring from a drawn sigil, walls of light, glyphs that turn a blow aside. Where they've had time to prepare, the party is very hard to kill.

- **Ward Rune.** A rune on an ally or yourself that, when triggered, negates the next hit or halves an area effect.
- **Glyph of Warding.** A surface rune that raises a 10 ft barrier (1d40 HP) when crossed or spoken.
- **Anchor Rune (Passive).** Allies on your inscribed ground gain +3 defence and can't be forcibly moved.
- **Reflective Sigil.** A triggered ward rune returns half the blocked damage to the attacker.
- **Sanctum (1/combat).** Trigger all ward runes within 30 ft at once; every ally gains a 20-point barrier and immunity to one status for 1d3 turns.

## Warbrands

Warbrands burn power into blade and bow. Their runes wait on a weapon until it strikes, then unleash fire, force or worse, turning any ally into a magic-armed threat.

- **Brand Weapon.** Inscribe a weapon; its hits this combat deal +1d8 of a chosen element.
- **Trigger Word.** Detonate a brand on command — 2d8 in 10 ft around the struck target.
- **Sharing the Forge (Passive).** Brand allies' weapons, not just your own; up to 3 brands active at once.
- **Overload Rune.** Stack a second brand on a weapon; its next hit deals both and crits on 17+.
- **Runic Volley (1/combat).** Brand every ally's weapon within 20 ft; their next hits each deal +2d6 and ignore resistance.

## Glyphtrapper

Glyphtrappers seed the ground with waiting death. By the time a fight starts, the floor is a minefield of sigils only they can see.

- **Trap Glyph.** A ground rune; the first enemy to cross it takes 2d8 and a condition you chose when inscribing it.
- **Snare Sigil.** A rune that roots or trips whoever crosses it (STR/DEX save) for 1d3 turns.
- **Chain Glyphs (Passive).** Link your ground runes; triggering one triggers all linked runes within 15 ft.
- **Hidden Hand.** Your glyphs are invisible to non-magical detection and are only found at disadvantage, even by casters.
- **Killing Field (1/combat).** Saturate a 20 ft area before combat; for 1d4 turns enemies entering or starting their turn there take 3d8 and a random debuff.

## Flesh Runes

Flesh-rune smiths inscribe living skin. Their tattoos are slow to apply but lasting, turning a willing ally's body into a canvas of borrowed power.

- **Rune Tattoo.** Over a rest, inscribe a lasting tattoo granting a passive (+2 to a stat, a resistance, or a minor at-will effect). Each ally can bear a number equal to a third of your level.
- **Activatable Ink (Passive).** One tattoo per ally can be fired as a bonus action for a surge — a burst of speed, a heal, a damage ward.
- **Blood Channel.** Overcharge a tattoo by spending the bearer's HP for a doubled effect.
- **Shared Mark.** Link two tattooed allies; while both live they share one buff or split incoming damage.
- **Living Masterwork (1/combat).** Light up every tattoo you've inscribed on the party at once; each bearer gains all their tattoos' active effects for 1d3 turns.

## Forbidden Script

Some runes were sealed away for good reason. Smiths of the forbidden script use them anyway — words of unmaking and ruin that cost the caster as dearly as the target if handled poorly.

- **Forbidden Rune.** A rune of devastating power (4d10 or a severe effect); when it triggers, you take 2d8 backlash unless you pass a check.
- **Words of Unmaking.** A rune that strips a target of buffs, summons or one ability on a failed wisdom save.
- **Greedy Glyph.** A rune that feeds on its victim — the more HP the target has, the more it deals.
- **Contain the Backlash (Passive).** Once per combat, redirect a forbidden rune's backlash onto an adjacent enemy.
- **The Last Word (1/day).** Inscribe a doomsday rune that takes a full turn to arm: 6d10 in 30 ft with a catastrophic effect, but you and allies in range take 3d8 and you can't inscribe for 1d3 turns after.

## Keystone
sixth: yes

*One Carving, One Lifetime*

Runesmiths inscribe gear, ground and flesh. The keystone inscribes a place, once, permanently. It takes months. It cannot be moved, copied or undone, and everything the runesmith is worth after that is measured against whether they chose the right spot.

Most keystones carve one in a lifetime. The ones who carve two are usually correcting the first.

- **The Keystone.** Over a period of downtime, carve one permanent rune into a location — a hall, a crossroads, a bridge, a vault, a battlefield. Agree its effect with your GM. It never expires, functions whether or not you are present, and cannot be dispelled by anything short of destroying the site.
- **Anchored.** Within your keystone's range, all your inscribed runes trigger without a rest to prepare them and may be laid live, during combat, as an action.
- **Known Ground.** You may travel to your keystone from anywhere given time and a road, and you always know what has happened at it since you left — who came, what they did, what they took.
- **Second Carving.** You may maintain one temporary keystone alongside the permanent one, carved over a long rest and lasting until you carve another. Its effect must be smaller in scale.
- **Set in Stone (1/day).** Within your keystone's reach, declare something fixed. For 1d4+2 turns one condition holds absolutely inside the area and cannot be changed by any means — a door stays shut, an ally stays alive, a spell stays suppressed, an enemy stays where they are standing.

# Skinwalker
class: Skinwalker

- Gain 1d10 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **Wear the Skin.** Take the full form of any creature whose skin you've claimed, gaining its stat block, movement and natural attacks while keeping your own HP, traits and subclass abilities. Changing form is a bonus action, with no penalty for shifting on a turn you act.
- **Claim the Skin.** After a kill, spend a short rest harvesting to add the creature to your roster of forms (max forms equal to your level; trivial creatures don't count). You may also hold one boss or magical-beast form regardless of the limit.
- **Beast Tongue.** Speak with and understand any creature whose form you currently hold; creatures of that kind treat you as one of their own unless given clear reason not to.

**Starting gear — choose one.** Skinning kit (clean harvests and advantage to claim a new form) · Totem of forms (store 3 extra forms beyond your maximum) · Bone fetish (every held form's natural attacks gain +1d6).

## Predator

Predator skinwalkers favour the swift killers — wolves, great cats, raptors. They run prey down and open throats before the fight has properly begun.

- **Pounce.** After moving at least 10 ft in a predator form, your attack knocks the target prone (STR save) and deals +2d8. If it drops them, you may immediately move and attack again.
- **Pack Hunter.** You and all allies attacking a target you've marked (a free action when you draw its blood) gain advantage and +1d6 against it. Keep up to two marks at once.
- **Run Down.** Your movement is tripled in any predator form; you ignore difficult terrain and have advantage to chase, escape or close distance. Catching a fleeing target lets you attack for free.
- **Throat-Tear (Passive).** Your beast attacks crit on 17+, crits apply 2 bleed stacks (1d6/turn each), and a creature already bleeding takes +1d8 from you.
- **Frenzy (3/combat).** Make 4 natural attacks at full damage against one target; each kill moves the frenzy onto a new target and heals you 1d8.

## Titan

Titan skinwalkers wear the great beasts — bears, oxen, trolls and worse. They wade into the thick of it and simply don't fall.

- **Bulk.** In a titan form you gain +50 temp HP (refreshed on each form change), can't be moved or knocked prone against your will, and your reach extends 5 ft.
- **Earthshaker.** A slam hits all enemies within 15 ft for 3d8, knocks them prone (STR save) and makes the ground difficult for a turn.
- **Thick Hide.** In a titan form, reduce all incoming damage by 8, and resist the first hit each turn.
- **Rend (Passive).** Your attacks deal +2d10 to objects, structures and creatures larger than you, and ignore non-magical armour.
- **Unstoppable (3/combat).** For 1d3 turns nothing can halt or control your movement; charge through enemies for 4d8 each while taking half damage from all sources.

## Aberrant

Aberrant skinwalkers claim the things that should not be — chimerical horrors and things from deep caves and deeper planes. Their forms carry gifts no natural beast could offer.

- **Unnatural Form.** Your roster can include aberrations and magical beasts; each grants two unnatural abilities (acid blood, a tail-stinger, extra eyes, a gaze attack) chosen per form.
- **Horrifying.** On taking an aberrant form, enemies within 20 ft make a wisdom save or are frightened for 1d3 turns; frightened foes take +1d6 from you.
- **Adaptive Flesh (Passive).** The first time each combat you take a damage type, gain immunity to it for the rest of the fight. Regenerate 1d8 at the start of each of your turns.
- **Many Limbs.** In aberrant forms, make two extra attacks per turn; each natural weapon may target a different enemy.
- **Become the Nightmare (3/combat).** Warp fully for 1d4 turns — sprout an extra natural weapon, gain every movement mode (climb, burrow, fly), and your attacks ignore all resistance and immunity.

## Mimic

Mimic skinwalkers wear faces, not pelts. They slip into a victim's place, voice and manner — and walk away with a piece of what made them dangerous.

- **Wear a Face.** Perfectly copy the appearance, voice and mannerisms of any humanoid you've claimed; you automatically beat non-magical scrutiny and contest magical detection at advantage.
- **Stolen Skill.** While wearing a claimed humanoid, gain all their skill and weapon proficiencies and one of their signature abilities.
- **Borrowed Trick (Passive).** Copy up to two non-magical class traits from humanoid forms you hold (a soldier's stance, a rogue's shadow-step), swappable on a shift.
- **Slip the Skin.** As a reaction to being attacked or exposed, drop your form and instantly take another; the triggering attack misses and you may reposition 10 ft.
- **Perfect Impostor (1/combat, or 1/day for a full day).** Fully become a claimed humanoid — their memories surface as instincts, you gain their full ability set, and even close companions can't tell (the DM rolls secretly).

## Chimera

Chimera skinwalkers refuse to choose. They stitch the gifts of many forms into one impossible body — the wings of one beast, the jaws of another, the hide of a third.

- **Hybrid Form.** Combine traits from up to 3 claimed forms at once, gaining a chosen feature (and the natural attacks) from each.
- **Grafted Gifts (Passive).** Permanently keep two natural weapons and two movement modes from your roster, even in your true form.
- **Patchwork Resilience.** Each fused form trait grants +15 temp HP at the start of combat (max 60) and a matching resistance.
- **Adaptive Fusion.** As a bonus action, swap which traits are active; once per turn, react to an effect by fusing in a trait that counters it (a resistance, a movement mode, an immunity).
- **Apex Chimera (1/combat).** For 1d4 turns, fuse up to 5 forms — gaining all their natural attacks, every movement mode and one signature ability from each — and attack with three of them per turn.

## Hollow
sixth: yes

*The One With Nothing Underneath*

Every other skinwalker takes a shape and returns from it. The hollow stopped returning. Somewhere in the count of forms worn, the shape they started in stopped being the one they came back to — and then stopped being anything at all. What is left does not have a body of its own to put back on. It has a collection, a preference, and an increasingly theoretical memory of a face.

The trade is straightforward and severe: a hollow no longer needs to kill a thing to wear it. Without a shape of their own to be constrained by, they take whatever the moment requires from anything they have seen closely enough. The skin does not have to have been claimed. It does not, strictly, have to have existed.

- **No Shape of My Own.** You have no true form. You cannot be identified, dispelled, polymorph-broken or returned to a base state, because there is nothing to return to. Effects that force a shapeshifter's true form show a shape of your choosing. Changing form costs nothing and is never an action.
- **Seen Is Enough.** You may wear the form of any creature you have observed closely, without killing or claiming it. Forms taken this way grant movement, senses and natural attacks but only half the creature's stat block, until you have spent a full scene studying that kind of creature — after which it is yours in full, permanently.
- **Shed.** Drop a worn form as a free action to shed damage with it. All damage taken while wearing that form is discarded, and the form is spent for the encounter. Usable once per form per combat, and you may hold as many forms ready as you have collected.
- **Something Like It.** Wear a form you have not seen — described to you, painted, remembered by someone else, sworn to exist. It functions at half stat block and something about it is always wrong. Creatures who know the real thing recognise the error, and it unsettles them: wisdom save or disadvantage on their next turn.
- **Nothing Left to Wear (1/day).** For 1d4+2 turns, be whatever the turn requires. Change form freely at any point, as often as you like, drawing on your whole collection and anything within sight, with no half-fidelity penalty and no cap. Nothing can hold, bind, restrain or contain you. When it ends, roll: on a failure you permanently lose one fact about who you were before — a name, a face, a language, a person you loved — and gain a permanent graft in its place. The player writes down what was lost. The character does not remember losing it.

# Pirate
class: Pirate

- Gain 1d8 hit points and improve two stats by 1 or one stat by 2.
- **Swashbuckler's Luck.** Three times per day (five at sea), reroll an attack, dodge or daring stunt and take the better result. If the new roll is a natural 20, you gain a moment of legend — disarm a foe, rally allies or escape certain doom in style (rule of cool, doubled).
- **Fighting Style – Cutlass & Pistol.** +2 damage when you alternate between a melee and a ranged attack in the same turn.
- **Fighting Style – Dirty Tricks.** Once per combat, throw sand, swing from a rope or feign surrender to gain advantage on one attack or check.

**Starting gear — choose one.** Flintlock & cutlass set (1d10 / 1d8) · Hook & peg (a hidden blade and an unsettling reputation — advantage on intimidation) · Spyglass & charts (advantage to navigate, spot trouble and find plunder).

## Privateer

Privateers raid under a flag of legitimacy — letters of marque that turn piracy into patriotic service. They fight for crown and coin, and know exactly what both are worth.

- **Letters of Marque.** Your plunder is legal in friendly ports; advantage on charisma with lawful authorities, and access to naval supply.
- **Prize Money.** After any victory you claim the best loot and sell it for 25% more in port.
- **Boarding Action.** When you lead a boarding or charge, you and allies who follow gain +2 to attacks for the first turn.
- **Warrant (Passive).** Choose a bounty target; you deal +1d8 to them, and others recognise your right to take them.
- **Broadside (1/combat).** Call a coordinated volley — 4d8 to a target or area if a heavy weapon is on hand; even improvised, your crew's covering fire gives +5 to all party attacks for a turn.

## Captain

A captain is only as good as their crew, and a great captain makes a crew unbeatable. They lead from the front, bark the orders that win fights, and command loyalty that outlasts death.

- **The Crew.** Command a small band of crew (3 mooks, 12 HP, 1d8) who act on your initiative and can be replaced in any port.
- **All Hands.** As a bonus action, give an order granting all allies and crew in earshot +2 to attacks or saves for a turn.
- **Loyalty (Passive).** Your crew will take a hit meant for you (one per turn) and fight to the last; allies near you are immune to fear.
- **Press the Advantage.** When you or a crew member drops a foe, another crew member may immediately attack for free.
- **Captain's Command (1/combat).** Take the field for a turn — every ally and crew member may immediately take a basic action or move, in the order you choose.

## Stormcaller

Stormcallers are sailors whose superstitions came true. Through shanties, knots and old sea-pacts they coax wind, wave and weather to their side — not true magic, but try telling that to a drowned crew.

- **Call the Wind.** Summon a gust to fill sails, push a target (STR save) or deflect projectiles for a turn.
- **Sea Shanty.** Sing to buff the crew; allies who hear it gain +1d4 to rolls and advantage against fear for 1d3 turns.
- **Read the Sky (Passive).** Predict weather and danger at sea; the party can't be caught off guard by storms, reefs or ambush on the water.
- **Squall (3/combat).** Call a sudden squall in 20 ft — enemies are soaked and battered (1d10, disadvantage on ranged) for 1d3 turns; allies are unaffected.
- **Maelstrom (1/day).** Summon a 30 ft storm of wind and water for 1d4 turns — 3d10/turn to enemies inside, dragging them toward its centre (anywhere there's water to call).

## Corsair

The corsair is the terror of single combat — a blur of cutlass, dagger and flintlock who turns a duel into a performance and leaves the loser disarmed, embarrassed or dead.

- **Riposte.** When you dodge a melee attack, immediately counter for your weapon damage.
- **Flintlock Flourish.** Once per turn, fire your pistol as a bonus action mid-melee; on a hit the target is staggered (loses its reaction).
- **En Garde (Passive).** In one-on-one combat you gain +2 attack and +2 defence, and your Swashbuckler's Luck rerolls cost no uses while duelling.
- **Disarming Strike.** On a hit, try to flick a foe's weapon away (contested); you may catch and keep it.
- **Coup de Théâtre (3/combat).** A flashy combination — two attacks and a stunt. If both hit, the target is disarmed and knocked prone, and you gain advantage on your next roll from sheer style.

## Cursed

Cursed pirates carry something the sea gave them and never took back — a drowned god's mark, a ghost crew, a hold full of things that should have stayed sunk. Death sits lightly on them, and on those they call up.

- **Ghost Crew.** Summon 1d4 spectral crew (15 HP, 1d8, immune to non-magic damage) to fight at your side for 1d4 turns.
- **Mark of the Deep (Passive).** The first time you would drop each combat, you linger at 1 HP instead — dripping seawater and unkillable until the end of your next turn.
- **Drowned Grasp.** Spectral hands hold a target (STR save), dealing 1d8/turn while held.
- **Davy Jones' Due.** When an enemy dies near you, bind its spirit to your crew for the rest of the encounter.
- **Call the Kraken (1/day).** Summon a great tentacled horror for 1d4 turns — it strikes for 3d10, grapples and drags creatures large or small, and turns the field's edges into hazards (best at sea, possible anywhere with water).

## Legend
sixth: yes

*The Story Got Ahead of the Facts*

Pirates trust fortune to drag them out. The legend stopped needing fortune involved. Somewhere between the third impossible escape and the fifth, the stories about them got ahead of the facts, and a pirate whose stories are believed hard enough finds the facts catching up.

They do not roll for it any more. They announce what happened, and the ocean, the crew and the enemy all quietly agree that this is how it went.

- **As the Story Goes (3/day).** Swashbuckler's Luck no longer requires a natural 20 for a moment of legend. Simply declare one, and the rule-of-cool outcome happens — disarm, rally, escape, a rope that was exactly there.
- **Told Ahead of You.** In any port, ship, tavern or crew you have not visited, you are known. Roll charisma to determine which version of the story arrived first: hero, monster, or something profitable.
- **Never the Last Time.** Your rerolls increase to five per day and seven at sea, and any reroll that would fail a second time may be spent instead to make the attempt take longer rather than fail — you are still hanging from the rigging next turn.
- **Crew of the Tale.** Allies who fight beside you gain one reroll per day each, and any ally who witnesses your moment of legend is immune to fear for the remainder of the encounter.
- **Legend (1/day).** Do something that will be told about. Declare an outcome of any scale that a story would allow — boarding a ship alone, surviving a fall, sailing into weather that sinks fleets, walking out of a hanging. It happens. Afterwards it is known everywhere, and everyone who might want you dead now knows exactly where the story says you were.
